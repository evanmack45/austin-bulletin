# First Read priority ladder — selection jam, 2026-08-29

Evan asked to tweak the daily-life-impact priority order toward an Austin
Chronicle sensibility (street-level city life) without giving up the
Bulletin's neutral news identity. Five persona agents with deliberately
different optimization functions each proposed a ladder independently
(no visibility into one another), then the proposals were synthesized into
three variants. Evan picked **Variant B, "Who Pays"**, with two grafts.

## Panel

- **Rosa, third-generation East Austinite** — optimizes for who is actually
  affected: displacement, rent, which neighborhoods bear growth's costs.
- **Danny, Red River musician / service-industry lifer** — optimizes for the
  city's cultural life as first-class news; a closing venue is a factory
  closing.
- **Priya, Mueller transplant with two kids** — optimizes for pure utility:
  will knowing this change what I do or budget this week.
- **Walt, retired city-desk editor** — optimizes for accountability:
  decisions by institutions that bind or cost residents, documented while
  contestable.
- **Mei, data-minded grad student** — optimizes for behavior-change-per-word;
  brutal about "important-sounding" vs. actually consequential.

## Unanimous findings (adopted regardless of variant)

1. "Major Texas news" loses its automatic second-place tier and becomes a
   filter: a state story ranks only when it converts into something specific
   for an Austin household.
2. Government process is never news by itself; a vote ranks by its landing
   effect on bills, streets, schools — not because a meeting happened.
3. Given the real 2026-08-29 story list, all five independently chose the
   cost-of-living cluster as the day's lead — the same call the edition
   made. The ladder change is about ordering the other days.

Also broadly shared: weather leads only when severe (it keeps its section
every day); several panelists demoted ribbon-cuttings vs. changes that
reroute someone's week.

## The three synthesized variants

- **A, "Street First"** (Danny + Priya): safety → cost of living → city
  street life as a first-class tier → getting around → schools →
  government-by-effect. The Chronicle sensibility taken at its word.
- **B, "Who Pays"** (Rosa + Mei + Walt's dollar test) — **CHOSEN**:
  household money, dated and quantified → safety failures with
  accountability attached → who absorbs the cost of growth, by
  neighborhood → systems when they actually change → street life and third
  places → the Texas filter. Street-level in the deeper sense: every story
  ranked by whose life it lands on.
- **C, "Power and the Street"** (Walt + Danny's texture): contestable
  decisions converted to household terms → money moving to/from residents →
  harm with institutional fingerprints → street life when power caused it →
  systems → the Texas filter.

## Grafts adopted into B

- **Danny's reframe**: venue/kitchen/festival stories are business stories
  about the city's economy and identity, never entertainment filler
  (EDITORIAL "Voice").
- **Walt's specificity principle**: import the Chronicle's specificity —
  named blocks, named bars, named people — not its instinct to let taste
  outrank consequence (EDITORIAL "Voice").

## Rejected

- Danny's test-day suggestion to fold a Reddit-only venue-closing report
  into the lead "flagged as unconfirmed" — accuracy rule 5 stands: social
  posts are never a sole source. A tip to chase, not a story.

## Where it landed

- PIPELINE.md Step 2 carries the ladder verbatim, plus the no-genuine-thread
  fallback (lead with the single most consequential story; say so in the
  log).
- EDITORIAL.md "Voice" carries both grafts.
- CLAUDE.md standing decisions records the ruling.

## Full persona proposals

### Rosa

1. Cost of staying — housing prices, evictions, property taxes, and any
   city/utility fee that hits a household budget directly.
2. Who absorbs the cost of growth — development, zoning, surveillance and
   infrastructure decisions, weighted by which neighborhoods bear them.
3. Life-and-death safety failures — especially involving workers or
   residents with the least power to protect themselves.
4. Schools and family stability — a family leaves the district before it
   leaves the city; ISD trouble is a housing story wearing a different hat.
5. Government action with real financial teeth — not symbolic votes.
6. Transportation and access, ranked by whose life it changes — not by
   ribbon-cuttings for corridors that already serve the well-off.
7. Community and cultural continuity — a real loss of place, worth grieving
   publicly, but it doesn't outrank anyone's rent or safety.
8. Statewide news with a specific, near-term Austin consequence only.

Demotions: Texas as standing #2; council-vote-as-inherently-newsworthy;
infrastructure completions as automatic news ("a project finishing is not
the same as a project mattering; who it serves decides that"); the
assumption that institutional scale equals consequence.

### Danny

1. Culture and commerce as civic infrastructure — venue/kitchen/festival
   news treated as business stories, not entertainment filler.
2. Safety and shelter.
3. Cost of living and housing.
4. Government and public money in motion — measured by street-level effect.
5. Getting around. 6. Weather and environment. 7. Schools.
8. Statewide only when it lands on Austin.

Demotions: Texas as a standing tier; government process; culture beneath
government and transportation ("backwards for a city whose identity and
tourism economy run on its scene").

### Priya

1. Household budget shock — what a family pays this week or month.
2. Kid & school logistics. 3. Getting around — direct, immediate action
items. 4. Safety & housing. 5. Weekend plan — nice to know, not need to
know. 6. City Hall that actually lands. 7. Texas backdrop, only when it
reaches a household.

Demotions: Texas as automatic runner-up; process stories ("I care once a
vote becomes a rule I have to deal with"); industry-ownership news as
top-tier unless it changes prices or availability.

### Walt

1. Power in the act of deciding — while it can still be contested.
2. Money taken from or promised to residents, converted to what a
   household actually pays. 3. Bodily harm with an institution's
   fingerprints on it. 4. The systems everyone has to use, when they
   actually change. 5. Who controls a civic asset. 6. Street life colliding
   with a decision — a permit, lease, zoning call, or funding cut, not
   shifting taste. 7. Weather when severe. 8. Ambient civic texture — real,
   but it never bumps tiers 1–7.

Key dissent: "Import the Chronicle's specificity and street-level voice
(named blocks, named bars, named people), not its instinct to let taste
and culture outrank power. The Chronicle's own best work was city hall,
not the listings section."

### Mei

1. Your wallet, dated and quantified. 2. Open physical risk — unsettled
stories where new facts change behavior. 3. Your week just changed —
road/transit/legal-deadline changes inside seven days. 4. Street life and
third places — "the real Chronicle instinct." 5. Decisions with a real
trigger — "council begins considering" isn't actionable. 6. Structural
trajectory — long-fuse stories. 7. Statewide with a local hook.

Demotions: Texas free pass; blanket city-government tier; weather's
permanent top slot; schools as a generic reserved seat.

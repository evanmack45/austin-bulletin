# Naming jam — First Read and The River (2026-08-29, late)

Evan asked for the naming pass the re-review panel recommended. Five
independent personas (sonnet agents, 10-call budgets, no visibility into
each other) proposed names for two slots: the opening synthesis
(provisionally "First Read") and the beat-grouped stream ("The River",
opaque to 5 of 7 cold readers in both review panels). Constraints given:
broadsheet register, plain to a first-time reader, no old-Austin cliché
(Violet Crown/Waterloo vetoed), no self-praise, no puns, no
tech-newsletter branding, and the set must read as one voice alongside
Weather, The Number, Countdown, One Good Thing.

## The panel

- **Eleanor, broadsheet historian** — register and precedent.
- **Grace, plain-language advocate** — cold-reader guess test.
- **Marcus, target reader** — "would I stop there in my four minutes?"
- **Sofia, Austin native with taste** — local texture without cliché.
- **Devon, naming-systems professional** — the set as one voice,
  referability, five-year durability.

## Verdict on "First Read": REPLACE, 4–1

- Marcus: overpromises — sounds like the whole digest, not the opener.
- Grace: cold readers guess "the first story" or "a quick skim" — the
  wrong mental model for a synthesis of the whole day.
- Eleanor: it was NBC News's branded political newsletter, 2003–2024
  (~5,000 editions; verified) — political-newsletter register, exactly
  the register Evan vetoed on the masthead.
- Devon: "every news product on earth is someone's first read" — it
  names nothing and will never be the word a reader reaches for.
- Sofia (dissent): keep — plainspoken, no metaphor to defend, and a
  second rename fight has its own cost.

## Verdict on "The River": dead on arrival, 5–0

No panelist defended it. Cold readers read it as literal water news in a
city on the Colorado River.

## Candidates that died, and why

- **The Takeaway** (Marcus's A pick) — WNYC/PRX's national radio show,
  2008–2023 (verified). Dead brand, live association.
- **Why It Matters** — Axios's signature Smart Brevity label; borrowed
  register.
- **The Ledger** (three panelists) — quiet rhyme with the Who Pays
  ladder, but three of three flaggers agreed it misreads as
  finance-only on any day the thread isn't money.
- **The Beat / Six Beats** — recursive next to the six beat names it
  sits above; Grace calls "beat" newsroom jargon a cold reader lacks.
- **The Docket** — legal-only misread, kills five of six beats.
- **The Wire** — wire-service misread plus the HBO collision.
- **The Throughline** — lit-crit jargon; same failure mode as The River.
- **News of the Day / Today's News** — period-correct and maximally
  plain, but a label rather than a name: nothing to own, no city in it.
- **The Rounds** (Sofia's B pick) — honest daily-coverage verb, but
  misreads as a health column and fails the cold-guess test.

## The pairs proposed

| Panelist | Pair |
|---|---|
| Eleanor | The Ledger + News of the Day |
| Grace | The Big Picture + Today's News |
| Marcus | The Takeaway + The Roundup |
| Sofia | First Read + The Rounds |
| Devon | The Briefing + City Desk |

## Synthesis presented to Evan (ranked)

1. **The Briefing + City Desk** — Devon's pair, with Eleanor carrying
   The Briefing independently. Both are century-old newspaper furniture,
   both pass the cold-guess test, both fit the "The + plain noun"
   system, and City Desk is the one candidate that actively advances the
   localize-harder direction. Full sequence: morning note → The Briefing
   → City Desk → Weather → The Number → Countdown → One Good Thing.
2. **The Briefing + The Roundup** — if City Desk reads too
   newsroom-insider. Roundup passed every cold-guess test and carries a
   faint ranching echo that is Texan without being a costume; its cost
   is heavy blog/newsletter usage.
3. **The Big Picture + Today's News** — Grace's maximal-plainness pair.
   Zero decoding required, and zero identity: Devon calls both
   unownable, Sofia says the city disappears.

Decision (Evan, 2026-08-29 ~10:56 p.m.): **The Briefing + City Desk.**
Applied the same night to Edition No. 7 and to EDITORIAL, PIPELINE and the
gate; earlier editions keep their printed names, and the code keeps
"river" as the stream's internal term.

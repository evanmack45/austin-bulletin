# Item-legibility jam — City Desk leads vs briefs (2026-08-30, after midnight)

Evan couldn't tell whether a lead's bold headline governed the briefs
below it ("it kind of looks like it does"). Two fixes failed before the
jam: uniform spacing, then short hairlines between items ("this is still
confusing. i don't like it"). Five independent personas (sonnet agents,
10-call budgets, no visibility into each other) diagnosed before
prescribing.

## The panel

- **Werner, broadsheet designer** — the inherited news-column craft.
- **Dot, wire-desk editor** — how "In Brief" columns solved this daily.
- **Grace, plain-language advocate** — the cold-reader comprehension test.
- **Marcus, target reader** — four-minute phone skim, in character.
- **Ines, screen typographer** — what survives print→scroll translation.

## Diagnosis: unanimous, and it indicts both failed fixes

This was never a boundary problem; separators can't cure it.

1. **The heading convention misfires.** The page's strongest learned rule
   — bold flush-left line governs what follows (the beat headings work
   exactly this way) — captures the lead headline, which is positionally
   identical to a heading (Grace, Ines).
2. **Briefs have endings but no beginnings.** The source tag closes an
   item, but nothing marks a brief's first word as a start; Marcus admits
   the tags read as "fine print I'm ignoring" (Dot, Marcus, Grace).
3. **The tiers are typographic twins.** Same size, measure, color, margin
   — no system for the eye to learn (Werner, Ines).
4. **The bold place label is a rogue pattern.** It taught "bold at start =
   new item," then most briefs broke the rule (Dot, Werner).

Why the hairlines failed: a rule says "something changed" but not *what*
— and it mimicked the beat heading's own hairline, muddying the one
grouping cue that worked (Ines, Werner).

## The three cures rendered (all render-time; no content changes)

- **A — Marked openings** (Dot + Marcus): every brief opens with its
  first words in bold. Verdict on the render: consistent rhythm, but the
  auto-bolded fragments emphasize arbitrary words ("**Austin police
  are**"), and headline-weight bold on briefs preserves the echo Werner
  warned about.
- **B — The brief block** (Werner + Grace, Marcus's marker folded in):
  after a beat's leads, a quiet IN BRIEF slug (muted small caps), then
  briefs as a visibly different genus — 0.92rem, oxblood em-dash hanging
  in the gutter, tucked-in margin, place labels de-bolded to small caps
  (dateline register).
- **C — Run-in headlines** (Ines): the lead's headline melts into its
  paragraph as an oxblood bold clause; briefs indent beneath. Kills the
  heading misfire at the root, but leads lose their skimmable display
  line and the oxblood bold half-reads as a link.

## Recommendation presented to Evan

**B.** It uses the page's one strong convention *correctly* — a label is
allowed to govern what follows, and IN BRIEF says so in words — while
giving briefs both a beginning (the gutter dash) and a tier identity
(size, indent, dateline labels). Zero editorial burden, zero content
changes, phone-safe, and the beat hairline stays the only rule in the
stream. A's catchword instinct survives inside B: the place labels
become consistent dateline-style openers where they exist.

Decision (Evan, 2026-08-30 ~12:10 a.m.): **B**, with the slug worded
"Also" (his pick from Also / Briefly / Short takes / Also today). Shipped
the same night in Run 9, alongside his larger ruling that made the
place-label question moot: suburb news is cut entirely — Austin proper
only (boundary in EDITORIAL.md).

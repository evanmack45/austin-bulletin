## The findings that matter

### 1. The page implies a major death without stating it (verified)

The morning note celebrates a Dolly Parton tribute weekend in a warm,
festive register; the tributes item says Austin "spends the weekend on
Dolly Parton"; and the only mention that she has died is a video caption —
"remembered amid her death." Ruth reread the page twice, genuinely unsure;
Alan called it "a real professional miss, not a style choice" and made it
a flip-me-back-to-no condition. Root cause: the morning cloud run dropped
her death as national wire while running the local tributes. Rule
candidate for EDITORIAL: when the day's items only make sense against a
fact, that fact must be stated plainly on the page, even when the fact
itself is out of scope.

### 2. The Voice cards cost the reader trust at exactly the wrong moments

4 of 7 flagged them. Bill — who survived everything else for the first
time — quit AT the r/Austin card ("I don't know what r/Austin is… why is a
stranger's comment next to real news about people dying?"). Ruth found the
condolence card beside the fatal-collapse item "jarring, almost
disrespectful"; Alan flagged the same placement as "a tone mismatch a copy
desk would flag"; Tyler read them as ads. Two candidate rules: a plain
framing label on every card ("A reader on Reddit"), and never adjacent to
a death or serious-injury item.

### 3. Geography is unlabeled and it costs the two local-utility readers

Dana: "Georgetown, Leander, Manor, Marble Falls, even San Antonio — mixed
in with actual Austin stuff… I had to read every line to check if it was
near my block." Priya the same. Items name places mid-sentence, but
nothing scannable says how far away a story is. Fits Evan's localization
push: a bold locality prefix per item (like the old In-brief labels) would
answer it.

### 4. The impatient half still needs First Read to BE the digest

Priya's ask — "a top 5 things Austin is talking about at the very top" —
is literally First Read's job, and she quit anyway: First Read today reads
as one story deep, not a map of five. Worth watching across more editions
before acting; today's edition genuinely was a one-cluster day.

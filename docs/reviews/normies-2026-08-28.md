- **CRITICAL (6–7 of 7):** River wall of text; unexplained acronyms; no visual relief.
- **IMPORTANT (4–5 of 7):** opaque section naming; masthead reads as costume;
  AI disclosure buried; no search.

  defects rather than matters of taste, and are cheap to fix.

## Verified before reporting

Three persona claims were checked against the live site rather than taken at
face value.

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Blank/broken video embed (Dana, Ruth, Bill, Alan) | **False — my capture artifact.** The Fox 7 iframe is `loading="lazy"` and never fired in a full-page screenshot. Scrolled into view on a real viewport it renders correctly, thumbnail and play button. Not a site defect. |
| Power-grid line reads "expects enough to around" (Bill) | **False.** Live text reads "…expects enough to go around." He misread it. |
| Timeline attribution printed twice (Marcus) | **True.** The credit line is baked into the timeline graphic *and* repeated verbatim as the HTML `<figcaption>` immediately below it. Two identical lines, stacked. |

Also confirmed directly against the page: no `<input>` element anywhere (no
search), no back-to-top link, and beat headings inside the River carry no `id`
attributes — only `the-big-story`, `the-river`, and `weather` do, so there is
nothing to jump to.

## What everyone praised

- **"Today at a glance" — 7 of 7.** The only element with universal approval.
  Tyler would screenshot it; Priya would bookmark for it alone; Alan said
  "Axios doesn't do this and I'd check it daily." The plain-English gloss under
  each number is doing the work — Bill singled out "Hat, water, early start" as
  "exactly how my wife used to talk to me before I walked out the door."
- **The About page's honesty — 5 of 7.** Stating outright that an AI produces
  the site, with a named publisher responsible and a visible-corrections policy,
  converted skeptics. Marcus: "that transparency does more to earn trust than
  any amount of typography." The recurring complaint is that it's a click away.
- **Sourcing discipline.** Every River item naming its outlet, and the Big Story
  closing with linked originals, read as honest to the three most skeptical
  personas.
- **The Big Story timeline graphic** (Marcus, Alan) and **the morning note**
  (Ruth: "genuinely lovely writing"; Bill).
- **"What's next"** (Priya: "answers the question I always want answered and
  most local news never does").
- **"One Good Thing"** (Marcus, Ruth).
- **Prose quality.** Ruth, who graded essays for four decades, found no grammar
  errors and credited the Big Story's structure as "sound newspaper structure."
  Alan: "clean and doesn't editorialize — no 'shocking,' no 'slams.'"

## The three findings that matter most

### 1. The River is the site's biggest asset and its biggest liability

Forty sourced items is genuinely more than anyone else in Austin puts on one
page — and it is the thing that ended five of the six sessions that reached it.
The problem is not the volume; it's that the volume is undifferentiated. Every
item is the same typeface, size, weight, and paragraph shape. A fatal MoPac
crash and a Buda ordinance legalizing pot-bellied pigs get identical visual
weight (Priya). There is no scannable layer, no way to jump to a beat, no
progress signal, and no back-to-top.

Ruth, the professional reader, diagnosed the prose side of the same problem:
nearly every item follows "[Who] did [what], according to [source]," which after
twenty repetitions "starts to read less like a newspaper and more like a very
competent student dutifully following a formula."

### 2. Local shorthand is assumed, and it costs the widest range of readers

Every one of the seven flagged at least one unexplained term. ERCOT, MW, ISD,
PUA, ESD 5, DKR, MoPac, Stage 3, Flock, Axon, 8VC. The air-quality tile shows
"59" with no scale — Ruth and Bill both asked "59 out of what?" Priya, an Austin
resident of 14 months, could not decode MoPac or DKR. This is the cheapest fix
on the list and it touches expert and novice alike.

### 3. The masthead splits the audience by age, in the wrong direction

The two oldest personas loved the blackletter nameplate — Bill: "this looks like
a real newspaper," Ruth: "dignified, like the Sunday paper my father used to
bring in." The five younger ones read it as costume: "cosplay" (Priya, Tyler),
"wedding invitation or a whiskey label" (Dana), "content-farm cosplay-of-
authority" (Marcus), "is this satire?" (Alan). It recovers for the patient ones
once the sourcing discipline becomes visible, but it spends the first two
seconds of credibility rather than earning them.

Worth noting this is a deliberate, locked-in identity decision. The finding is
not "change it" — it's that the design is currently doing the opposite of what a
six-day-old outlet needs, and the recovery currently happens too far down the
page.

## Recommendations, ranked

Nothing here is implemented. These are proposals.

| # | Fix | Who it helps | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Give River items a scannable layer — a bold lede clause or a short bold headline per item, so the beat can be skimmed without reading every paragraph | 6/7 | Medium |
| 2 | Expand every acronym on first use, and put a scale on the air-quality number ("59 of 500 — good") | 7/7 | Small |
| 3 | Cap or relocate state politics; it stacks mid-scroll where two personas quit | 2/7, both habitual readers | Small |
| 4 | Add beat jump-links under the "40 items · grouped by beat" line, plus a back-to-top | 2/7, verified missing | Small |
| 5 | Put a one-line AI disclosure on the homepage (the About text already exists and converts skeptics — it's just buried) | 4/7 | Small |
| 6 | Rename or subtitle "The River" so it explains itself; Priya read it as flood follow-up because it sat under a storm story | 5/7 | Small |
| 7 | Move Contact above Follow/RSS on About; Bill quit on "feed reader" before reaching the email | 2/7 | Trivial |
| 8 | De-duplicate the timeline attribution — drop it from the graphic or the figcaption, not both | Verified defect | Trivial |
| 9 | Break the River sources link dump into a structured list | 1/7 | Small |
| 10 | Add search over the archive | 4/7 | Larger |
| 11 | Reconsider the Reddit Voice card's selection criteria — the two most editorially-minded readers both found it tonally off against a "Neutral · Factual" masthead | 2/7 | Judgment call |

### Deliberately excluded

- **Email edition.** Priya asked for one and named it the single thing that would
  make her subscribe. This is a settled decision and is not being re-proposed.
- **Weekend/events coverage.** Dana and Tyler both left for want of it. This is a
  scope question for the publisher, not a defect.
- **Original reporting.** Alan's decisive objection — "a very polished table of
  contents to other people's newsrooms." Correct, and outside what the current
  pipeline is built to do.

## The verdict from the target reader

Alan is closest to the reader this site needs. His answer was no:

> It's a curation product wearing a broadsheet costume, six days old, with no
> original reporting… Right now it's a very polished table of contents to other
> people's newsrooms.

His three conditions for reconsidering: fix the obviously broken things, show
one piece of original work, and put a human name on the judgment calls rather
than "the AI decided." The second and third are strategy questions. The first is
this document.

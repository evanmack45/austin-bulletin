## The five voices

### 1. Renter in Riverside, 28, on the bus

**Bulletin.** I wanted anything that hits my rent, my bills or my ride. The front page gave me a golf course and a big "Austin, up close." graphic that looks like a placeholder. The side column says "Around Austin" and then nothing. I scrolled the full edition: home prices, crashes and robotaxis, but nothing on rent, CapMetro service or bills. The weather box at the top is actually useful. I'd come back for that strip and a "your wallet" line. I trust the source links, but the empty box makes me wonder whether the site is finished.

**Y'all, City Hall.** I typed "rent" into the search. It gave me 49 results about water treatment plants and easements. I left. I couldn't tell whether anything Council did this month touches renters. If the search understood words instead of letter strings, I'd try again. Nothing here looks false, it's just written for someone who already knows what they're looking for.

### 2. Parent in Allandale, 41, Austin ISD

**Bulletin.** I came for the Burnet and Webb takeover fight. Today's edition has it under Schools, and it's clear and calm. Later I learned there's a whole AISD guide, but nothing on the front page, the edition or the menu links to it. I only found it by typing the URL. That guide is also three days old on deadline day. Three days in early October are missing from the archive, which makes me doubt "every morning." The Reddit and X posts in the middle of the news feel like opinion slipping in. Put a schools link at the top and I'd check daily.

**Y'all, City Hall.** "KEEP COUNCIL WEIRD," "Swam upstream," "Who's tight with who?" To me this reads as a site with a point of view, even if the numbers are real. A search for "Burnet" found nothing. I didn't see school news here, which is fair, since it's about Council. The plain-language explanations of each agenda item are good. I'd trust it more if it sounded like a clerk instead of a columnist.

### 3. Retired engineer in Northwest Hills, 67, desktop

**Bulletin.** I came for the budget. The budget guide is excellent: tax rate to four decimals, the cost per household, the vote counts, a source on every line. It took me several minutes to find, though, because it's linked only from a council-races page. The archive is a column of dates with no headlines. Today's Muny piece gives three different acreages and doesn't reconcile them. Editions for October 7 to 9 are simply missing, even though the About page promises seven days a week, and it points me to "PIPELINE.md," which isn't a page I can read. I'd return for the guides if I could find them.

**Y'all, City Hall.** It's dense and well sourced, which I like. The "Budget" topic page, though, is a 24,000-word scroll that's mostly lawsuit settlements. The member page says "0% dissent," then lists ten votes in the minority. Rounding, I assume, but say so. The same consent-agenda quote is pasted under 22 items. The footer gives five "current through" dates, and official votes stop at August 27. It discloses that the summaries are AI-written. I'd still check them against Legistar.

### 4. Business owner on East Sixth, 35

**Bulletin.** I wanted closures, construction and anything Council did near my block. Today's edition mentions an old music venue on East Seventh for sale. That's close, but there are no street closures or permits. A good Oct 22 Council preview exists, and I only found it because the other site linked to it. Nothing on the Bulletin front page points to it. The preview also says the airport commission meets "October 14," which has passed. A "this week at Council" link on the home page would bring me back on Mondays.

**Y'all, City Hall.** I searched "East 6th" and got 97 results, the first being East 15th Street. "Sixth Street" found two, which is closer. The Oct 22 meeting page has every item in plain English, which is great, but it never says how to sign up to speak. The search box, the next meeting and the latest results are all on the first screen, which is the right idea. If search worked on my address, I'd bookmark it.

### 5. First-time voter at UT, 19

**Bulletin.** I wanted to know how voting works and who's running. The Vote 2026 guide answered that fast: dates, links, and a council-races page with my district. But tonight is the last candidate panel, District 1, and today's paper doesn't mention it. I only saw it in the guide. The front page has just one story and an empty box, so it looks unfinished. Without my pointing it out, nothing tells me this page changes every day. A "vote" banner with the next date on it would bring me back.

**Y'all, City Hall.** I found my member in about 20 seconds. I typed my dorm address on "Your District" and got District 9, Zo Qadri. That's the best moment on either site. Then the scorecard gave me 6,917 "vote records," a "dissent rate," and no way to email or call him. The next meeting page doesn't say how to show up or speak. "Your District" isn't in the top menu on the home page. I'd come back if it told me "here's how to go on the 22nd."

## Fix these first

1. **Bulletin home page placeholder and empty rail (finding 1).** It's the first thing every reader sees, and it makes a working daily paper look abandoned.
2. **Link the orphaned guides from the home page, edition and nav (finding 2).** The site's best work (AISD, budget, Council preview) is unreachable, so the readers it was written for never see it.
3. **Make council-record search match whole words or phrases (finding 3).** Search is the first thing Y'all puts in front of readers, and today it turns "rent" and "East 6th" into noise, so people leave.

## How we looked

We used a real browser, with screenshots: headless Chromium driven by Playwright at 390×844 and 1280×800. We captured the rendered text and ran live searches on both sites and an address lookup on Y'all, City Hall. Large-font or zoomed reading was not specifically tested.

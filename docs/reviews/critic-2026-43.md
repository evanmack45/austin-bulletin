## The five voices

### 1. Renter in Riverside, 28, on the bus

**Bulletin.** I came for anything hitting my rent, bills or commute. In 30
seconds I got the eviction-help headline, but then a big "Austin, up close"
poster and "Two items share one question." I had to open the full edition to
learn it's $2.25 million and up to 1,600 renters. The race-weekend closures I
actually needed were inside a tweet halfway down. I'd come back for the
"Today at a glance" box, which is great on a phone. I trust it because every
item names its outlet, but "0 likes" on the embedded posts felt like a bot
built the page.

**Y'all.** I searched "rent" and got airport rental cars and barrier rentals.
"Eviction" didn't find today's eviction contract at all. Every result says
"see full record" instead of telling me what happened. The speaker sign-up
times on the home page are clear, but they closed this morning before I got
here. I'd come back after today's vote if the result showed up in one line.
I don't distrust it. It just reads like a database. "Version cafbbf9" means
nothing to me.

### 2. Parent in Allandale, 41, Austin ISD

**Bulletin.** I came for school news. The AISD health-savings cut was right
there in the side rail with numbers and the union's view, so I got it fast.
The "Austin ISD and the state" guide is now easy to find from the home page,
but it says last checked Oct 16 and doesn't mention the Webb petition story
from Tuesday. I'd come back because it reads like facts, not opinion. The
About page says plainly an AI writes it and a person supervises. I'd want
that said on the edition itself, not two clicks away.

**Y'all.** Not much school news here, which makes sense, since it's City
Council. "Find your district" is buried, and the home page is mostly a
search box. "Pick a fight to follow" and "the eternal I-35 question" read
like a columnist, not a reference. It's toned down from what my neighbor
described, but I'd still hesitate to forward it to the PTA list. The vote
numbers look careful and each one links to its record, which I'd trust.

### 3. Retired engineer in Northwest Hills, 67, desktop, large font

**Bulletin.** I came for the budget and sourcing. The budget guide is
excellent: rate, no-new-revenue rate, dollars per household, each line
cited. Every edition item ends with its outlet. But the home page prints
"2026-10-22T11:09:07.665Z" like a log file. The About page sends me to
GitHub for the rulebook, and the Council preview still says a commission
"was scheduled to review" an item on Oct 14 without saying whether it did.
I'd come back for the guides. I would want each guide to say when it was
last re-verified against today's news.

**Y'all.** I wanted to know whether the numbers hold up. The member page
says "<1% dissent," the district card says "0%," and then ten votes are
listed "in the minority." The explanation is on the page, but it took three
reads. Official votes end August 27, nearly two months ago. The footer's five
disagreeing "current through" dates and "PIPELINE CHECKED" undercut an
otherwise rigorous site. The matrix should show how many votes each cell is
based on.

### 4. Small business owner on East Sixth, 35

**Bulletin.** I came for closures and what Council's doing to my block on
race weekend. The F1 shuttle closures at E. 10th and Red River are in an
embedded tweet, and the home page never mentions the race. That's the thing
that costs me money this weekend. I found the Halal Time trailer fire on
East Sixth and Onion in an "Also" line. I'd come back for a closures box.
The preview skipping item 22, the cover story, made me wonder who picked the
three items.

**Y'all.** I searched "East Sixth" and got two Public Improvement District
assessment items. Useful, but I had to open each to learn the outcome.
"East 6th" brought up 38½ Street. My address put me in District 3 right
away, which was nice. Today's meeting page is one huge list in agenda order,
so the Parmer and parkland items are buried under lawsuit settlements. I'd
come back if it led with "what passed today" by street or district.

### 5. First-time voter at UT, 19

**Bulletin.** I wanted to know how to vote and what Council does. The "Vote
2026" guide has dates and links, and today's edition says early voting runs
through Oct 30. The home page leads with a social-services money story and a
poster that says "Austin, up close," which didn't hook me. The edition looks
like a different website from the home page: a blackletter masthead and a
different menu with no search. I'd come back during early voting if the home
page said "where to vote today" up top.

**Y'all.** I came to find my council member. The home page is a search box
for "project words," and "Find your district" is way down. Once I found it,
typing 2100 San Antonio St told me District 9, Zo Qadri. That part was
great. The member page's notable votes are cut off mid-sentence, so I
couldn't tell what anyone voted on. Nothing tells me how to watch today's
meeting live. I'd come back if I could follow my member's votes from this
fall.

## Fix first

1. **Put Grand Prix closures and other street changes in a visible box on
   the Bulletin home page and edition (#2).** It's the one thing this week
   that changes how two readers get around or run a business, and right now
   it's inside a tweet.
2. **Put "Find your district" and the scorecards in Y'all's home header, and
   make the dissent figures consistent (#3, #5).** The first-time voter's
   whole reason to visit was two clicks and a long scroll away, and the
   conflicting 0% / <1% / "in the minority" numbers cost the engineer's trust.
3. **Replace the placeholder cover graphic and the "Two items share one
   question" dek with something that says what happened (#1).** It's the
   largest thing on the home page, and four of five readers took it as
   empty.

## How we looked

Real browser: Playwright 1.58.0 driving the preinstalled Chromium. Every page
was captured at 390×844 (phone user agent) and 1280×800, with viewport and
full-page screenshots plus rendered text. We typed queries into both sites'
search boxes and ran the Y'all district lookup with three addresses. No
console errors. The only horizontal overflow was a 1px overflow on the
edition at phone width.

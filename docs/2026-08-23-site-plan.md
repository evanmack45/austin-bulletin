# The Austin Bulletin — Plan to make it worth reading

Date: 2026-08-23 (evening). Author: Claude, for Evan.
Status: DRAFT for Evan's review. Nothing below is built yet except where marked.

## 1. Honest audit of what exists tonight

The site works, but it is a digest of other outlets' headlines with a nice
typeface. A reader gets nothing here they could not get from KXAN's front
page in the same three minutes. Specifically:

| Problem | Evidence |
|---|---|
| No reason to come *here* | Every story is a summary of one outlet's article with a link back. No original value, no data, no perspective a reader can't get elsewhere. |
| No daily utility | Weather is the last section. No events, no council calendar, no traffic, no lake levels — the things people check every morning. Every successful daily we studied leads with utility (6AM City opens on weather/sunrise; Axios has a Things-to-Do block). |
| Weak selection | Story choice is "whatever the RSS feeds had." A Hutto fire-station siting and a candle maker made the top seven on day one. |
| Images were an afterthought | Generic stock (a water tower, a Round Rock supermarket) chosen to fill a slot. Fixed tonight: verified photos or photorealistic generated images. |
| No human, no relationship | Anonymous publisher; until tonight no way to reply. KUT's relaunch and Mirror Indy's growth both hinge on a named person readers can write to. |
| No habit loop | RSS only (added tonight). Email is the retention channel in every example studied; we excluded it at launch. |
| Process optimizes for "passes the gate," not "is good" | The pipeline checks facts and links. Nothing checks whether the edition is worth reading. |

## 2. What the research says works (2025–2026 sources)

1. **A fixed template readers can predict.** 6AM City and From Boise both
   treat unchanging structure as their core loyalty asset.
2. **Utility first.** Sunrise/weather at the top makes the product a daily
   touchpoint, not a news read. Nieman Lab's 2026 framing: "What problem
   are we solving, and for whom?"
3. **Hard local news beats lifestyle for loyalty** (Sebastopol Times converts
   24.5% to paid on civic news). Our neutral-facts stance is an asset, not a
   limitation, if the selection is genuinely about daily life.
4. **A named author with a reply channel.** First-person, "reply and it goes
   to me."
5. **A personal closing note.** Mirror Indy credits its editor's sign-off for
   608% first-year growth.
6. **Email.** Every example's primary channel. Referral programs only matter
   after thousands of subscribers — not now.
7. **Don't over-format.** 6AM City stripped bolding/links after readers said
   it was hard to scan. Keep the page calm.

## 3. The promise

> **Austin in three minutes, every morning: the numbers that matter today,
> the five stories that matter, and nothing else.**

Reader: an Austin adult who wants to know, before leaving the house, what
today holds — heat, air, water, power, council, roads — and what happened
yesterday that affects them. Not a news junkie. Reads on a phone with coffee.

## 4. Proposed daily template (v2)

Order is fixed. Every edition has every section.

1. **Masthead + date plate** (exists).
2. **Today at a glance** — a compact strip of verified numbers, one line each,
   pulled from public data APIs with no editorial judgment:
   - Weather: high / low / alert (NWS — exists, moves to the top)
   - Air quality: ozone / PM2.5 category (TCEQ Austin monitors)
   - Lake Travis: percent full and change vs. last week (Water Data for Texas)
   - Barton Springs: water temperature (USGS gauge 08155500)
   - Power: ERCOT peak-demand forecast and any conservation notice
     (ERCOT dashboard) — summer and winter only
   - Sunrise / sunset
3. **The Morning Note** (exists, boxed).
4. **Top stories — exactly five**, lead story with a full-width photorealistic
   image, each story with a one-line **"What's next"** (a date, a vote, a
   deadline — factual, not opinion; this is how we add value without
   breaking neutrality).
5. **Today in Austin** — 3–5 dated items: council/commission meetings, major
   road closures, notable events. Sources to verify: City of Austin council
   calendar, Austin Transportation closures, TxDOT, KUT/Chronicle event
   listings (attribution rules to be settled).
6. **In brief** (exists, category labels).
7. **One good thing** — a two-sentence closer in the Morning Note's voice.
   Zero politics. (Mirror Indy pattern.)
8. **Footer**: Subscribe by email · RSS · Contact · About.

## 6. Process changes (so it stops feeling rushed)
- **A "would I read this?" pass** at the end of every run: the pipeline
  reads the built page top to bottom as a reader and rewrites any headline
  that is vague, any summary that buries the point, any image that does not
  earn its place. Logged.
- **Headline rule**: specific, active, a number when there is one. Never a
  question. Never a pun (already ruled).
- **Image rule** (done tonight): verified photo or generated image, never a
  stand-in.
- **Design changes go through a mockup first.** Tonight's plate and note box
  went straight to CSS; the wood-engraving style went straight to
  production and was rejected. New visual work gets a design canvas or a
  screenshot round before it ships.

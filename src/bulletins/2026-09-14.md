### The smallest line on the bill votes first

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-14/tax-bill-shares.png" alt="Bar chart of the share of a Travis County homeowner&apos;s property tax bill going to each taxing entity on 2026 figures: 44.1 percent to Austin Independent School District, 25.6 percent to the city of Austin, 18.3 percent to Travis County, 6.2 percent to Austin Community College and about 6 percent to Central Health, the county hospital district">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Austin Current, from 2026 taxing-entity figures</figcaption>
</figure>

Travis County commissioners vote Tuesday on the tax rate for Central Health, the hospital district that covers
low-income residents. The district has approved a $650 million budget and a rate 6% above the no-new-revenue rate.
Austin Current puts the increase at $36 a year for an owner of the median-priced home in the county. Central Health
told KXAN last week that most county homesteads would pay about $22 more. The two are not the same house.

Either way it is the smallest slice. About 6% of a Travis County tax bill goes to the hospital district.

The biggest slice is the one nobody votes on Tuesday. The Austin Independent School District takes 44.1%, and on
Sunday its parents walked the North Loop blocks around the shuttered Ridgetop Elementary, knocking on doors about a
possible Texas Education Agency (TEA) takeover. Burnet and Webb middle schools have each drawn a fifth straight F.
The agency has to act. It has not said how, and a decision could wait until December.

Tonight there is a third room. Council members and candidates take questions on housing costs at Assembly Hall
downtown. West Austin Properties data put the average Austin-area home sale at about $458,000 in July 2021. This July
it was nearly $593,000.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Travis County commissioners vote on the Central Health tax rate Tuesday.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### Commissioners decide Tuesday whether the hospital district gets its rate
Travis County commissioners vote Tuesday on Central Health's tax rate, 6% above the no-new-revenue rate, alongside the
district's $650 million budget. The budget carries $54 million in new mental health spending: $33.1 million for 22
inpatient psychiatric beds and 10 psychiatric emergency bays, $16 million toward a crisis center, and $1 million for an
additional crisis care team. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/14/austin-travis-county-central-health-dell-medical-school-tax/">Austin Current</a></span>

A Texas appeals court ruled in August that Central Health may keep its $35 million-a-year payment to the University of Texas at Austin's Dell Medical School; attorney Fred Lewis said he will appeal. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/14/austin-travis-county-central-health-dell-medical-school-tax/">Austin Current</a></span>

Texas now requires solar panel retailers and salespeople to register with the state; KXAN's investigators have been tracking a growing number of solar complaints filed with the Texas Attorney General's Office. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/inside-the-investigation/qa-solar-sales-under-scrutiny-as-texas-tightens-rules/">KXAN</a></span>

#### Growth & infrastructure

##### The I-35 rebuild adds carpool lanes. Whether they work is the open question
The Texas Department of Transportation's (TxDOT) three Interstate 35 Capital Express projects carry listed construction
costs of about $5.65 billion and add high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lanes along 28 miles. Kara Kockelman, a transportation
engineering professor at the University of Texas at Austin, told CBS Austin the lanes are almost impossible to enforce.
A 2026 San Antonio assessment found violation rates on Interstate 10 between about 42% and 78%.
<span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/txdot-bets-on-new-hov-lanes-in-multibillion-dollar-i-35-austin-rebuild-the-receipts">CBS Austin</a></span>

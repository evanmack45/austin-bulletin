### Tesla certified its own car. The council asked for a plan.

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-06/driverless-fleet.png" alt="Bar chart of vehicles registered for commercial autonomous operation in Texas: Waymo has 1,000, Tesla&apos;s robotaxi fleet totals 420, and 45 of those are Cybercabs authorized for driverless operation">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Austin American-Statesman and FOX 7 Austin, citing state registration figures</figcaption>
</figure>

Dozens of gold Cybercabs began carrying passengers on Austin streets Thursday. They have no steering wheel, no pedals,
no mirrors. Federal safety rules assume all three, so Tesla certified the vehicle itself, treating some of those
standards as inapplicable. Late that night the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration said it would audit how
the company reached that conclusion, in a query covering about 1,000 vehicles. The service kept running. Tesla shares
fell nearly 6 percent Friday.

Zoox, a competitor, asked for and won an exemption before it started commercial rides. Tesla did not. Three other
federal investigations into Tesla's driving software were already open, one involving a crash that killed a pedestrian.

The other transportation decision Austin made this week was quieter. On Aug. 27 the City Council passed a resolution
on the Lakeline commuter rail station, open since 2010, directing the city manager to study infrastructure and public
uses on the vacant land around it. Council Member Krista Laine, who sponsored it, said 15 years have passed with a
significant amount of that land undeveloped. A cost and a timeline for the work are not yet known.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Austin City Council's next regular meeting is Thursday, Sept. 10; speaker registration opens Monday at 10 a.m.</p>

#### City Hall & county

##### Austin's oldest known Black cemetery becomes a city landmark
City Council designated Bethany Cemetery on Springdale Road a City of Austin Landmark at its Aug. 27 meeting,
following a January recommendation from the Historic Landmark Commission. City documents show the case was first
opened in 1977 and stalled through a series of postponements, then returned in 2024 and timed out over missing
materials. Preservation Austin brought the nomination forward with the cemetery association. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/austins-oldest-known-black-cemetery-finally-gets-landmark-designation/">KXAN</a></span>

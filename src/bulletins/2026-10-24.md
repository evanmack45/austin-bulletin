### Viva La Vida today, a murder charge in a library death, and $1.2 million

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-24/place-agreements.png" alt="Bar chart from a City of Austin release: Place-Based Enhancement Program agreements of up to $182,664 for Esther&apos;s Follies, up to $84,390 for Escuelita del Alma, up to $82,077 for Royal Blue Grocery, and $864,210 for nonprofit office space at Waverly North. The City says the four agreements total more than $1.2 million.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · City of Austin</figcaption>
</figure>

Today. Mexic-Arte Museum's 43rd annual Viva La Vida Festival and Parade for Día de los Muertos runs noon to 6 p.m. Saturday, Oct. 24, and it's free.

The parade starts at noon on Sixth Street at Neches Street and ends at the festival grounds at Fourth Street and Congress Avenue. The City says downtown road closures start at 5 a.m.

Public safety. Austin police say a man assaulted at the Central Library, 710 W. Cesar Chavez St., on March 11 died June 12, and the Travis County Medical Examiner ruled the death a homicide in a report police received Oct. 14. Austin Police Department (APD) identifies the victim as Alonzo Mabry, 62. Police say Daniel Vasquez, 30, who was arrested at the scene and first charged with aggravated assault with serious bodily injury, was charged with murder Oct. 14. APD counts it as Austin's 47th homicide of 2026.

Business. The City says Council approved four agreements Oct. 22 through Austin Economic Development's Place-Based Enhancement Program, totaling more than $1.2 million: up to $182,664 over five years for Esther's Follies, up to $84,390 for Escuelita del Alma, up to $82,077 for Royal Blue Grocery, and $864,210 over 10 years for nonprofit office space at Waverly North in North University.

Around town. Austin Energy crews have finished stringing the 155-foot tree: 39 strings of LED bulbs, 81 bulbs each, topped by a 10-foot double star. The City says the lighting is typically the Sunday after Thanksgiving, and Parks and Recreation will post details in November. Mayor Kirk Watson and Tampere Mayor Ilmari Nurminen signed a memorandum of understanding at City Hall covering areas including semiconductors, startups, advanced manufacturing and defense technology; the cities say they'll set yearly goals and track progress.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The parade starts at noon on Sixth Street at Neches Street. Tips go to 512-974-TIPS or Capital Area Crime Stoppers at 512-472-8477.</p>

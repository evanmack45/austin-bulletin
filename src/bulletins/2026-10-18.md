### Early voting opens Monday, and the county is still catching up

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-18/voter-records.png" alt="Bar chart of three batches of voter records Travis County's voter registrar has reported this fall, from KXAN: 32,000 registration records sent by the Texas Department of Public Safety, which the county finished updating about a week before October 17; 20,000 paper registration applications the registrar said were still being reviewed at that time; and 1,100 name and address updates from the Texas Secretary of State, received by October 17.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin &middot; KXAN</figcaption>
</figure>

Early voting starts Monday, and Travis County's voter registrar spent the weekend on another batch of records. The Travis County Tax Office told KXAN on Saturday that it had received 1,100 more from the Texas Secretary of State.

They are name and address changes that voters made on Texas.gov. A state "issue" held up 15,000 of those updates across Texas, the Secretary of State's office said, and counties now have them in time for early voting. Because the changes are to existing registrations, those voters can still cast a ballot, the office said.

It is not the first backlog this fall. About a week earlier, the Tax Office finished updating 32,000 records sent by the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS). Celia Israel, the county's tax assessor-collector and voter registrar, said then that staff were still going through 20,000 paper applications.

Not everyone is reassured. Valerie Debill of the League of Women Voters Austin Area told KXAN that someone could try to vote Monday and not be in the system. The Tax Office said its staff are working nights and weekends to process as many records as they can before the polls open.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Early voting runs October 19 through 30, and Election Day is November 3. The <a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026 guide</a> lists every early voting site.</p>

##### What Props A and B would cost a homeowner
If voters approve both City bonds, the City estimates the average homeowner would pay $41.60 more a year, KXAN reported from the City's election guide; KUT earlier reported the City's estimate as $40 a year for a $495,000 home. Proposition A's $260 million includes $65 million for aquatics, possibly at pools such as Garrison and Big Stacy. Proposition B's $35 million could go to the Hampton at Oak Hill branch and a future Colony Park branch library. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/your-local-election-hq/austinites-to-vote-on-295m-bonds-city-charter-amendments/">KXAN</a></span> <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/austin/2026-10-13/austin-tx-district-5-city-council-race-november-election">KUT</a></span>

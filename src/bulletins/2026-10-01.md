<p class="morning-note">Good morning, Austin. It is finally raining, and it means to keep at it. The Hill Country has been under it
since before dawn, five inches at Junction already, and our turn comes late morning into the afternoon: a high of 90 falling
to 85, then two to three inches more overnight. The upside is real. The air is the cleanest it has been in weeks, pollen is
low, and Friday tops out at 82 with a north wind behind it, which is summer finally packing. The downside is every low
crossing in this county. Check atxfloods.com before you drive through anything, and if you are headed to Zilker tomorrow,
pack for mud. Sun up at 7:24, though you will have to take that one on faith.</p>

### The rain is upstream, and the sirens aren't up

More than five inches of rain fell on Junction by 5 a.m. Mason County took 2.5 to 3 inches. Storms have held west of Interstate
35 this morning and reach the Austin metro late morning into the afternoon, under a moderate flash flood risk, 3 out
of 4, across most of the area.

What falls here is not the whole of it. CBS Austin meteorologist Avery Tomasco said the Llano River is Thursday's biggest
concern: two to four inches have already landed on the western end of its watershed, and that water is headed downstream
toward the Highland Lakes. Austin City Limits (ACL) opens Friday at Zilker Park, and the city says the festival goes ahead
unless C3 Presents says otherwise. A cancellation or an evacuation is C3's call; Austin Emergency Management reviews the
plans and would help carry one out.

Travis County says all of its departments are ready, with helicopters stationed around the county. Not the sirens.
County spokesperson Hector Nieto said contracts have moved forward for sirens near Maha, Onion and Sandy creeks, and
that the county wants them running as soon as possible. Asked whether any would be installed before this week's rain, he
said, "I don't have an answer to that."

#### Money & bills

##### Austin ISD lost students, and its projected deficit went to $105 million
Enrollment at the Austin Independent School District (AISD) is 66,032 this year, down from about 69,000 last year and below
what the district had budgeted for. Chief Financial Officer Katrina Montgomery told the school board the projected deficit
rose from $19 million to $105 million. The district says it anticipates cutting about $60 million next
year. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/austin-isd-enrollment-continues-to-fall/">KXAN</a></span>

<p class="countdown">1 day until the Austin City Limits (ACL) Festival's first weekend &middot; Gates open Friday at Zilker Park; the festival runs Oct. 2-4 and Oct. 9-11.</p>

<p class="good-thing">Jay Trachtenberg has been to every Austin City Limits festival. That spans 25 years. The KUTX program manager and longtime midday host told KUT that they mostly run together now, but he can still place the first one, in 2002, when a few thousand people wandered a comfortable, half-empty Zilker and everyone assumed it would be the only year. He remembers Stevie Wonder, Paul McCartney, Lizzo. The one that stuck was Ben Harper's set in year two, when the whole park drifted to one stage on a beautiful night: "For me, it really felt like this magical moment of the whole thing coming together." In the dusty years he used to slip out to Barton Springs mid-festival and float there alone with the music still carrying over the water. "That was a real treat for two or three years," he said. He is clear-eyed about what the festival became as the city filled up, and goes anyway. "I take it in stride at this point," he said. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/life-arts/2026-10-01/austin-tx-kutx-jay-trachtenberg-acl-25th-anniversary">KUT</a></span></p>

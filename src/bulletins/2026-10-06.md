### Texas insurance costs put roof upgrades on lawmakers' agenda

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-06/texas-insurance-premiums.png" alt="Texas Department of Insurance statewide average annual homeowners premiums: $1,791 in 2016 and a preliminary $3,489 in 2025. <figcaption>Graphic: The Austin Bulletin &middot; Texas Department of Insurance, current overview; 2025 preliminary.</figcaption>
</figure>

The roof is part of the insurance bill. At an October 5 House hearing, insurance officials and industry representatives discussed whether stronger roofs and other home improvements could reduce disaster losses, Community Impact reported. No new homeowner grant was enacted at that hearing.

The numbers need care. The Texas Department of Insurance's annual-premium chart lists $1,791 in 2016 and a preliminary $3,489 in 2025, sourced to its Texas Statistical Plan for Residential Risks. Community Impact reports $3,506 for 2025 and links the same overview. We have not established that the two figures use the same policy scope; our graphic reproduces the department's labeled annual series. Neither figure tells an individual Austin homeowner what a renewal will cost.

According to the report, the department asked lawmakers to consider grants for weather-proofing homes. Governor Greg Abbott has sought insurer consideration of roof-fortification programs when setting rates. Industry representative Chelsea Stallings told the committee that reducing losses is necessary before premiums can fall. Similar Texas grant proposals did not pass in 2025.

These are proposals, with no guaranteed discount or newly available grant established here. The hearing also considered possible sales-tax exemptions for roofing materials and catastrophe savings accounts.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Any new grant or tax exemption would require further action; Monday's hearing did not create one.</p>

##### An insurance investigation includes an Austin surgery bill
Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton is investigating UnitedHealth over alleged coverage denials, including an Austin patient's reported approval reversal after a procedure at RedBud Surgery Center. His office is seeking documents to determine whether laws were violated. UnitedHealth told KXAN it had no response to share at the time; the allegations are not findings. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/texas/ken-paxton-investigates-unitedhealth-over-alleged-coverage-denials/">KXAN</a></span>

#### Public safety & courts

##### Home explosion review identifies emergency communication gaps
KXAN reports that a new City review of the April 2025 Double Spur Loop propane explosion found responders handled the immediate threat, but communication and coordination problems created confusion. Six people were injured in the explosion. The report recommends dedicated emergency communication channels, better notifications to City leaders and coordination among Austin and neighboring counties. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/austin-home-explosion-report/">KXAN</a></span>

##### Fire staffing has a deadline before a shorter workweek
Austin Fire Division Chief Mark Bridges says the department needs 54 funded new positions plus around 40 other vacancies filled before a shorter workweek begins October 3, 2027. CBS Austin reports that the labor agreement protects four-person engine staffing. Bridges said unfilled positions would require more overtime; the schedule change remains planned regardless of hiring. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/time-is-ticking-for-afd-to-hire-nearly-100-firefighters-to-reduce-workweeks-next-year">CBS Austin</a></span>

#### Growth & infrastructure

##### Large water users could face additional conservation rules
Austin Water took public comments Monday on possible conservation plans, reclaimed-water use and water budgets for major customers, CBS Austin reports. The utility currently defines a large user as one consuming more than 85 million gallons annually. No new requirements were adopted at the open house; recommendations would go to City Council for consideration. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/community-weighs-in-as-austin-considers-tougher-rules-for-major-water-users">CBS Austin</a></span>

The City's October 5 mobility bulletin describes CTXGO, a free app and website launched October 1, for regional construction information, roadway conditions and travel options. <span class="src"><a href="https://mailchi.mp/austintexas/austin-mobility-news-10-05-26#mctoc1">City of Austin</a></span>

##### Fifth Street utility work starts the week of October 12

#### City Hall & county

##### Travis County works through delayed registration records
Travis County had about 8,000 delayed records remaining from an original 32,000 sent by the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS), registrar Celia Israel told KXAN Monday. She said her team expected to finish by October 19, the first day of early voting. The October 5 application deadline has passed; residents can check their existing registration records. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/texas-politics/texas-counties-work-through-dps-voter-registration-backlog-on-deadline-day/">KXAN</a></span>

#### Schools

##### Austin ISD is selected for a state spending review
Austin Independent School District (AISD) is one of four districts selected for a comptroller spending review, with a report expected by December 31. Comptroller Don Huffines says it will examine legal compliance and classroom spending. His Democratic challenger, Senator Sarah Eckhardt, criticized additional bureaucracy and questioned excluding state-taken-over districts. Selection does not establish wrongdoing. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/austin-isd-spending-investigation/">KXAN</a></span>

Separately, Austin Current reports AISD would bear costs of possible Texas Education Agency intervention; any district bill remains unknown while the agency’s plans are pending. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/10/06/aisd-school-austin-takeover-tea/">Austin Current</a></span>

Wednesday, October 7, is National Walk and Roll to School Day; the City's current mobility bulletin links participation information and guidance for supervised bike groups. <span class="src"><a href="https://mailchi.mp/austintexas/austin-mobility-news-10-05-26#mctoc4">City of Austin</a></span>

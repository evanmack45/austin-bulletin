### The budget cut has a walking route

![Timeline of the Austin Independent School District's high school bus changes: the district adopted a $205 million reduction package for the 2026-27 school year; it opened the year with 98 bus hubs an average of 2.5 miles from students' homes, replacing neighborhood stops; Communities In Schools closed a campaign at more than $354,200 on Aug. 31 to cover what the budget could not; and by Sept. 2 the district had 108 hubs about 1.5 miles from homes, with transportation savings put at $2.3 million, while Austin Ed Fund delivered $350,000 to 48 teacher-led projects](/images/2026-09-02/aisd-bus-hubs.png)
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · Austin ISD figures, via KUT and Community Impact</figcaption>

The Austin Independent School District (Austin ISD) replaced neighborhood bus stops for high schoolers this year with
hubs at elementary schools, transit stops and community centers. The change saves an estimated $2.3 million. It also
left Margaret, a freshman at Anderson High School, with three ways home: the shoulder of Loop 360 with no sidewalk, a
trail that takes more than an hour, or a scramble down a concrete embankment and across Bull Creek. She said she nearly
had heatstroke the first time. Her mother, Daphne Harrington, left work early to collect her, which cut her pay. "I
realize there's budget cuts, but it shouldn't come at the expense of the safety of our children," Harrington said.

The district has been adjusting. Kris Hafezizadeh, who runs Austin ISD transportation, said his team read more than
3,500 comments and has gone from 98 hubs to 108, shortening the average walk from 2.5 miles to about 1.5. Every added
stop costs. A new route runs about $65,000 a year, he said.

Anderson itself lost its cooling on Tuesday. The chiller was reaching 40 percent of the campus and classrooms were
hitting the mid-80s, so Principal Brian Lancaster released students at 12:15 p.m., and a 400-ton rental chiller went in
overnight.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Hafezizadeh said a stop will be added by the Harrington family's apartment complex as soon as Friday.</p>

#### Public safety & courts

##### A deported Austin man, a citizen by birth, is allowed back into the country
Brian José Morales García, 25, who lived and worked in Austin, returned to Texas on Sunday, his lawyers said, after the
federal government agreed to let him re-enter while his lawsuit proceeds. He was deported to Mexico in April following
a traffic stop near Fredericksburg. The Department of Homeland Security disputed his account and said he entered the
country illegally; his lawyers submitted his Colorado birth record. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/02/texas-deported-citizen-brian-morales-ice-partnerships-local-police-sheriffs-287g/">The Texas Tribune</a> / <a href="https://www.kut.org/texas/2026-08-31/feds-allow-return-of-austin-man-a-us-citizen-who-was-deported-after-traffic-stop">KUT</a></span>

#### Growth & infrastructure

##### Lake Austin drops a dozen feet in October, and the marinas are counting
The Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) will lower Lake Austin about a dozen feet between Oct. 12 and Nov. 30 at the
city's request, closing public boat ramps and swimming at Emma Long Metro Park. David Callejas, who owns Float On Boat
Rentals, said October is one of his best months, and estimated he will lose hundreds of thousands of dollars and put
about 20 employees out of work. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.fox7austin.com/news/lake-austin-business-owners-concerned-about-economic-impact-lake-drawdown-fall">FOX 7 Austin</a></span>

##### An Austin school Texas rejected as a charter is now taking voucher money
Alpha School, founded in Austin, was turned down by the State Board of Education last summer when it sought to run
charter campuses on a model where software teaches lessons in two hours a day and adults handle motivation. It has
since roughly quadrupled to more than 30 Texas campuses, most of them accepted into the state's voucher program by the
comptroller's office, ProPublica and The Texas Tribune found. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/02/alpha-school-texas-vouchers-ai/">The Texas Tribune</a></span>

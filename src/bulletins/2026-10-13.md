### Two city bonds on the ballot: what they build, and what they cost

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-13/bond-split.png" alt="Bar chart of the two City of Austin bond propositions on the November 3 ballot, as reported by Community Impact: Proposition A, $260 million for park improvements and expansions; Proposition B, $20 million to improve and expand the Hampton Branch at Oak Hill and $15 million for a future branch in Colony Park.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin &middot; Community Impact</figcaption>
</figure>

Austin voters will decide two city bond measures on the November 3 ballot, and early voting opens Monday. Proposition A asks for $260 million for parks: more than two dozen trail, pool, recreation center and playground projects, plus land purchases citywide. Proposition B asks for $35 million for two library branches, Community Impact reports.

The City has not said how much each named project would get.

The Council voted 8-3 on August 4 to prepare the election, then ordered it without objection two days later. Council Member Paige Ellis said the money would be reinvested in "the parts of town that don't always get that level of investment." Mayor Kirk Watson, who voted no with José Velásquez and Marc Duchen, said, "I don't believe it is the time to negatively impact affordability."

<p class="correction">Correction: an earlier version said the Council sent the package to voters 8-3. The 8-3 vote, on August 4, directed staff to prepare the election; the Council ordered it on August 6 without objection.</p>

If both pass, the City expects to issue about $49 million in debt a year from 2027 to 2032. Its own estimate, reported by KUT, is a $40 yearly increase on an average-valued home of $495,000.

Repaying the $295 million would also mean nearly $220 million in projected interest.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Early voting runs from October 19; Election Day is November 3. Our <a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026 page</a> lists the ballot and every Travis County early voting site.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### Debt payments already take more than $500 of a typical city tax bill
Debt service taxes for the typical Austin homeowner topped $500 this year, just over 23% of the city property tax bill, and are expected to keep rising into the 2030s whether or not the November bonds pass, Community Impact reports. The City has nearly $2.4 billion in outstanding debt, and more than $760 million from bonds approved in 2016 through 2022 remains unspent. <span class="src"><a href="https://communityimpact.com/central-austin/election/parks-library-funding-on-the-austin-ballot/">Community Impact</a></span>

Council Member Mike Siegel, who supports the bonds, said a family of four's free day at a city pool is "worth far more than $7," calling the package an investment in affordable activities. <span class="src"><a href="https://communityimpact.com/central-austin/election/parks-library-funding-on-the-austin-ballot/">Community Impact</a></span>

Travis County commissioners approved up to $5.65 million to repair a closed county landfill beneath the Austin Country Flea Market on East US 290 and cover legal costs in three lawsuits. <span class="src"><a href="https://communityimpact.com/central-austin/government/travis-county-approves-up-to-5-65m-for-legal-fight-cleanup-at-closed-landfill/">Community Impact</a></span>

#### Public safety & courts

##### Norwood killing suspect had felonies dismissed a month earlier
Jesse Carl Monroe II, 33, charged with murder in the October 6 killing of Jeffery Grable at the Capital Metro Norwood Transit Center, has had 38 criminal charges filed against him in Travis County since 2011, court records reviewed by KXAN show. On September 4, five 2022 felony charges against him were dismissed when he was offered pretrial diversion in a plea agreement. Records show years of competency concerns. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/crime/large-piece-of-concrete-allegedly-used-by-austin-murder-suspect/">KXAN</a></span>

#### City Hall & county

##### Alter faces three challengers in District 5
Council Member Ryan Alter is seeking a second term representing District 5, in South and Southwest Austin, against David Weinberg, Liz Brink and Farrah Abraham, KUT reports. Alter said housing costs are lower than in any other major city in the country. Weinberg said the Council is "pretty distant" from residents' priorities. Abraham said families are squeezed while parks and roads are neglected. Brink did not respond to KUT. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/austin/2026-10-13/austin-tx-district-5-city-council-race-november-election">KUT</a></span>

On Proposition I, the charter amendment requiring independent external audits, Alter is opposed, saying the Council already passed an audit; Weinberg and Abraham support it. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/austin/2026-10-13/austin-tx-district-5-city-council-race-november-election">KUT</a></span>

Alter and Weinberg told KUT they support the parks and library bonds; Abraham said she supports strategic investments, "but taxpayers deserve complete transparency before approving new debt." <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/austin/2026-10-13/austin-tx-district-5-city-council-race-november-election">KUT</a></span>

Early voting begins Monday, October 19, for the November 3 election; our <a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026 page</a> lists every Travis County early voting site and its hours. <span class="src"><a href="https://communityimpact.com/central-austin/election/what-to-know-early-voting-begins-oct-19-in-texas-midterm-election/">Community Impact</a></span>

#### Schools

##### Burnet and Webb petition deadline is Thursday
Parents at the Austin Independent School District's (AISD) Burnet and Webb middle schools have until Thursday, October 15, to sign petitions asking the state to close the two campuses. Each petition needs parents of more than 50% of enrolled students. As of October 9, parents of 11% of Burnet students and 2% of Webb students had signed, Austin Current reported. The Austin Council of Parent Teacher Associations joined the push. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/10/09/austin-austinisd-burnet-webb-petition-state-takeover/">Austin Current</a></span>

A successful petition would not rule out a broader takeover of AISD: Education Commissioner Mike Morath could still appoint a board or a conservator. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/10/09/austin-austinisd-burnet-webb-petition-state-takeover/">Austin Current</a></span>

The University of Texas at Austin's Dell Medical School is partnering on two federal projects meant to shorten the 12 to 18 months it takes to start a clinical trial. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/10/12/dell-medical-school-partners-with-federal-program-to-standardize-clinical-trials/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

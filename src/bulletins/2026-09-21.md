### A federal officer shot a man on Anderson Square. The city spent Sunday asking to help investigate.

A U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot a man in the 8000 block of Anderson Square on Sunday
afternoon. Austin-Travis County Emergency Medical Services was notified at 12:56 p.m. and found him with a gunshot wound
to the torso. His attorney, Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch, identified him as Wilber Rafael Garces Perez, 28, a Venezuelan who
was working as a food delivery driver. Austin police were not involved.

That last fact is the whole problem. The city has no automatic claim on a shooting its own officers did not fire, so it
spent the afternoon asking. Police Chief Lisa Davis sent the unit that handles officer-involved shootings and asked the
Texas Rangers to assist. The Travis County District Attorney's office requested to be a "co-equal partner" and access to
federal evidence, saying the incident may have included violations of state law. Mayor Kirk Watson said ICE
investigating itself "would not be appropriate."

Neither ICE nor the Department of Homeland Security has given an account of the operation, and Davis said she had not
been briefed by federal authorities. Council Member Mike Siegel said federal officers and state police kept local police
off the scene for hours. A crowd gathered at Research Boulevard and Anderson Square. Officers used pepper balls.

By about 4:30 p.m. Garces Perez was out of the hospital and in ICE custody. Late Sunday night, Lincoln-Goldfinch said
the family still did not know where he was being held.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The Austin Police Department, the Texas Rangers and Homeland Security Investigations are each expected to investigate, with ICE running its own review; city officials have asked for dash and body camera footage to be released. More in <a href="#public-safety-courts">Public safety &amp; courts</a>.</p>

##### Data centers are now a governor's race argument about your power bill
Gov. Greg Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas in June to make data center companies fully fund their
electric needs and keep grid upgrade costs off Texas ratepayers, and in September told the Texas Water Development Board
to penalize centers that break water-reporting law. His opponent, Austin state Rep. Gina Hinojosa, wants a moratorium
until the Legislature meets. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/texas-politics/data-centers-and-the-texas-gubernatorial-race/">KXAN</a></span>

#### Schools

##### Six Austin schools just inherited failing grades they did not earn
The Texas Education Agency (TEA) reassigned the accountability histories of six closed Austin Independent School
District campuses to the schools taking most of their students. Bailey Middle School earned a B and now carries four
consecutive F ratings. Nine district schools, not three, start the year one failing grade away from triggering state
intervention. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/21/austin-austinisd-takeover-school-ratings-paredes-bailey/">Austin Current</a></span>

Education Commissioner Mike Morath was not obliged to move the ratings, said University of Houston researcher Toni Templeton: he "chose to do this, and it was not his only option." <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/21/austin-austinisd-takeover-school-ratings-paredes-bailey/">Austin Current</a></span>

TEA called the district's late-July closure of Paredes Middle School "abrupt and reactive" and has changed its policy to require all campus closure requests by May 31. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/21/austin-austinisd-takeover-school-ratings-paredes-bailey/">Austin Current</a></span>

The district appealed the failing 2025-26 ratings at Burnet and Webb middle schools last week; a fifth F at either could force the state to close it or replace the board. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/18/austin-austinisd-takeover-tea-ratings-burnet-webb/">Austin Current</a></span>

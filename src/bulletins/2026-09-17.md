### A monthly bus pass is $41.25. The plan takes it to $66.

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-17/capmetro-monthly-pass.png" alt="Bar chart of CapMetro&apos;s proposed local calendar-month pass prices, which cover bus, Pickup and UT Shuttle service. The standard pass is 41 dollars and 25 cents now, would rise to 49 dollars and 50 cents on January 10, 2027, and to 66 dollars in January 2029. The reduced-rate pass is 20 dollars and 60 cents now, would rise to 24 dollars and 75 cents in January 2027, and to 33 dollars in January 2029. The CapMetro board votes on the proposal September 28, 2026.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · CapMetro</figcaption>
</figure>

CapMetro has not raised a fare since 2015. Eleven years is a long time to hold a price, and the agency spent them launching
Rapid service in east Austin, running key routes more often, expanding its on-demand Pickup vans, standing up a transit
police department and rebuilding how riders pay. Now it wants some of that back.

The proposal in front of its board raises fares twice, in January 2027 and again in January 2029. A single local ride goes
from $1.25 to $1.50, then to $2.00. The monthly pass climbs faster. Chief Financial Officer Kevin Conlan told board members
the first step brings in roughly $3.25 million a year in fare revenue and the second another $1.1 million.

Not every rider pays more. CapMetro is folding Equifare, its discount for households under 200% of the federal poverty
level, into the deeper Reduced Fare program, so those riders move from 20% off to 50% off. Veterans, and households at or
below 150% of the poverty level, would become newly eligible.

Riders and transit workers raised concerns anyway at Wednesday's hearing. Peyton Rowlands, who drives a CapMetro bus, said
most of the bad moments with riders come from collecting the fare. Conlan said the terms are not expected to change before
the board votes.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The CapMetro board votes on the fare proposal Sept. 28, and the voter registration deadline is Oct. 5 (<a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026</a>).</p>

#### Schools

##### Whether a parent petition can stop a takeover is still unclear
State rules say the education commissioner "shall" order the action a valid petition requests, once parents of a majority
of students at a campus sign it. TEA declined to tell Austin Current whether that limits Commissioner Mike Morath's
separate authority to impose other sanctions. Burnet and Webb middle schools have each drawn a fifth consecutive failing
rating. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/16/austin-austinisd-parent-petition-takeover/">Austin Current</a></span>

##### The University of Texas stops enrolling students in three majors
Mexican American and Latina/o studies, African and African diaspora studies and women's and gender studies are closed to
new students, a university spokesperson confirmed, and those already enrolled may finish. Interim chair Danielle Pilar
Clealand told faculty the provost's office had rejected the department's proposal to keep them. Urban studies and American
studies stay. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/education/2026-09-16/university-of-texas-austin-cuts-ethnic-gender-studies-majors-dei">KUT</a></span>

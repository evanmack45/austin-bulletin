<p class="morning-note">Good morning, Austin. Three days of rain and we are still in it: a Flood Watch holds until seven
tonight, another inch or two is coming, and the high only reaches 81. The lake is going the right way for once. Water off the
Llano is working downstream and the Lower Colorado River Authority expects Lake Travis to pick up a foot or two by Sunday.
Three days of rain have washed the air clean, and there is nothing in it to sneeze at either. If you are
walking into Zilker today, the ground has been soaked since Wednesday and no amount of optimism will dry it out by noon.
Boots, not canvas. Check atxfloods.com before you take any low crossing, and give the creeks the rest of the weekend.</p>

### The bullet is out. The questions aren't.

Surgeons removed the bullet from Wilber Garcés Pérez's back on Thursday, about 11 days after a U.S. Immigration and Customs
Enforcement (ICE) officer shot him in north Austin. He was delivering food at the time. His lawyers, Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch
and Daniel Woodward of the Texas Civil Rights Project, said ICE would not let his wife into the operating room.

What the record holds about the shooting is thinner. At a federal hearing Wednesday, Department of Homeland Security (DHS)
special agent Rick Bazan testified that the officer who fired wore no body camera, and that the second officer at the scene
had switched his own off before arriving. Bazan said neither he nor his team had interviewed the officer who fired, or asked
him whether the car struck him. A separate DHS unit is examining that.

Garcés Pérez stays in custody. U.S. Magistrate Judge Dustin M. Howell found him a flight risk and rejected the government's
argument that he endangers the community. His defense attorney said he had been working toward legal residency; DHS says he
had a work permit that expired in 2025 and a final order of removal. He is charged with assaulting, resisting and impeding a
federal officer. If convicted, he faces up to 20 years.

##### Travis County's new budget took effect, and most tax bills tick down
The Travis County Commissioners Court adopted a $2.3 billion budget that began Thursday, putting more money into affordable
housing, mental healthcare and disaster preparedness. County taxes make up about a fifth of an Austin property tax bill.

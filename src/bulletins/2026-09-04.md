### The machines arrive first, the rulebook second

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-04/driverless-comfort.png" alt="Bar chart of a Pew Research Center survey of U.S. adults taken in February, showing how comfortable they would be riding in a driverless car: 70 percent said not too or not at all comfortable, 16 percent said somewhat comfortable, and 7 percent said extremely or very comfortable">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Pew Research Center, via The Texas Tribune</figcaption>
</figure>

Dozens of gold Cybercabs went into service on Austin streets Thursday. They seat two, they are hailed through
Tesla's Robotaxi app, and they have no steering wheel and no pedals, so a passenger who wants to intervene has
nothing to reach for. Austin has had Tesla robotaxis since June of last year. Those cars kept their controls.

The same afternoon, at a news conference and a City Council mobility committee meeting, Austin police set out what
they now expect of a very different machine. After a review with Austin Transportation and Public Works and the city
attorney's office, the city concluded that e-motos (pedal-less electric bikes with motors of 750 watts or more) are
motor vehicles under state law. Riders need registration, insurance and a license. No sidewalks, no Butler
Hike-and-Bike Trail. Officers will warn first.

Two machines, one rulebook, and in both cases the machine got here first. Assistant Chief Mike Chancellor said his
officers are called out to children riding e-motos through neighborhoods at 40 to 50 miles an hour. He said the
department has no figures yet on how many citations it has written since the campaign began this spring.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> State troopers run Operation CARE, the Crash Awareness Reduction Effort, on Texas highways from Friday through Monday.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### Austin puts its adult leagues back on the schedule
The Parks and Recreation Department will keep running adult indoor basketball and volleyball at the Austin
Recreation Center after players objected, then hand them to an outside company later. It had announced the
cancellation after the city adopted its budget last month, alongside cuts to youth outreach, senior programs and the
lending library. "The city needs additional time to find a suitable third-party vendor," the city said. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/03/austin-tx-adult-indoor-basketball-volleyball-outsourced-parks-and-recreation/">Austin Current</a></span>

Texas Comptroller Don Huffines asked lawmakers to add $1 billion to the private school voucher program for 2028-29, which would take it past $2 billion. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/03/texas-school-voucher-program-2-billion-expansion-budget-comptroller/">The Texas Tribune</a></span>

#### Public safety & courts

##### City leaders say the immigration arrests in North Austin have not let up
Mayor Pro Tem Chito Vela told the Austin Chronicle there has been a sustained increase in Immigration and Customs
Enforcement (ICE) activity in the North Lamar and Rundberg areas, and that the city had heard nothing from ICE or
the Department of Homeland Security about it. U.S. Rep. Greg Casar said at an Aug. 14 news conference in Rundberg
that government sources had described arrests running three to four times higher. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.austinchronicle.com/news/with-more-ice-reported-in-north-austin-city-leaders-urge-residents-to-be-prepared/">Austin Chronicle</a></span>

##### Texas keeps its own camera network switched on
Gov. Greg Abbott blocked state agencies from spending on Flock Safety cameras on Aug. 27, but the Department of
Public Safety (DPS) said it will keep using the ones already installed and only stop adding more. The Texas Tribune
reviewed a month of searches on the network and found 48 percent came from outside Texas. After those questions, DPS
said Thursday it will restrict searches to agencies that have signed agreements with it. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/04/texas-flock-cameras-dps-network-search-abbott/">The Texas Tribune</a></span>

##### A university department proposes cutting two programs and building one
The University of Texas at Austin's new Department of Social and Cultural Analysis proposed eliminating the race,
indigeneity and migration major and the LGBTQ studies minor and creating a six-track interdisciplinary major,
according to internal documents obtained by The Daily Texan. The documents say the two overlap other offerings. The
university and the department chair did not answer the paper's requests for comment. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/09/03/cola-recommends-adding-social-and-cultural-analysis-major-cutting-race-indigeneity-and-migration-major-lgbtq-minor-in-internal-document/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

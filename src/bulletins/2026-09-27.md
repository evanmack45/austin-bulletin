### The warrant came from the videos, six days later

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-27/ramos-six-days.png" alt="Timeline of the arrest of Austin City Council District 1 candidate Misael Ramos. Misael Ramos was walking his dog on Manor Road on Saturday morning when Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS)
troopers stopped him. He is 40, a community organizer, and one of six candidates for the District 1 seat on the Austin
City Council. By 9:31 a.m. he was booked into the Travis County Jail on a Class B misdemeanor charge of riot
participation. Ramos denies wrongdoing.

The charge dates to Sept. 20, when a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot Wilber Rafael Garcés
Pérez in North Austin and a protest followed. DPS said Ramos "was captured on camera taking construction barricades,
sandbags, and other items and attempting to obstruct traffic." The arrest affidavit says troopers identified him using
"open-source social media videos." His attorney, Edmund "Skip" Davis, told supporters outside the jail that he had seen
no footage resembling a riot, and that the method troubled him: "It strikes me that they're probably relying on
artificial intelligence and facial recognition to identify people."

The same Saturday, Austin's Office of Police Oversight held its second community expo at City Hall.
Director Gail McCant said what residents tell her office they want is accountability in policing. That office reviews
the Austin Police Department (APD). It has no authority over state troopers.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> More protests over the shooting and detention of Garcés Pérez were planned across Austin for Sunday, KUT reported; no court date has been set in the Ramos case. The rest of the week's arrests and oversight are in <a href="#public-safety-courts">Public safety &amp; courts</a>, and the Nov. 3 ballot in <a href="#city-hall-county">City Hall &amp; county</a>.</p>

#### Public safety & courts

##### State troopers arrested a council candidate six days after the protest
DPS troopers stopped Misael Ramos, 40, in the 2400 block of Manor Road at about 8:45 a.m. Saturday and booked him at
9:31 on a riot participation charge. He was released just after 5 p.m. on a $1,000 bond. DPS said he was filmed moving
barricades and sandbags to obstruct traffic. His campaign called the arrest political
retribution. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/crime-justice/2026-09-26/austin-texas-tx-city-council-candidate-arrested-ice-protest">KUT</a></span>

One condition of Ramos's release bars him from further protests, and bond conditions in related cases order people to stay 200 yards from the shooting site and the Texas Capitol. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.austinchronicle.com/news/misael-ramos-details-harrowing-arrest-for-protesting-ice/">Austin Chronicle</a></span>

#### City Hall & county

##### Seven counties have now counted the registrations the state lost track of
Travis County received about 32,000 backlogged applications this week, part of a statewide DPS failure that Harris County put at roughly 108,000 records. Registration for the Nov. 3 election closes Oct. 5. The Secretary of State says all the applications will be backdated to when they were submitted, and that they reached counties in time to be processed. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/state-of-texas/state-of-texas-counties-race-to-process-delayed-voter-registration-records/">KXAN</a></span>

#### Schools

##### University workers rallied near campus over who decides what UT teaches
Members of the Texas State Employees Union gathered near the University of Texas at Austin on Friday as their General Assembly opened. Organizer Tom Alter, a former Texas State University professor, said entire departments have been consolidated and eliminated. A university spokesperson said faculty were asked to justify courses for core status and that about half of those reviewed were accepted. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kvue.com/article/news/education/university-of-texas/university-texas-workers-rally-academic-freedom-curriculum-changes/269-bb2f4a38-ea66-4384-aff0-9ca3ddb15aa1">KVUE</a></span>

##### A billion-dollar school voucher program is on the ballot through the comptroller's race
Acting Comptroller Don Huffines, who says Texas Education Freedom Accounts funded 101,600 students and wants the program marketed to every family, faces state Sen. Sarah Eckhardt of Austin on Nov. 3. Eckhardt says she would audit the program vigorously, calling it "in all probability, going to become a runaway subsidy." The comptroller administers the money. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/education-freedom-accounts-and-the-texas-comptroller-race/">KXAN</a></span>

Women's and gender studies courses stop counting toward UT's core graduation requirements next semester and will be offered as electives; the new core phases in from spring 2027. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kvue.com/article/news/education/university-of-texas/university-texas-workers-rally-academic-freedom-curriculum-changes/269-bb2f4a38-ea66-4384-aff0-9ca3ddb15aa1">KVUE</a></span>

### Cheaper rent, fuller eviction court

![Timeline of Austin rents and Travis County eviction filings: the Austin Apartment Association counts about 86,000 rental units added since 2023; Senate Bill 38 took effect Jan. 1, 2026, limiting local courts from requiring mediation or a pretrial conference before an eviction trial; in June 2026 the median asking rent was $1,371, down 4.3 percent from a year earlier, and Travis County saw roughly 1,500 eviction filings, about 60 percent above the baseline; and over the most recent 12 months there were more than 15,600 filings, 35 percent above the 2023-24 average, or about one case for every 20 renter households](/images/2026-09-01/rent-and-evictions.png)
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · Eviction Lab and Realtor.com figures, via CBS Austin</figcaption>

Austin renters are paying less than they were a year ago. Realtor.com put the median asking rent at $1,371 in June,
down 4.3 percent, and the Austin Apartment Association credits about 86,000 rental units added since 2023 for the
slide. Eviction court has not noticed.

CBS Austin reported Monday that Travis County logged more than 15,600 eviction filings over the most recent 12 months,
35 percent above the 2023-24 average and about one case for every 20 renter households. More than a quarter of the
judgments landlords ask for are under $2,000, said Building and Strengthening Tenant Action (BASTA), an Austin tenant
group. "This should not be happening," said Abia Bakay, its research and policy director.

Emily Blair, who runs the Austin Apartment Association, said rent is the one line in a household budget that has come
down. "Household expenses on rent are actually going down right now and have over the last few years, but every other
cost has gone up," Blair said. She said rental assistance programs have shrunk since the pandemic, and that eviction
is usually a last step. The rules around that step changed on Jan. 1: Senate Bill 38 limits local courts from
requiring mediation or a pretrial conference before an eviction goes to trial.

#### Money & bills

##### Austin-area home sales rose 7 percent, and the listings are still piling up
Homes.com put the Austin metro's median home price at $439,995 in July, up 0.6 percent from a year earlier, with
sales up 7 percent. There were 14,200 homes listed in July, against a pre-pandemic average of 11,700. Senior market
analyst Israel Linares said builders in the outer suburbs are discounting hard to clear inventory, and that buyers
are returning. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/housing/austin-metro-home-sales-up-7-from-2025/">KXAN</a></span>

##### Texas hospitals start the fiscal year down $27 million a day
The state's fiscal year began Tuesday, and Texas hospitals expect to lose $27 million a day in additional Medicaid
money after the Trump administration withheld approval for about $9.8 billion, most of it from the Comprehensive
Hospital Increase Reimbursement Program (CHIRP). Local governments collect about $4 billion a year in taxes from
hospitals, and the federal government matches that money under the program. Four million Texans are on Medicaid. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/31/texas-hospitals-medicaid-funding-chirp/">The Texas Tribune</a></span>

Springdale Road on Aug. 27. <span class="src"><a href="https://communityimpact.com/east-austin/government/austin-to-spend-335m-on-evidence-storage-lease-as-public-safety-warehouse-faces-delays/">Community Impact</a></span>

thunderstorms after 1 p.m. There are no active alerts for Travis County. Tonight: partly cloudy, low around 78.

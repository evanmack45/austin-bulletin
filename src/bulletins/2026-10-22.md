### Eviction help returns to a Council that is checking what its social service money buys

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-22/i-belong-funding.png" alt="Bar chart from KXAN and the City of Austin's October 22 Council agenda: the I Belong in Austin eviction-prevention program had a $4 million budget until Council trimmed it to $3 million last year; on October 22 Council considers adding $2.25 million to the program's contract.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin &middot; KXAN; City of Austin Council agenda, item 22</figcaption>
</figure>

Two items share one question.

Item 22 on this morning's Council agenda would add $2.25 million to the City's contract with El Buen Samaritano for I Belong in Austin. The program began in 2023 as emergency rent help. After Council trimmed its budget from $4 million to $3 million last year, City staff focused it on legal partners who negotiate settlements in eviction cases, KXAN reported. El Buen's chief executive, Rosamaria Murillo, said up to 1,600 residents could lose rental support without the money. The City told KXAN that 74% of participants kept stable housing. The item sits on the consent agenda.

Council also considers a proposal to add geographic data to the City's social service reports, the Austin Chronicle reported. It follows a September 24 vote extending 14 nonprofit contracts worth about $4.1 million; a staff memo noting compliance concerns at five of the groups reached Council nine minutes after that vote.

Mayor Kirk Watson ordered a review this summer of the more than $100 million the City spends on social services each year. "Contracting something out doesn't contract out our responsibility," he said, Community Impact reported. Working groups are due to finish before January.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Council meets at 10 a.m. today at City Hall; the social services review is expected to shape the 2027-28 budget.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### Austin ISD will stop paying into employee health savings accounts
Austin Independent School District (AISD) will end its $133 monthly contributions to health savings accounts in January 2027 for the 5,274 employees on high-deductible, employee-only plans, KVUE reported. The district says the change saves $9.2 million toward its budget deficit while it absorbs higher medical costs. Trasell Underwood of Education Austin, the employees' union, said some staff fear losing care they receive now. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kvue.com/article/news/education/schools/austin-isd-end-monthly-health-savings-account-contributions-employees-2027/269-ed9cc013-c6e0-496f-ac3a-f089ad0ee119">KVUE</a></span>

Council votes today on item 22, which adds $2.25 million to El Buen Samaritano's contract running I Belong in Austin, a program that helps renters facing eviction reach settlements. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/city-of-austin-eyes-2m-for-supporting-renters-on-brink-of-eviction/">KXAN</a></span>

##### One person killed in an east Austin motorcycle crash

#### Growth & infrastructure

##### Council takes up Austin's requests for Parmer Lane today
A preliminary recommendation presented to the Capital Area Metropolitan Planning Organization (CAMPO) would turn Parmer Lane, from MoPac (Loop 1) to Whitestone Boulevard, into a highway with frontage roads. Council Member Krista Laine's resolution, item 44, asks the City to press for walking, biking, transit and safe crossings instead. CAMPO consultant Eric Busker called the recommendations "bold and tough to implement" and said they are not final. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/10/22/austin-txdot-parmer-lane-highway-campo/">Austin Current</a></span>

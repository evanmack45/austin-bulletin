### The city debt that never goes on the ballot

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-14/co-cost.png" alt="Bar chart of the certificates of obligation the Austin City Council approved in August 2026, as reported by Austin Current: $142.6 million borrowed, an estimated $86.2 million in interest over the life of the debt, and a total estimated cost of nearly $229 million.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin &middot; Austin Current</figcaption>
</figure>

Austin voters will decide $295 million in park and library bonds on November 3. They had no vote on the $566 million the City Council has borrowed since 2024 through certificates of obligation, Austin Current reports. That is more than 60% of the $883 million in certificates Austin has issued over the last decade.

These never go to voters. Both kinds of debt are repaid through property taxes, and each $100 million adds $14 to the average tax bill. Some of the recent borrowing restored projects left out of this fall's smaller bond package: $33 million for sidewalks, urban trails and traffic programs, and $3 million for Austin Animal Services facilities.

Council Member Chito Vela said the City has to move fast to buy property. "No one's going to wait for us," he said. John Bonura of the Texas Public Policy Foundation, a conservative think tank, said it will ask lawmakers to limit certificates to emergencies, "not just to fulfill your wishlist that didn't get passed." A City spokesperson said policy and state law allow it when costs are urgent or unexpected, among other reasons.

In August alone, the Council approved $142.6 million in certificates, with an estimated $86.2 million in interest on top.

#### Growth & infrastructure

##### Light rail still waits on $4 billion in federal money
Austin Transit Partnership (ATP) is still seeking more than $4 billion in federal grants, about half the cost of the nearly 10-mile, 15-stop light rail line, KXAN reports. The U.S. Department of Transportation put the project at $8.2 billion in September. Federal officials estimate the construction grant could come in summer 2027, or as late as 2028. ATP expects to break ground in 2027. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/traffic/transportation/austin-light-rail-still-needs-billions-in-federal-funding/">KXAN</a></span>

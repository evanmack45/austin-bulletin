### Rent here is a bargain. The apartment is 45 square feet smaller.

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-15/st-john-site.png" alt="Timeline of the St. John redevelopment site at 800 East St. John Avenue in Austin: the city bought the land in 2013 and it sat empty for nearly 20 years; in 2017 the City Council directed staff to plan the site with neighbors; earlier in 2026 the city signed a redevelopment deal with the Housing Authority of the City of Austin and Greystar for 526 rental apartments, commercial space and an expanded park; on September 14, 2026 Airbnb put in 6.4 million dollars toward the 201 fully affordable apartments; crews are now clearing the site and final delivery is anticipated in 2028">
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · KXAN and CBS Austin, from the city and Airbnb</figcaption>
</figure>

RentCafe put Austin third in the country for renters on Monday, scoring 150 cities on 20 measures: a cost of living it
puts 4.4% below the national average, a steady job market, a quarter of the apartment stock newly built. Yardi Matrix,
which runs RentCafe, also counts what those new apartments are. The average new Austin unit is 871 square feet, down 45.
One-bedrooms are now 55% of new construction, up from about 53% a decade ago. Both things are true.

Doug Ressler of Yardi Matrix said the trade is smaller than it sounds. On a $1,500 budget a renter got about 780 square
feet here last year and 784 this year, because rents came down as supply arrived. Land and construction costs went up,
he said, so builders are fitting more units on each site.

Monday also brought money for the other kind of housing. Airbnb put $6.4 million into the St. John block the city has
owned since 2013, the piece that was missing for its 201 fully affordable apartments. That evening, six people running
for the East Austin council seat took questions about costs and displacement. In that district, HousingWorks Austin's
2024 scorecard has 44% of renters spending more than 30% of their income on housing.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> District 1 picks a council member Nov. 3; the registration deadline is Oct. 5 and early voting runs Oct. 19 through Oct. 30 (<a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026</a>).</p>

#### Growth & infrastructure

##### Airbnb puts $6.4 million into the St. John block, and crews start clearing
Airbnb chief executive Brian Chesky announced the money Monday at Austin Central Library, making Austin the first city
in the company's Housing Accelerator. It covers the 201 fully affordable apartments the city requires before a companion
325-unit mixed-income building can go up. The city has owned the site at 800 E. St. John Ave. since 2013. Chesky said
Airbnb has "in the past been part of the problem." <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/airbnb-announces-64m-investment-to-help-restart-long-stalled-st-john-housing-project">CBS Austin</a></span>

##### Texas' tallest building opens at Cesar Chavez and Red River
The Waterline stands over 1,000 feet, the 24th tallest building in the United States, and took more than four years to
finish. The project's construction company, DPR Construction, says crews put in 4.5 million hours and finished two
months early. The cheapest apartment listed is 778 square feet at $4,027 a month, fees included, parking
extra. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/business/2026-09-14/austin-tx-waterline-tower-tallest-building-texas">KUT</a></span>

Gov. Greg Abbott directed the Texas Water Development Board to penalize data centers that skip required water-use surveys; Democratic nominee Gina Hinojosa said Texans need real regulations and a special session. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/abbott-orders-penalties-for-data-centers-that-fail-to-report-water-use/">KXAN</a></span>

Texas has about 71,000 electricians and added nearly 25,000 construction jobs between June 2022 and June 2026, and data center work is driving the demand, trade groups told CBS Austin. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/the-hidden-workforce-behind-texas-data-center-boom">CBS Austin</a></span>

#### City Hall & county

##### A federal judge blocks the new mail-ballot rules, citing Travis County
U.S. District Judge Carl J. Nichols blocked the Postal Service rules Sunday in a suit brought by the Travis County
Clerk's Office, writing that they risk leaving mail ballots uncounted. The order behind them, signed by President Trump
in March, is aimed at "reducing the risk of fraud and protecting the integrity of Federal elections." The Supreme Court
ruled 7-2 against the administration Monday. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/austin/2026-09-14/austin-tx-federal-judge-blocks-trump-mail-in-ballot-rules-travis-county-clerk">KUT</a></span>

Travis County Clerk Dyana Limon-Mercado's office ran public logic and accuracy testing of its ballot equipment Monday, a check that ballots are programmed and counted correctly, ahead of the Nov. 3 election. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/travis-county-clerk-begins-logic-and-accuracy-testing-ahead-of-nov-3-elections/">KXAN</a></span>

Limon-Mercado said in the mail-ballot suit that the county had already spent more than $21,000 on ballot envelopes the new federal rules would have made noncompliant. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/austin/2026-09-14/austin-tx-federal-judge-blocks-trump-mail-in-ballot-rules-travis-county-clerk">KUT</a></span>

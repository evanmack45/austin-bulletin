### Travis County approved 22 psychiatric beds. A median home pays $36 a year.

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-16/mental-health-54m.png" alt="Bar chart of the five largest lines in Central Health&apos;s 54 million dollars of new mental health investments for fiscal 2026-27: 33.1 million dollars for 22 inpatient psychiatric beds and 10 psychiatric emergency department bays, 16 million dollars to plan and launch a mental health crisis care center including 3 million for a sobering center, 1.5 million for justice-involved health and community-based complex primary care, 1 million for an additional crisis care team and 1 million for long-acting injectable medications; two smaller lines, 1 million to expand medication-assisted treatment and 400 thousand dollars for psychiatric emergency services through Integral Care, are not shown">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Central Health, via Austin Current and Community Impact</figcaption>
</figure>

Commissioners approved Central Health's 6% tax rate increase Tuesday, and the hospital district's budget with it. For the
owner of a median-priced Travis County home, Austin Current reported before the vote, that comes to about $36 more a
year. Central Health takes roughly 6% of a property tax bill.

The district itemized what the money buys. Twenty-two inpatient psychiatric beds. Ten psychiatric emergency department
bays. A crisis care center with a sobering center inside it, and a crisis team of two psychiatrists, two care managers
and one counselor. The full commitment to crisis and recovery care is $127.5 million, of which $54 million is new.

County Judge Andy Brown said the money will let the county "divert and deflect people with more serious mental illness to
get the care that they need outside of the county jail." Matt Mackowiak, co-founder of Save Austin Now, told KXAN earlier
this month that the timing was wrong: "If we continue on this path, Austin's gonna become a playground that only rich
people can afford to live in."

At the Capitol the same morning, the House Ways and Means Committee took up the opposite question. Texas school districts
collect about $29 billion a year in maintenance and operations taxes, and roughly $8.6 billion of that comes from
homesteads, the slice Gov. Greg Abbott wants to stop collecting altogether.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The city will ask the Legislature for crisis-center money in the 2027 session; the first of five council candidate panels is Thursday at 6:30 p.m. at the South Austin Senior Activity Center, and the voter registration deadline is Oct. 5 (<a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026</a>).</p>

#### Money & bills

##### Commissioners give final approval to a $1.015 billion Central Health budget
Travis County Commissioners Court signed off Tuesday on the hospital district's budget for fiscal 2026-27, which its
board of managers had adopted Sept. 9. Chief executive Dr. Pat Lee said untreated mental health needs among the county's

##### SoftBank's energy arm plans a data center on the old 3M campus

Blvd. into what it calls the HighPoint 2222 Campus: 1.1 million square feet of office and lab space on 156 acres, built

<p class="good-thing">Eighty Austin police officers spent part of Tuesday getting fitted for running shoes. The Central Texas Public Safety Commission is starting something called Miles for Mental Health in October: one mile a day, every day, for thirty days, done as a group rather than alone. Fleet Feet Austin handled the shoes. The American Heart Association ran health screenings alongside. Ashley Copeland, the commission's chief operating officer, said officers carry heavy physical and mental demands every day, and that supporting their wellness is part of supporting the people who serve. There is something plain and likable about the shape of it. A mile is not far. Thirty days is not long. The whole point is that nobody has to do it by themselves.

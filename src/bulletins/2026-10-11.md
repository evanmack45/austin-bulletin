### Austin starts writing rules for its biggest water users

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-11/large-water-users.png" alt="Timeline of Austin Water's proposed rules for high-impact water users: in July 2026 the City Council passed a resolution on large water users; on October 5 Austin Water held an open house proposing rules for customers using more than 85 million gallons a year; recommendations are due to the Council's Climate, Water, Environment and Parks Committee by December 9; the first report on large users' water consumption is due by May 2027.">
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin &middot; Austin Chronicle, from Austin Water</figcaption>
</figure>

Austin Water has sketched what it would ask of the city's heaviest commercial customers, and residents got their first look at an open house on October 5, the Austin Chronicle reported.

The draft would cover any commercial or industrial site that uses, or is projected to use, more than 85 million gallons a year. Those customers would file conservation plans, annual reports and water budgets. Go over the budget and the rate goes up. The utility is also weighing plan updates every five years, with five- and 10-year goals.

The numbers frame the stakes. In 2025, high-impact commercial customers used 10.2% of Austin's water and residential customers 60.1%, according to Austin Water figures presented that night.

Data centers were a focus of residents' concerns. Director Shay Ralls Roalson said the proposals are "about high-impact water users, regardless of what kind of development it is." Northwest Austin resident Carol Phillips asked how the City can plan "without knowing every drop you're using," including water used at the power plants that supply a data center.

Roalson said the utility's practices ensure costs "are borne equitably by each customer class." A 2023 ordinance requires developers to pay for the utility infrastructure they need.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> City Manager T.C. Broadnax must bring recommendations to the Council's Climate, Water, Environment and Parks Committee by December 9.</p>

#### Public safety & courts

##### Man shot dead in Menchaca Road dispute over rent
Miguel Guevara was shot and killed Tuesday night in the 11300 block of Menchaca Road, the Austin Police Department (APD) said. Police said his roommate, Christopher Foy, 24, shot him during an argument over rent money, shortly after pointing a gun at his girlfriend in a separate dispute. Officers found Foy hours later on Nuckols Crossing and arrested him for murder. It is Austin's 46th homicide of 2026. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/police/news/homicide-investigation-11300-block-menchaca-road">Austin Police Department</a></span>

##### Murder charge in a fentanyl death at a construction site
APD arrested Jose Antonio Martinez, 54, on October 5 on a first-degree murder warrant in the June death of Luis Gerardo Cancel-Vargas. He was found unresponsive in a portable toilet at a construction site at 101 Brushy Street. An autopsy found fentanyl and other drugs; police said Martinez provided them. It is Austin's 44th homicide this year. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/police/news/homicide-investigation-101-brushy-street">Austin Police Department</a></span>

##### Parts of Zilker's Great Lawn to stay closed after ACL
Once the Austin City Limits (ACL) Festival's second weekend ends and crews finish load-out, portions of Zilker Park's Great Lawn will stay closed so the soil, turf and trees can recover, the City says. Sections reopen as work allows. The City says organizers have committed to restore the lawn to its previous condition or better, "at no cost to taxpayers." <span class="src"><a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/parks/news/austin-parks-and-recreation-prepares-zilker-park-acl-fest-weekend-two">City of Austin</a></span>

Council Member Ryan Alter told CBS Austin that organizer C3 repairs the lawn each year under its contract, but he wants to be sure the festival is not causing deeper damage. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/dryer-acl-weekend-eases-mud-but-zilker-park-grass-damage-needs-long-term-fix">CBS Austin</a></span>

##### Council makes Doris Miller Auditorium a historic landmark
The City Council voted October 8 to designate the Doris Miller Auditorium at Rosewood Neighborhood Park, 2300 Rosewood Ave., a City historic landmark. The building opened in 1944 as a recreation hall for Black service members and was named for the Navy cook honored for heroism at Pearl Harbor. Nat King Cole, Louis Armstrong and Count Basie played there. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/parks/news/doris-miller-auditorium-designated-city-austin-historic-landmark-austin-city-council">City of Austin</a></span>

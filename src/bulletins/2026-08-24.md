## The Big Story

{% bigstory %}
### Texas reopens the plans behind its power and water, in the hottest stretch of the year

![Timeline of the Texas 765-kilovolt transmission line buildout, from Winter Storm Uri in 2021 to pending Public Utility Commission decisions in 2026](/images/2026-08-24/transmission-timeline.png)
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · The Texas Tribune</figcaption>

Austin wakes up under an Extreme Heat Warning with ERCOT forecasting a peak
demand of about 91,600 megawatts this afternoon against roughly 111,400
available. Behind that arithmetic sit two long-range state plans — one for power
lines, one for water — and both are being reopened at once.

Start with the lines. After the Legislature signed off in 2023 on a large
expansion of transmission capacity, the Public Utility Commission approved a
plan to build 765-kilovolt lines across Texas, a type larger than any previously
built in the state. Residents in the rural counties the first three West Texas
routes would cross have objected that the lines eat up their land, and they
commonly point to House Bill 5066, passed in 2023, which cut the PUC's window to
consider an application from 365 days to 180, the Texas Tribune reported.
Following a House Committee on State Affairs hearing Wednesday, a bipartisan
group of 30 representatives called on the PUC to deny the applications, which
would effectively end the project in its current form. Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick is
among those who have called on the commission to reject the lines outright. On
Thursday, the State Office of Administrative Hearings recommended the PUC reject
a portion of the West Texas buildout — the first time it had done so for this
project.

The grid operator and the industries that use the grid say a pause carries its
own bill. ERCOT has said West Texas is at risk for rolling blackouts if the
lines are not built, and its chief operating officer testified in July that
rolling blackouts are possible in the Permian Basin by next summer. The PUC's
independent market monitor, Potomac Economics, found average demand in the West
Zone grew 12.3 percent in 2025 against 6.1 percent statewide, with congestion
inside the zone up 27 percent. "The world's most important energy basin is
currently running on a patchwork of portable generators that just are not
equipped for long-term needs or production growth," said Todd Staples, president
of the Texas Oil and Gas Association. The utilities that would build the lines
now concede the landowners' central point: "The 180-day process for landowners
for these large, bulk-rate, long-length lines is not sufficient, and I think
that's an accurate and fair assessment," said Mark Bell, president and CEO of
the Association of Electric Companies of Texas.

Data centers are tangled in the same knot. Gov. Greg Abbott this month ordered
an audit of data centers seeking to connect to the grid, and ERCOT paused the
study it uses to approve large new loads. There are now nearly 2,000 requests in
ERCOT's queue, 90 percent of them data centers, representing 466,497 megawatts
of demand — more than five times the grid's peak demand record set July 22. "I
don't believe we have an option of doing nothing," PUC chair Thomas Gleeson told
lawmakers at a June Senate committee meeting. "We have to get electricity to
customers in the Permian."

The water plan is being reopened the same week. A report from the nonprofit
Texas 2036 says the state's water planning process, created nearly 30 years ago,
did not project the shortage that brought Corpus Christi within months of a
water emergency this year. Officials there were making hurried plans for
surcharges and water-use reductions that would have affected roughly 500,000
people and the region's energy industry before summer rain brought relief. The
report recommends stress testing plans against worse droughts and verifying the
data behind them. "The lessons learned from our report is not that the plans or
the planning process is bad, but it can certainly be perfected with better data
and better planning objectives to better identify what we need to do to build
the water infrastructure for a drought-prone and growing state," said Jeremy
Mazur, director of infrastructure and natural resources policy at Texas 2036.

Both plans were written against assumptions about how much power and water Texas
would need. Camp Mabry had recorded 21 triple-digit days this August through
Sunday, and Lake Travis is 97.1 percent full.

#### Roads & transit

CapMetro suspended Red Line rail service today through Thursday, Aug. 27, while crews finish track work, crossings, signals and safety testing for the future North Burnet/Uptown Station. A temporary shuttle, Route 545, connects the Leander, Lakeline and Howard stations to downtown during the closure; the station is expected to open in 2027. <span class="src">Statesman</span>

#### Public safety & courts

Austin firefighters confined Sunday night's fire at the Austin Country Flea Mart along East U.S. 290 to the building where it started. The first call came in about 6 p.m. and was upgraded to a second alarm; embers pushed downwind started a brush fire that involved a tire pile, producing the black plume visible from Interstate 35. Two people who were not firefighters were treated for minor injuries, several flea market structures collapsed, and the cause is under investigation. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

{% voice "bsky-3mtszfy4wwc2o" %}

Visitors to Austin's Central Library will see more police and security staff under an Austin Police Department "Library Initiative" launched in June. APD said drug-offense reports at the library have more than doubled since June and trespassing reports have nearly tripled, partly because officers are working with staff to issue and enforce criminal trespass notices; officers conducted 63 directed patrols there in July, up from 48 in June. The Austin Public Library said it has also added security staffing, upgraded incident-reporting technology and arranged on-site personnel through the Travis County Sheriff's Office. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

{% voice "bsky-3mtsdmfy6n62s" %}

The Cedar Park Fire Department said it responded to a car fire Sunday afternoon, posting about the call around 2:45 p.m. and asking residents to keep their distance from crews at emergency scenes. <span class="src">CBS Austin</span>

#### City Hall & county

Community Impact published candidate questionnaires Monday for four races on Central Texas ballots in November: Texas House District 49, which runs through parts of South, Central and North Austin, where Arshia Papari and Montserrat Garibay are running; State Senate District 5; U.S. House District 35; and Williamson County clerk. Candidates answered in up to 50 words per question. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

#### Schools

The University of Texas began fall classes Monday after receiving more than 100,000 undergraduate applications for this fall's class, up from last year's record of nearly 91,000 freshman applications. UT estimates an in-state undergraduate will spend about $32,500 to $35,500 this school year including tuition, housing, food, transportation and books, and says nearly 150 new faculty members are joining this year. A proposed new core curriculum would raise the number of requirements to 12 from 10 while cutting total core credits to 36 from 42, with new classes possible in fall 2027. <span class="src">CBS Austin</span>

Austin Community College also started its fall semester Monday; the 16-week and first eight-week sessions both run from today, according to the college's academic calendar. <span class="src">ACC, CBS Austin</span>

#### Health

Jonestown was placed under a boil water notice Sunday after low water pressure and outages. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality required the Jonestown Water Supply Corporation to tell customers to boil water at a rolling boil for two minutes before drinking, cooking, making ice, brushing teeth or washing hands and face, or to buy bottled water; the system will notify customers when the notice is lifted. <span class="src">CBS Austin</span>

Fromm Family Foods is recalling 3,852 cases of Fromm Turkey Pâté wet canned dog food and 1,973 cases of Fromm Diner Classics Milo's Meatloaf Pâté over potential metal contamination, in a recall the Food and Drug Administration said was announced Friday. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

#### Business & tech

COTALAND, the theme park at Circuit of the Americas, will open Sept. 26 with more than 30 attractions, including Circuit Breaker, which the park says is the first tilt roller coaster in Texas, plus karting, mini golf and a zip line. "COTALAND was created to be far more than a traditional amusement park," COTA founder Bobby Epstein said in a news release. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

#### Around town

Austin Resource Recovery curbside customers may schedule up to three on-demand collections a calendar year for bulk items, brush and household hazardous waste, and items must be at the curb only on the scheduled collection day and out by 5:30 a.m. <span class="src">Statesman</span>

Make-A-Wish Central & South Texas has granted 58 wishes so far in its Summer of Wishes campaign, which aims to grant 100 wishes in 100 days for local children facing critical illnesses. <span class="src">CBS Austin</span>

#### Texas

Counties across Texas are facing public pushback over Flock Safety's license plate cameras, and officials say the contracts are hard to unwind because most networks are bought by retailers, homeowners associations and municipal utility districts rather than by the county. Austin and San Marcos have moved on from the cameras; Guadalupe and Hood counties have ended their contracts, as have Bandera, Lago Vista and Magnolia. Advocacy groups that track camera placements estimate more than 13,000 cameras in Texas. "Public safety and privacy are not mutually exclusive," Harris County Commissioner Rodney Ellis said. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

A Texas Tribune and ProPublica investigation published in May found Texas lawmakers missed dozens of chances over nearly 60 years to restrict building in flood-prone places; in July, intense rain hit the Kerr County area again, flooding some of the same spots and killing one person. Lawmakers did require youth camp cabins to be moved away from flood-prone riverside spots after last year's Guadalupe River flood, which killed at least 119 people in Kerr County, but did not change the rules for homes or RV parks. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

#### Sports

Central Texas high school football opens this week in dangerous heat, with afternoon temperatures of 103 to 106 degrees possible Monday through Wednesday and some model guidance nearer 107 to 109. The University Interscholastic League's heat-safety guidelines use wet bulb globe temperature, which accounts for sunshine, wind and surface temperature as well as air temperature. <span class="src">Statesman</span>

The Statesman's first Class 4A and small-schools poll of the season puts LBJ first, off a 9-3 season that ended in the area round, and Wimberley second after an 8-5 year and a regional semifinal run. <span class="src">Statesman</span>

Texas junior Colin Simmons enters the season as a preseason first-team All-American from Walter Camp and Phil Steele after leading the SEC with 12 sacks in 2025, adding a team-high 15.5 tackles for loss. He anchors a defensive front that also returns Hero Kanu, Maraad Watson and sophomore Lance Jackson under first-year defensive coordinator Will Muschamp. "I think Muschamp puts his best players in a position to go make a play," Simmons said. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

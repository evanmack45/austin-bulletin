### Austin's school district is two points from losing its board

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-19/aisd-two-points.png" alt="Bar chart showing how far two Austin Independent School District middle schools fell short of a D rating in the preliminary 2026 Texas Education Agency accountability ratings. Austin Current reported that Burnet Middle School fell one point short of a D, scoring 59, and that Webb Middle School fell two points short, scoring 58. A score of 60 is required for a D. Each campus has now received five consecutive failing ratings, which under state law requires the education commissioner either to close the campus or to replace Austin ISD&apos;s elected school board with an appointed board of managers. The district appealed both ratings; the deadline to appeal was September 11, 2026.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Texas Education Agency preliminary ratings, via Austin Current</figcaption>
</figure>

Austin Independent School District (AISD) has appealed the failing ratings of Burnet and Webb middle schools to the Texas
Education Agency (TEA), the agency confirmed Friday. The margin is the story. Burnet's preliminary score was 59, Webb's
58, against the 60 that earns a D.

Five straight unacceptable ratings at a single campus take the decision out of the district's hands. State law then
requires Education Commissioner Mike Morath either to close that school or to replace Austin ISD's elected board with
appointed managers. Dobie Middle School, which had four consecutive failures, earned a D this year and fell out of the
count. Burnet and Webb did not. A TEA spokesperson told Community Impact that Austin ISD is the only district in Texas
that may face intervention on the 2026 ratings.

Districts usually appeal on the data behind a rating, and the agency can change a grade if it finds an error. TEA would
not say when the district filed or on what grounds, because the process is open. The deadline was Sept. 11. Austin ISD
has told families it is adding coaching for teachers and more intensive academic help at both campuses, and that it will
not gather signatures for the parent petition that could close Burnet and Webb instead of costing the district its board.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> A state decision could come as early as January, Superintendent Matias Segura told the Austin ISD board at an Aug. 13 information session.</p>

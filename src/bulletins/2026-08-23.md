## Top stories

### Austin ties a daily heat record as the dry spell reaches 29 days

![Forecast highs for the next seven days, all above 100 degrees](/images/2026-08-23/heat-week-ahead.png)
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Data: National Weather Service</figcaption>

Austin reached 107 degrees on Saturday, tying a daily record set in 2024 and
sitting 10 degrees above the seasonal average of 97, FOX 7 Austin reported. An
Extreme Heat Warning remains in effect through Sunday evening, with temperatures
expected to hit 107 again and heat index values reaching up to 110. FOX 7 said
Austin has not recorded measurable rain in 29 days, and rain coverage stays
under 10% through Wednesday. A westward shift in the high-pressure ridge could
bring widely scattered showers Thursday and Friday.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The Extreme Heat Warning ends at 7 p.m. Sunday; a Heat Advisory follows through 7 p.m. Wednesday, and the first rain chance comes Thursday.</p>

### Body of missing swimmer recovered from Lake Travis

![Map of Lake Travis with a pin on Pace Bend Park; the swimmer's body was recovered across the lake from the park](/images/2026-08-23/lake-travis-map.png)
<figcaption>Map: Google · Pin: Pace Bend Park. The body was recovered across the lake from the park, per the sheriff's office</figcaption>

The Travis County Sheriff's Office said crews recovered the body of a man in his
20s from Lake Travis late Sunday morning. The sheriff's office said multiple
agencies responded to a 911 call about a missing swimmer in the Lago Vista area
around 5:47 p.m. Saturday, and that rescue efforts became a recovery mission. The
Lake Patrol searched into Saturday evening and resumed at 6 a.m. Sunday,
recovering the body at about 11:30 a.m. from a depth of 17 feet in an area of the
lake across from Pace Bend Park.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> No further announcements had been made by Sunday afternoon.</p>

### State judges recommend denying a 765-kV power line across Central Texas

![Map of Central Texas with markers on the county seats of Bell, Williamson, Burnet, Llano, and Mason counties](/images/2026-08-23/power-line-counties.png)
<figcaption>Map: Google · County seats of the counties named in the judges' recommendation</figcaption>

State administrative law judges have recommended that the Public Utility
Commission of Texas deny the Bell County East to Big Hill 765-kilovolt line, a
nearly 200-mile Oncor and LCRA project crossing Burnet, Williamson, Mason and
Llano counties, KVUE reported. The judges found the utilities missed some
rules, including notice to landowners, and did not show the line is needed.
Oncor said it strongly disagrees and that the evidence of need "has only
continued to grow." Thirty-one Texas House lawmakers also asked the commission
to deny it.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The recommendation does not bind the commission, which must rule by late September.</p>

<p class="source-line">Source: <a href="https://www.kvue.com/article/news/politics/texas-legislature/puc-proposed-765kv-transmission-line-judges-regulators-project-application/269-e275e4bd-9db7-49f1-90a1-6042fd54adfa">KVUE</a></p>

### Tens of thousands attend Austin Pride parade and festival

![A Pride parade crowd waving rainbow flags on a downtown street at dusk](/images/2026-08-23/pride-parade.jpg)
<figcaption>AI-generated image</figcaption>

Tens of thousands of people filled downtown Austin on Saturday night for the
36th annual Austin Pride celebration, held under this year's theme "Viva La
Resistencia," CBS Austin reported. Outreach Director Jamal Crier said the theme
reflects Pride's origins and its continued purpose. The parade finished at the
Ann Richards Bridge near Lady Bird Lake, and attendees at the Fiesta Gardens
festival told KXAN they used water and shade to get through one of the year's
hottest days. CBS Austin said the celebration came weeks after the rainbow
crosswalks at Colorado and Fourth streets were removed following a state order,
and that the city placed a permanent historical marker at the intersection on
Friday.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Austin Pride's 2026 events are over; the city's historical marker at Colorado and Fourth streets stays in place.</p>

### Judge denies Karmelo Anthony's request for a new trial

![Timeline of the Karmelo Anthony case from the April 2025 stabbing to the denied motion for a new trial](/images/2026-08-23/anthony-case-timeline.png)
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · Source: FOX 7 Austin</figcaption>

A judge on Saturday denied Karmelo Anthony's motion for a new trial in the fatal
stabbing of Frisco student Austin Metcalf, FOX 7 Austin reported. Anthony, 19,
was convicted of murder in June and sentenced to 35 years in prison. Judge
Michael Chitty, newly appointed to the matter after the judge who oversaw the
trial was removed from the appeal process, wrote that the court "has determined
that the motion should be denied." Anthony's attorneys argued his rights were
violated by undocumented conferences and by decisions limiting courtroom access,
while prosecutor Bill Wirskye testified that an agreement to keep certain
character evidence out of the trial protected both sides and that the state gave
up "a whole lot."

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The case continues in the appeal process, from which the original trial judge has been removed.</p>

<p class="source-line">Source: <a href="https://www.fox7austin.com/news/karmelo-anthony-new-trial-decision">FOX 7 Austin</a></p>

## Top stories

### Austin ties a daily heat record as the dry spell reaches 29 days

Austin reached 107 degrees on Saturday, tying a daily record set in 2024 and
sitting 10 degrees above the seasonal average of 97, FOX 7 Austin reported. An
Extreme Heat Warning remains in effect through Sunday evening, with temperatures
expected to hit 107 again and heat index values reaching up to 110. FOX 7 said
Austin has not recorded measurable rain in 29 days, and rain coverage stays
under 10% through Wednesday. A westward shift in the high-pressure ridge could
bring widely scattered showers Thursday and Friday.

### Body of missing swimmer recovered from Lake Travis

The Travis County Sheriff's Office said crews recovered the body of a man in his
20s from Lake Travis late Sunday morning. The sheriff's office said multiple
agencies responded to a 911 call about a missing swimmer in the Lago Vista area
around 5:47 p.m. Saturday, and that rescue efforts became a recovery mission. The
Lake Patrol searched into Saturday evening and resumed at 6 a.m. Sunday,
recovering the body at about 11:30 a.m. from a depth of 17 feet in an area of the
lake across from Pace Bend Park.

### State judges recommend denying a 765-kV power line across Central Texas

A panel of State Office of Administrative Hearings judges has recommended that
the Public Utility Commission of Texas deny approval for the Bell County East to
Big Hill 765-kilovolt transmission line, a nearly 200-mile Oncor and LCRA
Transmission Services Corporation project that would run from the Permian Basin
to Bell County and cross several Central Texas counties, including Burnet,
Williamson, Mason and Llano, finding that the utilities failed to comply with
some rules, including those requiring proper notice to landowners, and did not
provide enough evidence that the project is necessary, KVUE reported. Oncor said it strongly disagrees with several of the conclusions,
including those on need and notice: "these projects were identified through the
established transmission-planning process, by the grid experts charged with
maintaining reliability, and the evidence supporting their need has only
continued to grow," the company said in a statement, adding that it remains
"confident that the record supports moving these critical reliability projects
forward." Thirty-one Texas House lawmakers issued a joint statement Friday
asking the commission to deny the application and to let the Legislature reform
the process; on increased generation and transmission, they said, "We are
prepared to work toward solutions that yield both in the 90th legislative
session." The
recommendation does not bind the commission; PUC Chairman Thomas Gleeson said
the commission will review the testimony and evidence and decide at an upcoming
meeting, and a 180-day deadline gives it until late September to rule.

### Tens of thousands attend Austin Pride parade and festival

Tens of thousands of people filled downtown Austin on Saturday night for the
36th annual Austin Pride celebration, held under this year's theme "Viva La
Resistencia," CBS Austin reported. Outreach Director Jamal Crier said the theme
reflects Pride's origins and its continued purpose. The parade finished at the
Ann Richards Bridge near Lady Bird Lake, and attendees at the Fiesta Gardens
festival told KXAN they used water and shade to get through one of the year's
hottest days. CBS Austin said the celebration came weeks after the rainbow
crosswalks at Colorado and Fourth streets were removed following a state order,
and that the city placed a permanent historical marker at the intersection on
Friday.

### Judge denies Karmelo Anthony's request for a new trial

A judge on Saturday denied Karmelo Anthony's motion for a new trial in the fatal
stabbing of Frisco student Austin Metcalf, FOX 7 Austin reported. Anthony, 19,
was convicted of murder in June and sentenced to 35 years in prison. Judge
Michael Chitty, newly appointed to the matter after the judge who oversaw the
trial was removed from the appeal process, wrote that the court "has determined
that the motion should be denied." Anthony's attorneys argued his rights were
violated by undocumented conferences and by decisions limiting courtroom access,
while prosecutor Bill Wirskye testified that an agreement to keep certain
character evidence out of the trial protected both sides and that the state gave
up "a whole lot."

### Hutto Fire Rescue names the site for its sixth fire station

Williamson County Emergency Services District No. 3, also known as Hutto Fire
Rescue, announced Saturday where it plans to build Fire Station 6, KXAN
reported. According to the district's press release, the property is on the east
side of Hutto along County Road 132, near the intersection of County Road 134.
Planning for a station in that area started in 2019, and district commissioners
approved the purchase of the property in April 2026. The release said the
district serves an estimated population of more than 73,000 across a 63-square
mile jurisdiction, operates four fire stations plus a headquarters and warehouse,
and has secured land for two more future stations.

### An Austin candle maker's products are headed to H-E-B shelves

KXAN reported that Akriti Marwaha left her full-time job as a software engineer
to spend more time on her handmade candle business, and that her products are
about to reach H-E-B shelves. Marwaha said she started making candles after
someone in her family was diagnosed with cancer and she wanted fewer toxins in
her household. She said she worked through different waxes, fragrances and oils
until she settled on a formula using coconut and apricot wax.

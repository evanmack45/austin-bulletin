today, a hundred tomorrow, ninety-nine on Monday. Thursday's storms did leave a mark south of here: Luling woke
up to split trees and a church missing its roof. Closer in, the weekend has a soft spot for the Queen of Country.

## The Big Story

### Prices are falling in Austin. The bills are not.

![Bar chart of Austin ISD's projected deficit for the 2025-26 school year as the district revised it: $19 million in the budget trustees adopted in June 2025, $49 million by February 2026, $95 million in June 2026, and $105 million in the unaudited year-end figure presented to trustees on Aug. 27, 2026](/images/2026-08-29/aisd-deficit.png)
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Austin ISD figures, via Austin Current</figcaption>

Austin home prices fell again in July, down 1.4 percent from a year ago, in a slide the Home Buying Institute
says has been running for four years. Rents have come down from their pandemic peak too. Almost none of that has
reached the households and institutions that spent the week doing the math.

Eviction filings across Austin and Travis County are at record highs and still climbing. Building and
Strengthening Tenant Action, or BASTA, counted 10,732 filings as of late August, about 900 more than at the same
point last year. Housing experts told Austin Current the fall in rents has mostly benefited people near the
region's median income, while lower-income renters are still squeezed by the cost of everything else.

"People are still, unfortunately, at times in Austin living paycheck to paycheck," said Council Member Zo Qadri,
who filed a resolution the council approved Thursday directing the city manager to audit Austin's
eviction-prevention programs and find the gaps. Perla Aguirre, a community education specialist at BASTA,
described a client who chose car repairs over rent because the car was how she got to work, and another who paid
rent and sat in the dark. "A lot of tough decisions are being made," Aguirre said.

The money for that work is going the other way. Aside from $2.25 million for rental assistance, Qadri said the
finalized fiscal 2027 budget left out longstanding anti-displacement services the city had funded before.
Rosamaria Murillo, CEO of El Buen Samaritano, whose "I Belong in Austin" program has distributed more than $16
million to over 3,100 households since 2021, said the city cut her group's $4 million contract by 25 percent in
fiscal 2026.

The city is doing its own arithmetic. Council Member Marc Duchen, who represents District 10, said falling values
shrink the tax base at the same time costs rise. The budget the council passed two weeks ago raises property
taxes at the state-mandated maximum of 3.5 percent and lifts fees, which CBS Austin reported comes to more than
$350 a household next year. Asked whether a cheaper housing market means Austin is no longer unaffordable,
Duchen said no. "Part of the reason that housing costs are going down is that they've been astronomically high in
a bubble for 5, 6 years," he said. The parks department is carrying about $700 million in overdue repairs, enough
that a shade structure over the swings at Ramsey Neighborhood Park took the parks foundation, a neighborhood
association, a council office and the city between them.

The sharpest number belongs to the schools. Austin ISD closed the 2025-26 year $105 million in the red, an
unaudited figure Chief Financial Officer Katrina Montgomery gave trustees Thursday night. Trustees adopted that
budget expecting a $19 million shortfall. It was $49 million by February and $95 million in June. Even after
$205 million in cuts that reached classrooms, bus routes and hundreds of jobs, the district expects to borrow
$140 million this fall to make payroll while it waits on property tax revenue, and needs to find $10 million more.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Austin ISD expects to borrow the $140 million in October, the city manager's eviction-intervention recommendations are due to council early next year, and a parks bond goes to voters Nov. 3.</p>

#### Roads & transit

TxDOT will open one new main lane in each direction of Loop 360 on Sunday morning, letting drivers bypass the signals at Westlake Drive and Cedar Street. Connector roads still reach both streets, and the Westlake Drive bridge handles non-signal U-turns to northbound and southbound Loop 360. The wider project, which replaced the two signals with bridges, is expected to finish in late 2026. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Members of the Texas House Transportation Committee pressed TxDOT at an Aug. 17 hearing to communicate better about construction and closures. Executive Director Marc Williams said the agency manages more than 200,000 miles of state highway and that about 72 percent of all vehicle travel in Texas happens on them. The agency's website lists more than 11,000 projects underway. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

A school bus and two other vehicles collided just before 9 a.m. Friday at West Parmer Lane and Corpus Christi Drive in northwest Austin, and one vehicle rolled over. The Travis County Sheriff's Office reported no injuries and said two inside lanes were blocked. KXAN said it was working to confirm which district the bus belonged to. <span class="src">KXAN / FOX 7 Austin</span>

Austin police are looking for the driver in a hit-and-run that injured a person riding an e-bike about 10 p.m. Aug. 22 in the 8400 block of Johnny Morris Road in east Austin. The driver left without stopping to render aid. The rider was taken to a hospital with injuries that were not life-threatening. Tips go to Capital Area Crime Stoppers at 512-472-8477. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

#### Public safety & courts

Gov. Greg Abbott ordered state agencies Thursday night to stop funding Flock license plate cameras. "To the extent any funding comes from Texas agencies, those agencies are clarifying that those funds cannot be used for Flock cameras," spokesperson Andrew Mahaleris said. Abbott described a crackdown underway as cities cancel contracts, and pointed to a Lufkin officer facing 100 counts of misusing public information over surveillance of 11 people. <span class="src">KXAN / The Texas Tribune / CBS Austin</span>

The order came as The Texas Tribune was preparing an investigation into where that money originated. A 2023 law, passed unanimously to fight catalytic converter theft, added $1 to every Texas auto insurance policy. The Motor Vehicle Crime Prevention Authority, whose board is mostly appointed by Abbott, has since put at least $30 million of it into roughly 3,200 Flock cameras, including $15.9 million for nearly 1,200 at DPS. Lawmakers said Flock was never discussed. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

The federal Occupational Safety and Health Administration is investigating Thursday's fatal house collapse in south Austin. Travis County ESD 5 said four men were working inside the house at 12002 Salvador St. when the entire structure came down, including the second floor; two got themselves out and two were trapped. Crews stabilized and lifted the house to free the survivor. <span class="src">CBS Austin / FOX 7 Austin</span>

A suspect was taken into custody Friday after an hours-long police operation in the 1700 block of Mustang Drive in Marble Falls. Marble Falls ISD placed Colt Elementary and Marble Falls High School in Secure status through the morning, keeping students inside with exterior doors locked while classes continued. The district said there was no threat to either campus and lifted the status by 10:30 a.m. <span class="src">CBS Austin / KXAN</span>

Travis County District Judge Maya Guerra Gamble said Friday she needs time to decide whether to block the state health department's method for testing THC in hemp products. Hemp businesses argue the agency's undefined use of "trace amount" amounts to a rule made without rulemaking. Jim Rich, for the attorney general's office, said testing falls under existing law on adulterated food and that there is no irreparable harm. A temporary injunction hearing is set for September. <span class="src">KXAN / CBS Austin</span>

The Department of Public Safety named Jason Bobo chief of the Texas Rangers Division, confirmed at Thursday's Public Safety Commission meeting. Bobo joined DPS as a highway patrol trooper in 1999 and the Rangers in 2010. He replaces Chief Scotty Shiver, who is retiring after 31 years. Ranger Major Kip Westmoreland was promoted to assistant chief. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

A 59-year-old woman from East Texas was found dead on a trail on the west side of Enchanted Rock State Natural Area on Thursday afternoon, after park staff were notified at 3 p.m. The Texas Parks and Wildlife Department said the investigation is ongoing and her cause of death is not known. It is the second reported visitor death at the park this year. <span class="src">KXAN / CBS Austin</span>

#### City Hall & county

Austin's park system carries about $700 million in overdue repairs, and neighborhood projects increasingly depend on money the city did not put up. At Ramsey Neighborhood Park in Rosedale, the Austin Parks Foundation covered project management and about $100,000 toward new shade over the swings, play equipment and safety surfacing, alongside a neighborhood association, a council office and the parks department. A parks bond goes to voters in November, the first in eight years. <span class="src">CBS Austin / Community Impact</span>

Round Rock City Council took the first of two required votes on its 2026-27 budget on Aug. 27, approving a tax rate of $0.423 per $100 of valuation and a $684.1 million operating budget. Chief Financial Officer Kevin Klosterboer said the city's median home value fell by a little over 6 percent. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

Bastrop Mayor Ishmael Harris and City Manager Sylvia Carrillo-Trevino defended the city's two-day retreat in Fredericksburg before the Texas House Select Committee on Governmental Oversight on Aug. 26. Council discussed the proposed budget and tax rate, Flock cameras and data centers there. State Rep. Stan Gerdes, R-Smithville, whose district includes Bastrop, said he plans to file legislation aimed at preventing out-of-town meetings like it. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

#### Business & tech

Penske Media Corporation will become the full owner of South by Southwest. Nick Barbaro, who co-founded the festival in 1987 with Louis Black, Roland Swenson and Louis Meyers, said he and the 29 other shareholders in SXSW Holdings LLC are selling their stake and winding up the firm. Penske became a partner in 2021 after the cancelled 2020 event; that deal carried a five-year option. "Basically they had operating control," Barbaro said. <span class="src">The Austin Chronicle</span>

Austin video game workers are pressing Microsoft for a first union contract three years into bargaining. Anne Barrett, a Bethesda Game Studios programmer laid off in July, said the studio unionized with the Communications Workers of America in 2024 partly out of fear of layoffs. Xbox CEO Asha Sharma announced a 3,200-person reduction on July 6. Local 6215 vice president Alex Doblado said guardrails on AI are a pillar of talks. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Levitation moved three days of shows from Radio East to The Far Out Lounge, which becomes the festival's central hub, after the final permit for the planned Radio East layout was not approved. Lineups and schedules for those shows are unchanged. American Football separately cancelled all three of its Texas dates, including Sept. 10 at Stubb's, and is issuing refunds. The festival runs Sept. 10-13. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

The Defense Department picked Fort Hood as one of five Army installations where contractors will build and run nuclear microreactors under the Army's Janus Program, with General Atomics Electromagnetic Systems as the vendor there. The Army is awarding up to $2.2 billion across five companies for fiscal years 2027 through 2031 and says it eventually wants more than 20 microreactors. The first is targeted to start operating by September 2028. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

Scheels opens Saturday in Cedar Park as the first piece of the 117-acre CedarView development. The store has a 65-foot indoor Ferris wheel, a 16,000-gallon saltwater aquarium holding 600 fish, a taxidermy display, a cafe and a candy store. City leaders have said sales tax from CedarView will help Cedar Park hold its services steady against rising costs. <span class="src">KXAN / Community Impact</span>

#### Texas

The Public Utility Commission of Texas voted unanimously Friday to approve two 765-kilovolt transmission lines running southwest from Bosque County, more than 400 miles across at least 30 counties, over objections from landowners and calls from the Texas Senate and some House members to wait for the Legislature. Commissioners tabled a decision on a third line. "They're about your land," Commissioner Courtney Hjaltman told landowners. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune / KXAN</span>

Six weeks after July's floods in southwest Texas, most of the federal disaster assistance the state requested for individuals and local governments has not been approved. Uvalde County has dozens of roads and several bridges awaiting permanent repairs. "That's what we're looking for: help," Acting Uvalde County Judge Jerry W. Bates said. Kerr County, flooded twice in two years, cannot cover temporary housing and vehicle replacement on its own. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

Harris County District Attorney Sean Teare said Friday he will take evidence in the July 7 fatal shooting of Lorenzo Salgado Araujo by immigration officers to a grand jury within weeks, partly to compel the release of details federal officials have not provided. He said his office knows the four ICE agents' names but will not release them, and may sue the Department of Homeland Security. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

A bipartisan pair of Texas lawmakers objected to the Railroad Commission ending general public testimony at its monthly meetings unless a sitting commissioner asks for it. The commission said it remains committed to meaningful public participation and pointed to new listening sessions, which elected commissioners are not required to attend. The Tribune found it is the first known state regulatory agency to reverse such a policy. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

Several Texas laws take effect Sept. 1. Solar panel sellers and their employees must register with the state, carry insurance and supervise salespeople under SB 1036, which also bans deceptive claims and unlicensed installation. Makers of some electronics must give owners and independent shops the same parts, tools and manuals authorized shops get, with medical devices, game consoles, appliances and vehicles excluded. Qualifying gold and silver become legal tender that nobody is required to accept. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

About 1.2 million Texas voters are on the state's suspense list with early voting less than two months away. Alicia Pierce of the secretary of state's office said the figure is not unusual against roughly 19 million registered voters, and that it usually means a registration card came back undeliverable. Voters who stay on the list through two full election cycles are removed from the rolls. Registration status is at VoteTexas.gov. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

Work on the border barrier project in Big Bend is paused until at least Sept. 15 after a hearing in San Antonio on Friday, state Sen. Roland Gutierrez said, with both sides agreeing to the stoppage. The Customs and Border Protection chief had already paused the work for an on-the-ground review. CBP says the project is an access road, road improvements, detection technology and vehicle barriers, not a 30-foot wall. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

U.S. District Judge David Hittner struck down Senate Bill 12, the 2023 law restricting "sexually oriented performances" in public or in front of minors, four months after an appeals court let it take effect. The ruling stops Attorney General Ken Paxton from levying civil fines of up to $10,000 on venues. The UT System's own 2025 ban on drag shows at its campuses is unaffected. <span class="src">The Daily Texan</span>

Kelly Lee Brower, 68, was arrested Aug. 19 in Cottonwood, Arizona, and is awaiting extradition to Texas on a capital murder charge in the 1981 killing of Yolanda Herrera, 23, whose body was found in a Bexar County field that April. Investigators said she had been sexually assaulted and beaten. Modern forensic testing identified Brower after the case went unsolved for more than 45 years. <span class="src">CBS Austin / FOX 7 Austin</span>

The Great Austin Shake Off was dreamed up by Stephanie Crugnola, artistic director of Walking Shadow Shakespeare Project, and her husband Mike, who is also with the company; a performer from The Baron's Men supplied the name. They had briefly considered a WWE-style showdown before landing somewhere gentler. "This is essentially a monologue competition inspired by the wholesome, friendly nature of the Great British Bake Off," Crugnola said. The Baron's Men, Austin Shakespeare and Walking Shadow each sent a performer out for a monologue in every round, and the audience bought votes and spent them on whoever moved them most. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/life-arts/2026-08-28/austin-tx-shakespeare-companies-competition-great-shake-off">KUT</a></span></p>

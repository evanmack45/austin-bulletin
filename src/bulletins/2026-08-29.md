### Prices are falling in Austin. The bills are not.

![Bar chart of the Austin Independent School District (Austin ISD) projected deficit for the 2025-26 school year as the district revised it: $19 million in the budget trustees adopted in June 2025, $49 million by February 2026, $95 million in June 2026, and $105 million in the unaudited year-end figure presented to trustees on Aug. 27, 2026](/images/2026-08-29/aisd-deficit.png)
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Austin ISD figures, via Austin Current</figcaption>

Austin home prices fell again in July, down 1.4 percent from a year ago, and rents are off their pandemic peak. Almost none of that relief has reached the bills. Eviction filings across Austin and Travis County are at record highs — 10,732 by the count of Building and Strengthening Tenant Action, a tenant group, about 900 ahead of last year — and housing experts say the falling rents have mostly reached people near the median income, not the renters furthest behind.

The budget the council passed this month runs the other way too: property taxes rise the state-maximum 3.5 percent and fees climb, together more than $350 a household next year. The sharpest number belongs to the schools — Austin ISD closed its year $105 million in the red, a shortfall that grew five times over against the district's own projections, and expects to borrow $140 million this fall to make payroll. Falling prices, rising bills: the week's arithmetic, done by renters, the council and the school district alike.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Austin ISD expects to borrow the $140 million in October, the city manager's eviction-intervention recommendations are due to council early next year, and a parks bond goes to voters Nov. 3.</p>

##### Loop 360 gets its first signal-free stretch Sunday
The Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) will open one new main lane in each direction of Loop 360 on Sunday morning, letting drivers bypass the signals at Westlake Drive and Cedar Street. ##### Abbott cuts off state money for Flock cameras
Gov. Greg Abbott ordered state agencies Thursday night to stop funding Flock Safety license plate cameras, with spokesperson Andrew Mahaleris saying state funds "cannot be used" for them. ##### State approves 400 miles of new high-voltage lines
The Public Utility Commission of Texas voted unanimously Friday to approve two 765-kilovolt transmission lines running southwest from Bosque County across at least 30 counties, over objections from landowners and calls from some legislators to wait. ##### New Texas laws arrive Tuesday
Several Texas laws take effect Sept. 1. Solar panel sellers must register with the state, carry insurance and supervise salespeople under Senate Bill 1036, which also bans deceptive claims. Makers of some electronics must give owners and independent shops the same parts, tools and manuals authorized shops get. Qualifying gold and silver become legal tender that nobody is required to accept. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

### Two years and $51 million into the childcare tax

![Timeline of the Travis County childcare tax: voters approved a property tax increase in November 2024 raising about $75 million a year; a 2025 United Way for Greater Austin survey found turnover above 40 percent, with 418 early childhood teachers leaving in a year; by July 2026 about 150 providers were receiving monthly gap payments totaling $4.2 million; and by August 2026 more than $51 million had been allocated, covering 7,500 children in after-school and summer programs and 1,000 new scholarships](/images/2026-08-31/childcare-tax.png)
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · Austin Current</figcaption>

Travis County voters raised their own property taxes in November 2024 to pay for childcare and after-school programs.
About $75 million a year. Austin Current reported Monday on what that money has done: more than $51 million
allocated, 7,500 children in after-school and summer programs, 1,000 new early childhood scholarships, monthly
payments to about 150 providers.

The system underneath is still bending. Childcare costs more in Travis County than anywhere else in Texas, and a
2025 United Way for Greater Austin survey found providers could not fill classrooms or keep teachers. "Raising Travis
County has stopped the bleeding," said Krista Anderson-Hill of Open Door Preschools, where the county money paid for
a 2 percent raise this year. Workforce Solutions Capital Area expects federal cuts to take 530 subsidies out of the
county's supply by 2027.

The next asks are already scheduled. Central Health, the Travis County Healthcare District, has proposed a 2026 tax
rate higher than the rate that would raise the same revenue from the same properties as last year, and it takes
public comment this week. A $260 million parks bond and the Austin school board races go to voters Nov. 3.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Central Health's board holds its public hearing and votes on the rate Wednesday at 5:30 p.m. at 1111 E. Cesar Chavez St.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### The University of Texas System adds $65 million to its tuition promise
The University of Texas System will put another $65 million into Promise Plus, which covers full tuition for Texas
undergraduates whose families earn up to $100,000 and part of tuition for families up to $125,000. Board chairman
Kevin Eltife said the money holds the current income limits rather than lifting them, and that raising them is the
goal. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/08/30/board-of-regents-expands-system-budget-allocates-more-funding-to-promise-plus/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

##### A padel club will take the convention center garage roof
The city is finalizing a lease with San Antonio's Slice Padel Co. for the 42,000-square-foot rooftop of the Austin
Convention Center parking garage, about 118 spaces that see little use during the center's rebuild. Slice will spend
$500,000 on six courts, a clubhouse and a cafe and bar, and pay the city $3,000 a month at first. Council approved
the lease plan Aug. 27. <span class="src"><a href="https://communityimpact.com/central-austin/government/rooftop-padel-club-planned-at-downtown-austin-convention-center-garage/">Community Impact</a></span>

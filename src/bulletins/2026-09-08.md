### Austin's power bill is now a campaign promise

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-08/austin-energy-fight.png" alt="Timeline of the fight over city-owned electric utilities in Texas: in 2002 the state deregulated its power market and Austin and San Antonio opted out; a February 2023 ice storm cut power to hundreds of thousands of Central Texas customers; in 2023 state Rep. Ellen Troxclair filed a bill to make public utilities offer retail shopping and it died in committee; in May 2026 a Texas Senate committee examined utility payments to city budgets; on Aug. 3, 2026 Gov. Greg Abbott proposed legislation ending city utility monopolies; and on Sept. 8, 2026 Austin and San Antonio said the change would raise rates">
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · The Texas Tribune</figcaption>
</figure>

Gov. Greg Abbott wants Austin to stop owning its own electric company. His campaign proposed legislation on Aug. 3 to
end the monopolies that Austin Energy and San Antonio's CPS Energy hold, promising 10% off the average bill and
describing city-owned utilities as cartels that use ratepayers as a piggy bank. The cities disagree. Austin Energy
transferred about $125 million to the city in 2025, money that pays for libraries, parks and first responders, and
Mayor Kirk Watson said converting the utility would take years and cost over a billion dollars that customers would
pay.

The argument lands in a month when nothing that burns or plugs in is getting cheaper. Regular unleaded in the
Austin-San Marcos area is running about 98 cents a gallon above last year as conflict involving Iran disrupts shipping
through the Strait of Hormuz. The statewide average residential electricity rate has gone from 11.50 cents a
kilowatt-hour in 2020 to 16.11 cents this year.

Austinites trying to bring that bill down themselves now have a state referee. Provisions of the Residential Solar
Retailer Regulatory Act took effect Sept. 1, requiring solar retailers and salespeople to register with the state.
Texans have filed more than 3,000 complaints about solar companies with the state attorney general's office since 2020.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Abbott's plan is a campaign proposal that the Legislature would have to enact. Texas votes Nov. 3, and the last day to register is Oct. 5.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### The governor wants Austin out of the power business
Gov. Greg Abbott's campaign has proposed legislation ending the monopolies held by Austin Energy and San Antonio's
CPS Energy, saying private competition would cut the average bill by 10%. Mayor Kirk Watson said deregulating the
utility would take years and cost customers over a billion dollars. There are 72 city-owned utilities in Texas serving
5.1 million people. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/08/texas-austin-energy-san-antonio-cps-abbott/">The Texas Tribune</a></span>

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-08/texas-power-rate.png" alt="Bar chart of the average residential electricity rate in Texas: 11.50 cents per kilowatt-hour in 2020 and 16.11 cents per kilowatt-hour in 2026">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · ElectricChoice, via The Texas Tribune</figcaption>
</figure>

Rooftop solar retailers and their salespeople must now register with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR), which has signed up 18 retailers and 500 salespeople so far. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/investigations/make-it-right-new-texas-law-regulates-solar-retailers-and-salespeople/">KXAN</a></span>

The same law gives buyers five days to cancel a solar purchase or lease without penalty, and sets fines of $50,000 to $100,000, higher when the customer is 65 or older. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/investigations/make-it-right-new-texas-law-regulates-solar-retailers-and-salespeople/">KXAN</a></span>

Diesel is above $5 a gallon, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported, and jet fuel has nearly doubled in a year as conflict involving Iran squeezes shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/iran-conflict-squeezes-strait-of-hormuz-shipping-raising-energy-costs-in-austin">CBS Austin</a></span>

Beef prices are forecast to rise nearly 10% this year, fruits and vegetables 5.9% and sugary snacks 7.1%; eggs are forecast to fall about 30%. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/iran-conflict-squeezes-strait-of-hormuz-shipping-raising-energy-costs-in-austin">CBS Austin</a></span>

#### Public safety & courts

##### Austin dropped Flock. The cameras never left town
The Austin Police Department (APD) has no Flock Safety contract and no access to the company's searchable database,
but private Flock cameras run all over the city: at Barton Creek Square and the Domain, at Home Depot and Academy, at
homeowners' associations. The Texas Department of Public Safety runs cameras on state roadways in Austin, shares that
data with more than 200 Texas agencies, and passes some to APD case by case. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/crime-justice/2026-09-08/austin-texas-area-flock-cameras-dps-round-rock">KUT</a></span>

Gov. Greg Abbott paused state funding for new Flock cameras late last month and told Glenn Beck the cancellations amount to a crackdown. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/crime-justice/2026-09-08/austin-texas-area-flock-cameras-dps-round-rock">KUT</a></span>

State District Judge Maya Guerra Gamble in Austin denied four hemp companies' request to halt enforcement of Texas rules on delta-8 products while their lawsuit proceeds. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/business/article/texas-hemp-enforcement-lawsuit-ruling-delta-8-22420232.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) opened an audit query Sept. 3 into Tesla's certification that its Cybercab meets federal safety standards, days after the taxis began carrying passengers here. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/how-could-tesla-cybercabs-impact-austin-first-responders-protocols">CBS Austin</a></span>

#### Schools

##### A state takeover would reach past Austin's classrooms
Mayor Kirk Watson said he wants to meet Texas Education Commissioner Mike Morath about the Austin Independent School
District (Austin ISD), which faces state intervention if preliminary failing ratings for two middle schools become
final. Austin ISD recently rose to a B overall. Rachel White, a University of Texas at Austin education policy
professor, said accountability ratings can affect property values and whether families stay. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/08/austin-austinisd-takeover-superintendent-mayor/">Austin Current</a></span>

Watson said one alternative to replacing the elected board would be closing Webb and reconfiguring Burnet, which is getting a $158 million rebuild under the 2022 bond program. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/08/austin-austinisd-takeover-superintendent-mayor/">Austin Current</a></span>

The State Board of Education approved social studies standards Friday that teach radical Islam as the motivation for the Sept. 11 attacks. Muslim advocacy groups said they will work to overturn them. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/education/2026-09-07/texas-state-board-of-education-approves-controversial-curriculum-that-muslims-call-discriminatory">KUT</a></span>

The U.S. Department of Education proposed a rule on Aug. 24 letting it cancel grants "for convenience" when they no longer match agency priorities, language that would reach university research money. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/09/07/department-of-education-proposes-rule-to-cancel-grants-for-convenience/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

The American Bar Association (ABA) council that accredits 198 law schools, the University of Texas at Austin's included, voted Aug. 21 to repeal diversity requirements. UT Law said it is unaffected. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/09/07/american-bar-association-votes-to-reduce-diversity-requirements/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

#### Business & street life

##### A vent hood fire closes Perla's, and its neighbors with it
Employees found flames in a kitchen vent hood at Perla's, 1400 S. Congress Ave., about 6:15 p.m. Sunday and got
everyone out. The Austin Fire Department sent 14 units and more than 41 firefighters, and reported no injuries. A sign
on the door says the restaurant is closed until further notice. Gelato Paradiso, attached to the same building, posted
its own sign: "Closed Due to Fire Above." <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/fire-at-perlas-on-south-congress-quickly-contained-no-injuries-reported/">KXAN</a></span>

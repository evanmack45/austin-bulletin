<p class="morning-note">Good morning, Austin. Rain — actual rain — is on the table this afternoon, and
after 32 days without a measurable drop at Camp Mabry it is the closest thing to news the sky
has offered all month. Take the umbrella anyway; the Heat Advisory runs to seven tonight and
we are still headed for 104. If the clouds clear off after dark, look up: a deep partial lunar
eclipse peaks a little after ten, and 96 percent of the moon slides into Earth's shadow. And
somewhere north of here, four hundred goats are eating Pflugerville's greenbelts on your behalf.</p>

### Three Texas immigration detention cases ended this week, two of them in federal court

![Timeline of three Texas immigration cases between Aug. 16 and Aug. 27, 2026: ICE detains Victor Martinez Nieto and his 5-year-old son at an Austin traffic stop on Aug. 16; a federal judge in San Antonio orders an Austin mother released from detention within two days on Aug. 24; ICE releases her and the father and son are deported to Mexico on Aug. 25; a federal judge in Brownsville declines to hold ICE officer Christian Castro past the legal limit on Aug. 26; and Texas' 90-day cap on holding Castro before extradition expires on Aug. 27](/images/2026-08-27/immigration-week.png)
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · KXAN, CBS Austin, KUT and The Texas Tribune</figcaption>

A 5-year-old boy and his father, detained by immigration officers during a traffic stop in
Austin on Aug. 16, were deported to Mexico on Tuesday night, the family's attorney said. Two
other Texas immigration cases turned this week on how long someone may be held in custody, and
in both a federal judge ruled against keeping the person in.

Victor Martinez Nieto and his son Liam were stopped in Austin on Aug. 16 and taken to the Dilley
detention facility south of San Antonio, which is set up to hold families. Immigration attorney
Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch told KXAN the two were on their way to a soccer game when the truck they
were in was pulled over and officers asked about their status. She said Martinez Nieto asked from
the start that Liam be released to the boy's mother, Maria, and that the Department of Homeland
Security would not allow it — leaving a choice between being removed alone while his son stayed
in detention, or taking Liam with him.

"It's a sad outcome in this case," Lincoln-Goldfinch wrote Wednesday. She told CBS Austin the
two received no U.S. government help getting home after they landed, and that the Mexican
consulate and the Mexican government paid for the travel that reunited them with relatives.

DHS said ICE arrested Martinez Nieto during a vehicle stop and that he had been removed once
before, on Feb. 12, 2026, after accepting voluntary departure following a 2025 arrest in Hutto,
then reentered at an unknown time and place. "Parents have a choice if they want to be removed
with their children or ICE will place the children with a safe person the parent designates," a
department spokesperson said, adding that Dilley "is retrofitted for families" and that children
there have access to teachers and classrooms. U.S. Rep. Greg Casar, who raised the case
publicly, had called for Liam's release and for the Dilley facility to close: "kids should be
playing soccer, going to school, living their lives, not being arrested on the streets."

On Monday, U.S. District Judge Orlando
Garcia in San Antonio ordered the government to release a 19-year-old Austin woman within two
days, ruling it had violated the constitution by detaining her without a hearing; she had spent
about two months apart from her 8-month-old son, and ICE released her Tuesday. Such cases "must
not be allowed to founder in a procedural morass," Garcia wrote.

On Wednesday, U.S. District Judge Fernando Rodriguez Jr. in Brownsville declined Minnesota's
request to hold ICE officer Christian Castro in the Cameron County jail past the 90-day Texas
limit on pre-extradition detention. Minnesota has charged Castro with four counts of assault
over a January shooting in Minneapolis and sued to force Gov. Greg Abbott to sign extradition
paperwork. Rodriguez found the suit not yet ripe because Abbott has not denied the request, and
wrote that while Castro's "flight to Mexico would impose a hardship on Minnesota's prosecutorial
efforts," that possibility "does not alter the analysis."

Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison said he strongly disagrees and that "Christian Castro
will not escape justice in Minnesota." Abbott's press secretary, Andrew Mahaleris, said the
order "rebuffs Minnesota's efforts to conscript federal courts into commandeering the authority
of Governor Abbott's office." Abbott has told the court he has "serious doubts" that Castro
qualifies as a fugitive and ordered Secretary of State Robert Howden to look into it; that
inquiry is still open.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The 90-day limit on holding Castro before extradition expires Thursday, and Abbott's review of whether he is a fugitive remains pending.</p>

#### Roads & transit

The Texas Transportation Commission approved $95 billion Wednesday as part of the 2027 Unified Transportation Program, the core of a 10-year, $138 billion plan announced by TxDOT and Gov. Greg Abbott; the remaining $43 billion goes to development and routine maintenance. "It is focused on practical outcomes — improving safety, preserving the system we have, and supporting reliable movement for people and goods," TxDOT Executive Director Marc Williams said. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

![Bar chart of the four costliest Central Texas projects named in TxDOT's 2027 Unified Transportation Program release: the I-35 University segment at $343.8 million, the I-35 Downtown segment at $343.5 million, an added lane in each direction on SH 130 in Williamson County at $274.5 million, and an overpass at SH 71 and FM 1209 in Bastrop County at $109.8 million](/images/2026-08-27/txdot-central-texas.png)
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin · Texas Department of Transportation, via KXAN</figcaption>

TxDOT's release named a series of Central Texas projects for funding, among them two I-35 Capital Express Central segments at an estimated $343.8 million and $343.5 million, an extra lane each way on SH 130 between Georgetown and SH 45 North at $274.5 million, and bridges at Loop 360 and Lakewood Drive and at Loop 360 and Spicewood Springs Road. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Safety advocates and injury attorneys reacted Wednesday to the death of an e-scooter rider struck on northbound MoPac. State law limits scooters to roads posted at 35 mph or below and Austin's city code bars them from highways, attorney Lenore Shefman said. The city's Vision Zero dashboard shows at least 40 e-scooter riders seriously injured or killed in Austin since 2022. <span class="src">CBS Austin</span>

Kyle's City Council approved a $478,638.51 task order on Aug. 18 to restudy 12 intersections where roundabouts have been recommended, on a 4-3 vote. Phase 1 will rank options beyond signals and roundabouts; staff expects no more than four intersections to need a second phase. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

#### Public safety & courts

Austin police identified the motorcyclist killed in a north Austin crash as Isela Moreno, 21. Officers responded Sunday at 10:03 p.m. in the 10300 block of Anderson Mill Road; Moreno was taken to a hospital and died Monday. The other driver stayed at the scene and is cooperating. It is Austin's 53rd fatal crash of 2026, with 53 deaths. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

James Charles Johnson, 36, was arrested Aug. 21 and charged with first-degree felony murder in a 2024 fentanyl death, according to Travis County court documents. A man was found unconscious at a Parmer Lane gas station on Feb. 13, 2024, and pronounced dead; the medical examiner's office found mixed drug toxicity including fentanyl. KXAN said it has contacted Johnson's attorney. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Mobile Loaves and Fishes, the nonprofit that runs Community First Village, filed 77 evictions last year — its highest ever, against six in 2021 — and 51 so far this year, KUT found; the site's filing rate is more than three times Travis County's. Of last year's filings, 44 residents were removed. Founder Alan Graham said the nonprofit changed a fine structure after advocates raised concerns. <span class="src">KUT</span>

Liberty Hill's police chief and a Flock Safety public affairs manager briefed the City Council and about thirty residents Wednesday on the city's seven license plate readers. Chief Jeff Ringstaff said the first camera went in during April 2025 at an initial cost of $21,000 and cited four cases the readers helped since December 2025. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

#### City Hall & county

Austin City Council members brought forward a resolution Wednesday, led by Council Member Krista Laine, to declare the Austin Public Library a "Book Safe Harbor." It comes a week after Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick called on school districts to remove books he described as "so disgusting, so filthy, so dirty, so vulgar, so pornographic" that he would not open them in public, and said 80 percent of Texas schools were not complying with Senate Bill 13. "Our responsibility is not to build a collection that reflects only one perspective," library director Hannah Terrell said. <span class="src">KXAN / FOX 7 Austin</span>

Travis County has joined a federal lawsuit challenging the Trump administration's proposed mail-in ballot rule, which would require election officials to submit lists of eligible voters before the Postal Service delivers ballots. "This rule creates unnecessary confusion and puts the burden on voters and local election officials," County Clerk Dyana Limon-Mercado said. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

The Travis County Clerk's office is hiring poll workers and seasonal election staff at $20 an hour, with a job fair Saturday from 10:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. at 5501 Airport Blvd. Limon-Mercado said the office is aiming for more than 40 early voting sites and about 200 on Election Day. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Parks and Recreation Director Jesús Aguirre told the department's board Monday that cutting the city's two adult indoor leagues, volleyball and basketball, was one of several budget reduction options in the fiscal 2026-27 plan. A department spokesperson said it removes two programming positions and about $400,000. Players said they had signed up for fall leagues and were refunded last week. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

QWELL Community Foundation is looking for land for privately funded LGBTQ+ community centers, each with ground-floor retail, affordable apartments and a community floor, as the city studies a downtown LGBTQ+ cultural center. "We have to build spaces where people want to spend their time. We're not building crisis centers," executive director Clayton Gibson said. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Austin Watershed Protection won a Gold award in the Texas chapter of the American Planning Association's 2026 awards, in the environmental planning category, for Rain to River, its strategic plan for the city's creeks and watersheds. "When we plan for healthier creeks and watersheds, we are also planning for safer, more resilient, and more livable communities," director Jorge Morales said. <span class="src">City of Austin</span>

#### Schools

The Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board has begun work on three-year bachelor's degree options after a letter from Abbott, and will form a work group on standards, accreditation and workforce demand. "Every semester adds tuition, fees, housing, food, transportation, and other living expenses and delays students' ability to begin their careers," Abbott said. Recommendations are due by Dec. 31. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

More than 170 public and charter campuses in Texas have closed or been considered for closure since the 2023-24 school year, according to an analysis by the nonprofit Our Schools Our Democracy that KXAN examined this week. More than 20 had an A rating when their closures were approved. Widen Elementary is among 10 Austin campuses closing amid falling enrollment and budget pressure. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Eanes ISD trustees adopted a tax rate of $0.8254 per $100 of valuation on Aug. 18 — $0.7054 for operations and $0.12 for debt — and postponed a vote on employee compensation until September. Trustees also indefinitely postponed a land sale to the Westbank Community Library District that had been counted on for about $1.5 million. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

Hays CISD will charge athletics fees of $10 to $315 a year for the first time, scaled by grade, number of sports and financial need; families approved for reduced lunch pay half and those on free lunch get a 90 percent discount. The district announced $12.25 million in cuts in March after voters rejected a higher tax rate in November. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

UT-Austin launched the National Center for STEM Education on Aug. 11, replacing the UTeach Institute and keeping director Kimberly Hughes. The center will work on STEM teacher shortages and partnerships with business and government. STEM teaching certificates awarded nationally fell 40 percent between 2011 and 2022, according to data on the center's site. <span class="src">The Daily Texan</span>

A toilet valve failure flooded a second-floor suite in Falls Hall at Texas State University on Aug. 19, during the first week of classes. The university said staff shut off the water, the valve was repaired and students returned that evening with no relocations. Freshman Peyton Faour, who posted video of the water, said some belongings were damaged. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

#### Health

The Texas Third Court of Appeals dismissed Birch v. Travis County Hospital District, the 2017 taxpayer suit challenging Central Health's $35 million-a-year payments to UT's Dell Medical School, finding the plaintiffs lacked standing and the payments fall "squarely within" the district's authority. Travis County voters authorized the payment in 2012. <span class="src">KUT</span>

We Are Blood, Williamson County EMS and county organizations are running Wilco Saves 2026, a blood drive that starts Thursday and runs through Sept. 8 at chamber offices, city halls and police departments across the county. We Are Blood said donations typically fall around Labor Day. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

The U.S. Department of Agriculture recalled nearly 30,000 pounds of raw beef imported from Argentina and sold in Texas and Florida over missed import re-inspections. The Food Safety and Inspection Service classified it Class I, its highest risk category, without naming a contaminant. It was not immediately clear which retailers sold the beef. <span class="src">CBS Austin</span>

#### Business & tech

Meta agreed to pay nearly $17 billion to settle claims by 47 states over teen social media addiction, and Texas will receive about $1 billion, the state attorney general's office said. The agreement also requires daily usage limits for teens, limits on school-hours push alerts and stronger parental controls. Texas says the money will fund youth mental health services, digital literacy and school grants. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

A federal judge gave initial approval to a $359,925,000 settlement fund in the RealPage rent-pricing antitrust case, covering anyone in the U.S. who paid rent to an owner or manager using RealPage revenue management software between Oct. 18, 2018, and Nov. 21, 2025. The defendants denied wrongdoing and the court did not rule on the allegations. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

A Texas 2036 report released Wednesday found 56.7 percent of Texas parents say childcare costs have affected their family planning, 30 percent passed up a promotion and 11 percent left a job in the past year. The report estimates employers lose about $7.9 billion a year. Austin's Kerbey Lane Cafe told a House committee its turnover fell from about 300 percent in 2020 to 78 percent after schedule changes. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

Industrial Electric Manufacturing will build a $200 million, 1-million-square-foot plant at Brooks City Base in San Antonio, expected to bring 3,000 jobs by 2030. Abbott's office said the California company was offered a $3.36 million Texas Enterprise Fund grant tied to 448 of the jobs. <span class="src">FOX 7 Austin</span>

Scheels cut the ribbon on its 300,000-square-foot Cedar Park store Aug. 25 ahead of an Aug. 29 public opening. The employee-owned store, the chain's second in Texas, has a 65-foot indoor Ferris wheel and a 16,000-gallon saltwater aquarium; the first 50 people in line get swag and pizza, with doors at 9:30 a.m. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

Peoples Pharmacy, opened by Bill Swail in 1980 and now four Austin locations, has been sold to Kimberli Wiley, owner of CPRx Pharmacy. Wiley is starting a collaboration with Victory Medical that adds GLP-1s, peptides and in-store nurse practitioners at all four stores. Leadership stays largely unchanged. <span class="src">Community Impact</span>

Vertical construction has begun on The Ferguson, a four-story, 82-unit condominium project at 1300 W. Second St. in downtown Taylor, the city's first. Austin-based Metropolitan Development Company expects to finish in summer 2027. "The Ferguson is not just a housing development," Mayor Jim Buzan said, calling it a response to demand as new jobs arrive. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

#### Texas

The Ross Fire in Palo Pinto County grew to nearly 82,000 acres overnight and was 12 percent contained Wednesday, officials with the Texas A&M Forest Service and the county's emergency services district said. Crews stopped the fire outside Bryson with retardant drops and dirt lines. About 350 residents had been evacuated and those orders have been lifted; some structures are lost, with damage still being assessed. <span class="src">KXAN / CBS Austin</span>

Sen. Ted Cruz said on Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick's podcast Wednesday that "it's no secret that I want to be president," and answered "time will tell" when asked directly whether he would run in 2028. Cruz finished second in the 2016 Republican primaries. He goes to Iowa this weekend to campaign for U.S. Rep. Ashley Hinson. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

Gov. Greg Abbott and state Rep. Gina Hinojosa have laid out competing education agendas before November. Abbott wants a 10-year minimum sentence without parole for teachers convicted of abusing children, fewer laptops and tablets in classrooms, and a law cutting district administrative spending. Hinojosa says she would fire Education Commissioner Mike Morath on her first day, end the STAAR test and "tests like it," stop state takeovers of districts, and repeal the accounts that let families spend state money on private school. <span class="src">The Texas Tribune</span>

A federal judge in Boston lifted a nationwide block on the Postal Service's mail-in ballot rule Wednesday, after a Supreme Court ruling that a separate challenge was premature. Judge Indira Talwani wrote that the plaintiff groups are "not likely to prevail as to ripeness" without amending their complaint now that a final rule has issued. Democratic-led states filed a new challenge the same morning. <span class="src">KXAN</span>

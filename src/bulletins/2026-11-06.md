## The Briefing

{% bigstory %}
### Council clears Austin Energy bonds and a housing-blueprint rewrite.

<!-- desk:kicker Bulletin original -->

At Thursday’s first meeting since the election, council approved on consent an ordinance letting Austin Energy sell up to $695 million in electric-utility revenue refunding and/or improvement bonds by April 22, 2027, with debt service paid from utility revenues, not property taxes.

It also approved as amended a resolution directing the city manager to update the Strategic Housing Blueprint — production and preservation goals, data work, displacement strategies, stakeholder outreach and consideration of a potential bond program — and on consent locked in up to $1 million so a dedicated center-running transit lane at Dean Keeton Street and I-35 can be built into TxDOT’s (Texas Department of Transportation) Capital Express Central project; costs above $1 million would need another vote.

On three readings, council approved the North Lamar Combined Neighborhood Plan FLUM change for 604–706 Beaver Street (Single Family to Mixed Use) and the matching DB90 rezoning for the North Lamar / Beaver Street tracts.

Also approved: a consent resolution ordering a targeted commercial-space rent buy-down program for eligible local and legacy small businesses; floodplain variances at 1213 Fort Branch Boulevard for two single-family homes (staff backup said hardship for two units does not exist and one unit could be approved administratively); and, not on consent, a Tarryhollow HOA wastewater easement valued at $4,086.

Withdrawn before a vote was the related Exposition Boulevard easement (appraised $20,594).

Postponed to Nov. 19: the Save Our Springs site-specific amendment for 5811 Southwest Parkway, and the 3601 Davis Lane rezoning (at the applicant’s request).

Action Notes are labeled tentative and do not list roll-call tallies; those land in later minutes.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The Save Our Springs amendment and the Davis Lane rezoning return Nov. 19.</p>

## News

{% river %}
##### City will spend up to $5.48 million on land for the Rutland-to-Northgate trail.
Council approved on consent buying about 1.15 acres in fee simple plus access and trail easements at 9511 Metric Blvd. from Butler Broadcasting Company, Ltd., for a total not to exceed $5,481,865 including closing costs, for Austin Parks and Recreation’s Rutland to Northgate Trail project. The money is in Parks’ capital budget.
<span class="src">Source: <a href="https://services.austintexas.gov/council_meetings/action_notes.cfm?mid=1520">Action Notes item 14</a></span>

##### Mobility Committee meets Thursday on the downtown Core Transportation Plan.
The Mobility Committee’s Nov. 12 agenda, posted Thursday, includes a briefing on the near-term implementation strategy for the Austin Core Transportation Plan — the downtown piece of the Austin Strategic Mobility Plan — plus a report from the Urban Transportation Commission on right-of-way vacation and CAMPO (Capital Area Metropolitan Planning Organization)/TxDOT Parmer Lane recommendations, and a Transportation and Public Works update on lighting, speed management and other mobility programs.
<p class="if-you">The committee also takes up 2027 meeting dates. Public comment is allowed in person or by phone.</p>
<span class="src">Source: <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/council/2026/20261112-mobc">Nov. 12 Mobility Committee meeting page</a></span>

##### Hinojosa’s first transition picks put public schools and labor at the table, KUT reports.
Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa named her first transition-team appointments Thursday, KUT reports, including former Republican state Rep. Ernest Bailes, who helped block school vouchers in 2023; former Austin Independent School District (ISD) trustee Tamala Barksdale; Ryan Pollock of the Texas State Building and Construction Trades Council; and Houston consultant Joe Madden as executive director.
<p class="if-you">Hinojosa told KUT she will be “the Governor for all the people of Texas,” and said she and outgoing Gov. Greg Abbott have already been in contact about a smooth handoff. During the campaign she promised more public-school funding and an educator as education commissioner.</p>
<span class="src">Source: <a href="https://www.kut.org/2026-11-05/gina-hinojosa-texas-governor-transition-team">KUT</a></span>

##### Council canvasses the election Nov. 17; the next regular meeting is Nov. 19.
The city’s runoff calendar still puts the canvass of the Nov. 3 results at 11 a.m. Nov. 17, when council is also scheduled to order the Districts 1, 3 and 5 runoffs.
<p class="if-you">The council calendar lists a work session that day and a regular meeting Nov. 19; Thursday’s consent agenda already set a Nov. 19 public hearing on an emergency-demolition code amendment, and the Barton Springs and Davis Lane items postponed Thursday return that day. Runoff registration still ends Nov. 12; early voting runs Nov. 30–Dec. 8; Election Day is Dec. 12.</p>

##### Veterans Day parade rolls Saturday from South Congress to the Capitol.
The 70th Annual Austin Veterans Parade starts at 9 a.m. Saturday, Nov. 7, from the South Congress Avenue bridge to the Texas State Capitol, free and open to the public. A veterans resource event follows at 10 a.m., and a ceremony is at 11 a.m. on the south steps of the Capitol.
<p class="if-you">Downtown streets will close; the city points people to CapMetro, Bikeshare and the Butler Trail.</p>

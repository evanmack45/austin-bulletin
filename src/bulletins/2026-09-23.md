### Twelve days to register, and the state has just found applications it never sent

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-23/registration-calendar.png" alt="Timeline of Texas voter registration for the November 3, 2026 election. Since September 2020, after voting rights groups sued the state, Texans renewing a driver&apos;s license or updating an address online could also register to vote through the same Department of Public Safety portal; about 6 million Texans used it between 2020 and 2024. Last week the secretary of state&apos;s office learned that an unknown number of those applications were never sent on to county voter registrars because the records were incomplete. On September 22 the state said it would begin delivering the unsent registrations to counties overnight. The registration deadline is October 5. Early voting runs October 19 through October 30. Election Day is November 3.">
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · Texas secretary of state's office and DPS, via KXAN; Texas election calendar</figcaption>
</figure>

Texas election officials spent Tuesday learning that an unknown number of people who registered to vote at the driver's
license window never reached the rolls. The Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS) did not pass their applications on
to county voter registrars. The records came through incomplete. Nobody has said how many, or since when.

The portal matters more than it sounds. Texas has no online voter registration, so renewing a license or changing an
address online is the closest thing to it, and about 6 million Texans used that route between 2020 and 2024.

Then there is the calendar. Registration closes Oct. 5, and the state began pushing the missing applications out to
counties overnight, landing them on registrars in the busiest stretch of their year. Alicia Pierce of the secretary of
state's office said a voter who registered through DPS and does not appear on the rolls can cast a provisional ballot,
and that it will be counted once officials confirm the registration was filed in time.

The stakes arrived the same afternoon. A Texas Southern University poll put the U.S. Senate race between Ken Paxton
and James Talarico at essentially a dead heat, with the economy named the top issue and more than a quarter of
self-identified moderate Republicans saying they plan to break ranks and vote for the Democrat.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Registration for the Nov. 3 election closes Oct. 5, and early voting runs Oct. 19 through Oct. 30; the Bulletin's <a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026</a> page carries the official links. More in <a href="#city-hall-county">City Hall &amp; county</a>.</p>

##### Austin police released video of the immigration stop. It stops short of the shooting.

<p class="good-thing">About a dozen World War II veterans, not one of them under 100 years old, spent Sunday afternoon at the Texas Military Forces Museum on Camp Mabry telling their stories to a few hundred people. Bernard Snyder talked about a promise he and his brother made each other in the Pacific: when they got home to Austin, they were going to do something about the discrimination they had grown up with. They came back and spent their lives on it. The Gathering of World War II Heroes was put on by The War Remembered, an oral history project founded by the historian Patrick Reed. "Looking into their eyes, it's really as close as you can get to being there yourself," Reed said. "It's just a really captivating experience to sit down with them and hear their story, hear about things that happened, at this point, 80 years ago, what it would have felt like to be there."

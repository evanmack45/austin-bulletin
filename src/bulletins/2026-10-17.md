### Before voting on new bonds, a look at the last $2.45 billion

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-17/bond-dashboard.png" alt="Bar chart of the $2.45 billion in City of Austin bonds voters approved in 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2022, from KXAN's reading of the City's new bond dashboard: $1.72 billion spent, $240 million committed to existing projects but not spent, and about $490 million remaining.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin &middot; KXAN</figcaption>
</figure>

Early voting starts Monday, and two City bonds are on the ballot: $260 million for parks in Proposition A and $35 million for libraries in Proposition B. Commenters on KXAN's social media have been asking where the money from earlier bonds went. The City's new bond dashboard gives an answer.

Voters approved $2.45 billion across bond programs in 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2022. The City has spent $1.72 billion of it, KXAN reported from the dashboard. Another $240 million is committed to projects but not yet paid out, and about $490 million remains.

The 2016 transportation bond was $720 million. Of its 413 projects, 306 are finished and 24 were canceled. The 2022 affordable housing bond has spent 35% of its $350 million.

Spent and finished are different measures. The City says a project can be complete while final payments are still going out, and unspent money is not necessarily free for other projects.

Registration hit a record. Texas has more than 19 million registered voters, the Secretary of State said Friday.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Early voting runs October 19 through 30, and Election Day is November 3. The <a href="/austin-bonds-2026/">Props A and B guide</a> covers both bonds.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### What Proposition A's $260 million would build
Proposition A splits $260 million five ways, Community Impact reported: $65.5 million for Austin Parks and Recreation buildings, $65 million for parkland improvements, $65 million for aquatics facilities, $35 million to buy new parkland and $29.5 million for parkland infrastructure. City officials narrowed this year's package to parks and libraries ahead of larger bond packages planned for 2028 and 2034. <span class="src"><a href="https://communityimpact.com/east-austin/election/what-austin-voters-need-to-know-about-10-ballot-propositions-from-bonds-to-charter-changes/">Community Impact</a></span>

Austin Energy's expanded e-ride rebate pays individual customers up to $600, fleets up to $800 a vehicle and Customer Assistance Program participants up to $1,300, first come, first served. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/austin-energy-wants-to-help-pay-for-your-next-e-moto-bike-or-scooter/">KXAN</a></span>

#### City Hall & county

##### Another state error delays voter address updates
A state software error held up name and address changes Texans made on texas.gov from September 1 until last week, and the Secretary of State's office sent 15,000 records back to counties, Travis among them. Before early voting opens Monday, counties must sort out which updates came in by the October 5 deadline. Voters can check their registration at votetexas.gov. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-10-16/another-texas-voter-registration-backlog-state-software-error">KUT</a></span>

University Presbyterian Church, at 22nd and San Antonio streets, will be an Election Day polling place from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., West Campus's first in-neighborhood site in five years. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/west-campus-church-to-serve-as-new-polling-location-on-election-day/">KXAN</a></span>

Three of nine Austin Community College board seats are on the ballot: Dana Walker faces Gloria Gonzales-Dholakia, Nan McRaven faces Trevor Hale, and Gigi Edwards Bryant is unopposed. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/10/16/austin-community-college-board-candidates-2026-election/">Austin Current</a></span>

Austin Economic Development opened a nine-question survey, in English and Spanish, to shape the economic development policy the Council ordered in May; listening sessions follow in early 2027. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/economic-development/news/city-invites-community-input-economic-development-policy">City of Austin</a></span>

##### Mayor says Burnet and Webb petitions appear to clear the bar
Mayor Kirk Watson said Friday that enough parents appear to have signed to trigger closure of Webb and Burnet middle schools and prevent a state-appointed board, though nothing is final until the October 30 enrollment snapshot.

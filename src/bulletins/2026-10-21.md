### Travis County's first day of early voting beat every midterm this century

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-10-21/first-day-turnout.png" alt="Bar chart from KUT, using Texas Secretary of State data: on the first day of early voting in Travis County, 4.14% of registered voters cast a ballot in the 2018 midterm, 2.8% in 2022 and about 4.3% in 2026.">
<figcaption>Chart: The Austin Bulletin &middot; KUT, from Texas Secretary of State data</figcaption>
</figure>

Monday set the pace.

Nearly 41,000 Travis County residents voted on the first day of early voting, about 4.3% of registered voters, KUT reported from Texas Secretary of State data. That beat every first day of a midterm this century, including 2018 at 4.14% and 2022 at 2.8%.

County Clerk Dyana Limón-Mercado said her office set out to make this "the most accessible election in our community's history." There are 45 early voting sites, up from 38 in the last midterm, open from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. every day, weekends included. A Travis County voter can use any of them.

The pace was quicker here than across Texas. By Tuesday evening, KXAN's tracker of state data put statewide turnout at about 3.7% and turnout across its 15-county area at 5.2%.

On the Austin ballot are five City Council seats, a $295 million parks and libraries bond in Propositions A and B, and Proposition I, a recurring independent audit of City finances. Supporters say Prop I sets stronger standards than the City's audit ordinance. Opponents say it will be expensive and give the auditor a reason to cut deeper, KUT reported.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Early voting runs through October 30, when seven sites stay open until 10 p.m.; Election Day is November 3.</p>

##### Travis County keeps paying for a teen diversion program
County commissioners have allocated $2 million to the Travis County Transformation Project, which lets some youths facing a family-violence assault charge enter treatment instead, KXAN reported. Finishing the program keeps the charge off their record. It has served 150 minors since its 2023 pilot. "We think that the data supports" that it made the community safer, District Attorney José Garza said. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/how-a-teen-diversion-program-is-working-so-far-in-travis-county/">KXAN</a></span>

##### The airport's busiest stretch starts Thursday
Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS) expects heavy traffic from Thursday through Tuesday, with Monday, the day after Sunday's Formula 1 race, its single busiest day. It advises arriving 2.5 hours before domestic flights and three hours before international ones. On October 19 the airport began a utility project for its expansion, due to finish in 2029. #### Schools

##### Austin ISD trustees vote October 29 on new boundaries
Austin Independent School District (AISD) trustees vote October 29 on boundary changes aimed at overcrowding at Linder Elementary and at giving Marshall Middle School its own attendance area. Some households now zoned to Dobie, Kealing, Lamar and Webb middle schools could move to Marshall. The district says Lamar is at 114% of capacity; Marshall, built for 915, could see about 962 students assigned. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/austin-isd-trustees-to-vote-on-school-boundary-changes-for-linder-marshall">CBS Austin</a></span>

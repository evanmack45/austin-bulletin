### Austin's 48th homicide of 2026 came a week before 2025's.

<!-- desk:kicker Bulletin original -->

Austin police are investigating Thursday's fatal shooting at the Circle K at 3909 Guadalupe St. as the city's 48th homicide of 2026. Police said Bruce Williams, 61, followed a man through the parking lot while threatening to assault and kill him, and the man turned and shot him at close range, then called 9-1-1, stayed at the scene and gave a statement. No charges have been filed, and police say the investigation is ongoing.

Last year, the Austin Police Department's (APD) 48th homicide came on Nov. 12, 2025, a downtown shooting at East Fifth and Trinity streets. This year's 48th came on Nov. 5, 2026. A Bulletin calendar-day comparison of Nov. 5, 2026 and Nov. 12, 2025 is seven days. KXAN's year-end tally put 2025 at 55 homicides. These are APD's working case numbers, which are assigned as cases are opened and can change as investigations go on; the comparison shows timing only and says nothing about why.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> No charges have been filed, and police say the investigation is ongoing.</p>

##### Austin will rewrite APD's rules on working with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) before Hinojosa takes office, KUT reports.
Mayor Kirk Watson said Friday he has asked Police Chief Lisa Davis to update APD's general orders on cooperating with Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) so new rules can take effect when Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa replaces Gov. Greg Abbott in January, KUT reports. APD has changed the policy twice in the past year: once in March to limit ICE involvement in local policing, and again after Abbott threatened to withhold state money.
<p class="if-you">The current rules say officers should contact ICE "when operationally feasible" when someone has an ICE administrative warrant. Watson said the city will keep following Senate Bill 4, the state law barring cities from blocking cooperation with ICE.</p>

##### Council's Nov. 19 draft agenda: Muny-UT deal changes, a $25 million trail boardwalk and a $55 million Bulletin sum for Hornsby Bend.
The draft agenda for the Nov. 19 council meeting, posted Friday, includes amendments to the city's agreement with the University of Texas covering land development rules around Lions Municipal Golf Course (Muny), its planned conveyance, right-of-way vacations and a tax increment reinvestment zone (item 50).
<p class="if-you">Council would also accept $25 million in state money from the Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) to design and build a boardwalk on the south bank of Lady Bird Lake next to the Hyatt Regency, connecting to the Butler Trail (items 11 and 52). The draft lists two Matous Construction contracts at Austin Water's Hornsby Bend biosolids plant for not more than $22,420,200 and $32,906,529 (items 14 and 15). A Bulletin sum of those source amounts is $55,326,729, which rounds to about $55.3 million and to $55 million. Six eminent domain resolutions would acquire small easements so Austin Energy can move transmission lines for the I-35 Capital Express project (items 77-82). Sponsored by the mayor and three council members, items 70 and 71 would put social-services contracts under standard procurement and anti-lobbying rules. The 5811 Southwest Parkway Save Our Springs amendment and the 3601 Davis Lane rezoning, both postponed Thursday, return as items 87 and 93. The city labels this a draft; items can change before the final agenda.</p>

##### Ten affordable townhomes for sale break ground on Menchaca Road, with 2-bedrooms capped at $253,000.
Construction has started on Menchaca Townhomes at 7331 Menchaca Road in District 5, 10 for-sale homes reserved for households earning at or below 80% of area median income, Austin Housing announced Friday. City affordable-housing guidelines cap prices at $253,000 for a two-bedroom and $289,500 for a three-bedroom, and the homes carry long-term affordability and resale restrictions.
<p class="if-you">The project by Capital A Housing got $1.9 million from the Austin Housing Finance Corp.'s ownership program, funded by the Housing Trust Fund and voter-approved bonds. Work began in early August; the first three homes are due in February 2027 and the rest by early 2028. Buyers will go through income verification with Austin Housing, and the city says details will come later.</p>
<span class="src">Source: <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/housing/news/new-affordable-menchaca-townhomes-bring-ten-homeownership-opportunities-south-austin">Austin Housing release</a></span>

##### Police charge two in Northeast Austin copper-cable thefts and seek one of them; a separate East Seventh café burglary is charged.
APD's Commercial Burglary and Metal Theft Unit says thieves used vehicles to pull down wooden telephone poles in Northeast Austin starting in August to strip AT&T communication cable. Police say Adam Ray Lawrence, 37, sold more than 600 pounds of copper days after one theft; he was charged with theft of material (copper), a state jail felony, and booked into the Travis County Jail in October.
<p class="if-you">A warrant on the same charge is out for Clifford Nelson Locklin, 52, who police believe lives in Bastrop. Tips go to the unit at (512) 974-6941 or Crime Stoppers at 512-472-8477, with a reward of up to $1,000. In a separate case, police charged Larry Ray Vasquez, 56, with burglary of a building in the July 7 break-in at The Cenote Café, 1405 E. Seventh St., where a safe was forced open and cash taken; he was served while already in jail on an unrelated burglary charge. A charge is an accusation, not a finding of guilt.</p>
<span class="src">Sources: <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/police/news/apd-arrests-suspect-copper-cable-theft-series-seeks-public-assistance-locating-second">APD copper theft release</a> · <a href="https://www.austintexas.gov/police/news/apd-arrests-suspect-connection-east-austin-commercial-burglary-series">APD burglary release</a></span>

##### Senfronia Thompson says she has the votes to be Texas House speaker after Democrats won the chamber.
State Rep. Senfronia Thompson, D-Houston, said Friday that a majority of the Texas House has pledged to back her for speaker, which would make her the first woman and first Black lawmaker to lead the chamber, the Texas Newsroom reports via KUT. Austin Democratic Reps. Lulu Flores and Aicha Davis stood with her at the Capitol announcement.
<p class="if-you">The vote comes when the Legislature convenes Jan. 12; it takes 76 of 150 votes. Thompson has served since 1973.</p>
<span class="src">Source: <a href="https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-11-06/texas-house-speaker-senfronia-thompson-democrats">KUT / The Texas Newsroom</a></span>

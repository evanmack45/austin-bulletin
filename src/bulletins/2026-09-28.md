### Two bus systems, one fuel bill

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-28/capmetro-monthly-pass.png" alt="Bar chart of CapMetro calendar-month pass prices under the fare proposal its board votes on September 28, 2026, taken from CapMetro's published fare table. The board meets at noon. CapMetro's directors vote today at their East Fifth Street headquarters on the first
system-wide fare increase since 2015, a single local ride going from $1.25 to $1.50 in January 2027 and to $2 in
January 2029, with steps of the same shape for commuter and paratransit riders. The agency says inflation in fuel,
equipment, maintenance and operations has outrun what it could keep absorbing.

The Austin Independent School District (AISD) is doing the same arithmetic with no fare to raise. It spent nearly twice
as much on diesel in the first month of this school year as in the first month of last, and ridership was lower. That
is after cutting $1.8 million out of transportation and moving high school students off neighborhood stops onto hubs.
Trustees heard Thursday that enrollment landed under what the budget assumed and that roughly $60 million has to come
out for 2027-28.

Riders told CapMetro both things. "There's never going to be a good time for a fare increase," said Awais Azhar, chair
of the Project Connect Advisory Committee, who backs the proposal anyway. Anthony Rodriguez, out of work and saving for
a used car, asked the board in a written comment to find the money somewhere else. The 50% reduced-fare discount for
seniors, riders with disabilities, Medicare card holders and eligible military personnel stays either way.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> The CapMetro vote is at noon today; if it passes, the first step lands Jan. 10, 2027. AISD's chief financial officer starts bringing cuts to the board in January. The fare detail is in <a href="#money-bills">Money &amp; bills</a> and the district's numbers in <a href="#schools">Schools</a>.</p>

#### Money & bills

##### CapMetro votes today on its first fare increase in eleven years
Directors meet at noon at the agency's East Fifth Street headquarters. A local bus or Pickup ride would go to $1.50 on
Jan. 10, 2027 and $2 in January 2029; commuter service goes to $4 then $5, and Access, the paratransit service, to $2.25
then $3.25. CapMetro says fares have not changed system-wide since
2015. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/traffic/transportation/capmetro-directors-consider-fare-increase-monday/">KXAN</a></span>

A monthly local pass would go from $41.25 to $49.50 in 2027 and $66 in 2029; a commuter pass from $96.25 to $110 and then $137.50. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.capmetro.org/fare-change">CapMetro</a></span>

Ticket vending machines come out of bus stations and transit centers immediately after any approval, leaving one machine at each rail station, which CapMetro attributes to low use. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.capmetro.org/fare-change">CapMetro</a></span>

The Equifare program would fold into the Reduced Fare program, and existing Equifare riders would keep a 50% discount; CapMetro says its Title VI review found no disproportionate burden. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.capmetro.org/fare-change">CapMetro</a></span>

"I rely on the bus for my appointments, job, and school," Anthony Rodriguez wrote to the board, saying he is out of work and saving for a used car. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/traffic/transportation/capmetro-directors-consider-fare-increase-monday/">KXAN</a></span>

Jamie McClinton paid $161.30 through the resale site TickPick for two tickets to a Raiders tailgate that turned out to be free and open to the public, she told CBS Austin. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/woman-warns-of-ticket-scams-after-paying-for-free-tailgate-the-receipts">CBS Austin</a></span>

University of Texas athletics staff told the station they see counterfeit tickets at nearly every game, some fake outright and some screenshots of one real ticket resold to several buyers. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/woman-warns-of-ticket-scams-after-paying-for-free-tailgate-the-receipts">CBS Austin</a></span>

#### Public safety & courts

##### About 160 people came back to the corner where the shooting happened
A week after a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot Wilber Garcés Pérez, protesters filled the
intersection of Anderson Square and Research Boulevard in North Austin on Sunday afternoon. Under the overpass was
graffiti reading "Free Wilber." A makeshift memorial held candles and flowers. Lucila Carlos, a master's student at the
University of Texas at Austin, said it was her first
protest. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-09-27/austin-texas-tx-ice-protests-wilber-garces-perez">KUT</a></span>

The Party for Socialism and Liberation organized Sunday's rally; spokesperson Yayo Ayala said the change protesters want would take "hundreds and thousands of people." <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/austin-ice-shooting/protest-against-ice-happening-in-north-austin-sunday/">KXAN</a></span>

Misael Ramos, the District 1 council candidate arrested Saturday on a riot participation charge and released the same day, was at Sunday's protest. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-09-27/austin-texas-tx-ice-protests-wilber-garces-perez">KUT</a></span>

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) said in a post last week that ICE "will not be slowing down operations in Austin" and that it does not choose which laws to enforce or where. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/austin-ice-shooting/protest-against-ice-happening-in-north-austin-sunday/">KXAN</a></span>

DHS said Garcés Pérez's work permit expired in 2025, that a permit alone confers no legal status, and that he has had around-the-clock medical care including pain medication. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/austin-ice-shooting/protest-against-ice-happening-in-north-austin-sunday/">KXAN</a></span>

Garcés Pérez, 28, is Venezuelan and was working as a food delivery driver the day he was shot, said Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch, the Austin immigration attorney representing him. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/austin-ice-shooting/protest-against-ice-happening-in-north-austin-sunday/">KXAN</a></span>

##### The consultants told the city to keep 911 inside the police department
Mission Critical Partners told a Tuesday meeting that Austin should hire civilian leadership for the existing center
rather than build the separate Office of Joint Emergency Communications the City Council began planning in August. Vice
President Bonnie Maney said the Austin Police Department (APD) sits in the top 5% nationally for response times.
Austin Police Association president Michael Bullock said a new center would add
bureaucracy. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/09/27/new-report-recommends-against-city-councils-independent-emergency-call-center-decision/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

Council Member Marc Duchen of District 10 called a separate department "a more expensive way" to improve the call center, citing coordination and records problems. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/09/27/new-report-recommends-against-city-councils-independent-emergency-call-center-decision/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

Rebecca Webber, speaking for the nonprofit Equity Action, said she is suing Austin police over a deaf client she says was wrongly prosecuted after a 911 ambulance call brought an officer. <span class="src"><a href="https://thedailytexan.com/2026/09/27/new-report-recommends-against-city-councils-independent-emergency-call-center-decision/">The Daily Texan</a></span>

#### City Hall & county

##### Paige Ellis has spent a month working out how she lost by 62 names
The District 8 council member turned in her third-term petition at 4:19 p.m. on Aug. 17, 41 minutes before the
deadline, with 365 more signatures than the 3,044 she needed. A week later the city clerk rejected it. The office
counted 3,317 names and struck 335 for duplicates, missing birthdays, addresses outside the district and other reasons.
Candidates have no role in that review and no appeal. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/28/austin-citycouncil-paigeellis-government-term-limits/">Austin Current</a></span>

Ellis started collecting Feb. 17, believing a 180-day window applied; she learned in January there is none. "I could have started in June of 2025 if I had known that," she said. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/28/austin-citycouncil-paigeellis-government-term-limits/">Austin Current</a></span>

Kathie Tovo, the only council member to win a third term in the last decade, turned in 4,700 signatures against a 3,500 threshold in 2018, after six months and about 80 volunteers. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/28/austin-citycouncil-paigeellis-government-term-limits/">Austin Current</a></span>

Ellis's husband, political strategist Ed Espinoza, said he found at least 26 struck names he believes were valid, rejected over two-word street names, nicknames and handwriting. <span class="src"><a href="https://austincurrent.org/2026/09/28/austin-citycouncil-paigeellis-government-term-limits/">Austin Current</a></span>

##### The District 8 seat Ellis cannot defend has two candidates and a mood
District 8, which runs from Barton Creek down through Oak Hill to Circle C, is open for the first time in eight years.
Selena Xie, an Austin-Travis County EMS commander and former Austin EMS Association president, faces Jeffery Bowen, a
construction manager and spending watchdog. Consultant Mark Littlefield, a resident for 23 years, said this will be
"the toughest election for incumbents on Council." <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/news/local/article/austin-district-8-city-council-race-22422222.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

Six District 8 voters interviewed by the Statesman raised property taxes, city spending and growth; the district has met about 20% of its affordable housing goal under the city's Strategic Housing Blueprint. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/news/local/article/austin-district-8-city-council-race-22422222.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

The last day to register for the Nov. 3 election is Oct. 5, and Travis County Tax Assessor-Collector Celia Israel said her office is working nights and weekends to keep up. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-09-28/austin-tx-travis-county-texas-voting-registration-deadline-vote">KUT</a></span>

Israel said the office took in 1,000 applications from would-be volunteer deputy registrars in a single morning, and that primary voting this year was up 30%. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-09-28/austin-tx-travis-county-texas-voting-registration-deadline-vote">KUT</a></span>

Anyone registering on the deadline should drop the form at the voter registrar's office rather than mail it, Israel said, because postmarks can lag by days. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-09-28/austin-tx-travis-county-texas-voting-registration-deadline-vote">KUT</a></span>

Early voting in Travis County runs Oct. 19 through Oct. 30; the Bulletin's <a href="/vote-2026/">Vote 2026</a> page carries the official registration and polling links. <span class="src"><a href="https://votetravis.gov/current-election-information/current-election/">Travis County Elections</a></span>

#### Schools

##### Austin ISD has fewer students than it budgeted for, and a bigger hole next year
Chief Financial Officer Katrina Montgomery told trustees Thursday the district had 65,951 students enrolled as of
Sept. 18 against the 68,329 it projected. About 69,000 were enrolled last year. The district needs to cut about
$5.7 million this year, or up to $14.8 million if no more students arrive, and roughly $60 million for
2027-28. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/news/education/article/austin-isd-enrollment-budget-cuts-22449371.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

The district spent $364,369 on diesel in the first month of school, against $186,447 in the same month last year, and Montgomery expects the year's diesel bill to run $1.9 million higher. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/news/education/article/austin-isd-enrollment-budget-cuts-22449371.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

That came after the district cut about $1.8 million from transportation, including moving high school routes to a hub model instead of neighborhood stops, and ridership is down. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/news/education/article/austin-isd-enrollment-budget-cuts-22449371.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

### Austin turned its cameras off. The data still arrives.

<figure class="graphic">
<img src="/images/2026-09-07/plate-readers.png" alt="Timeline of Austin&apos;s automated license plate reader program: a Flock Safety pilot began in 2023 with 40 fixed cameras and nearly 500 vehicle-mounted cameras, the city manager declined to bring a renewal to City Council in June 2025, police switched the readers off in July 2025, the council passed a surveillance ordinance in April 2026, and as of September 2026 Austin police still receive license plate data from other agencies">
<figcaption>Timeline: The Austin Bulletin · Austin American-Statesman</figcaption>
</figure>

Austin has not run its own license plate cameras since July of last year. The pilot started in 2023 with 40 fixed
cameras and nearly 500 mounted on city vehicles. Then a city audit found that 10% to 20% of officer searches carried
no clear reason or case number, and City Manager T.C. Broadnax declined to bring a renewal to the council. The readers
went dark. The data did not.

Assistant Police Chief JJ Schmidt told the Austin American-Statesman that Austin officers do not search a plate
database themselves, but that a partner agency with lawful access may share results during a joint investigation. The
department has no written agreements covering those exchanges and keeps no record of them, so nobody can say how often
it happens or which agencies supplied what.

The question is live because the vendor is in trouble everywhere else. At least five Central Texas cities have moved
to drop Flock Safety since Aug. 25, and Gov. Greg Abbott has ordered state agencies to stop spending money on the
cameras. So Austin has to decide: buy a different system, or write rules for data its officers get from somewhere
else. Mayor Kirk Watson said the conversation is not all-or-nothing. Three council members told the Statesman they
would not run plate readers at all.

<p class="whats-next"><strong>What's next:</strong> Any new plate reader contract needs council approval. The council's next regular meeting is Thursday, Sept. 10; speaker registration opens today at 10 a.m.</p>

##### Austin renters say the repairs pile up and the phone goes unanswered

cannot get anyone to write back. Collin Taylor, at Eastwood Apartments, said his air conditioning was out for three

#### Public safety & courts

##### Austin's council splits on ever running plate readers again
Council Members Mike Siegel, Vanessa Fuentes and Zo Qadri told the Austin American-Statesman they oppose automated
license plate readers in any form. Chito Vela, who wrote the city's surveillance ordinance with Siegel, said he would
still want them for a stolen car or a recent shooting, but with a retention window shorter than the seven days the old
policy allowed. Michael Bullock, president of the Austin Police Association, said reasonable guardrails have never
been a problem. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/news/local/article/flock-austin-central-texas-license-plate-readers-22411459.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

##### A council member wants Lake Austin's jet ski ban made bigger
People who live along Lake Austin told KXAN the mix of boats and rented personal watercraft has made a bad collision
feel like a matter of time. Council Member Marc Duchen said a jet ski recently struck a boat and hit a child, and that
his office and the city's legal department are working to widen the ban to more hours and holidays. The police lake
patrol runs one boat on weekends. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/concerns-grow-over-safety-jet-ski-use-on-lake-austin/">KXAN</a></span>

The city's ban on personal watercraft on Lake Austin, which covers three weekends a year, holds until sunrise Tuesday. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.fox7austin.com/news/personal-watercrafts-banned-lake-austin-labor-day-2026">FOX 7 Austin</a></span>

Austin-Travis County Emergency Medical Services took 13 people from Saturday's Texas football opener to hospitals, at least three with complaints that could be heat-related; the temperature reached 102. <span class="src"><a href="https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/austin-travis-county-ems-reports-13-people-transported-from-seasons-first-football-game">CBS Austin</a></span>

#### Growth & infrastructure

##### The old 3M campus is headed for data center work
SE Cosmos, a unit of SB Energy affiliated with SoftBank Group, told the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on
Sept. 1 that it will turn the 107-acre former 3M campus in northwest Austin into a research and data center site,
with nearly $1 billion in financing. S&P Global said the filing describes 50 megawatts of critical information
technology load. City Council has directed staff to draft data center limits. <span class="src"><a href="https://www.statesman.com/business/technology/article/austin-3m-campus-data-center-softbank-22416647.php">Austin American-Statesman</a></span>

## November 2026 voting in Austin

Voting for the November 3, 2026 election is over.

<p class="results-payoff"><a href="/election/2026-11-03/">Election results</a></p>

This page is the record of that election for Austin residents registered in Travis County: the ballot, the dates, and the county lists. If you are registered in Hays or Williamson County, use your own county’s election information; the <a href="{{ vote2026.links.cityVoterResources }}">City of Austin voter resources</a> link to each county. No endorsements; no ads.

<h3 id="do-these-first" tabindex="-1">After November 3</h3>

November 12 is the last day to register to vote in the runoff, if the Council orders one. It falls before the scheduled November 17 canvass. Do not wait for a runoff order to register.

The Travis County count of City of Austin measures is on the <a href="/election/2026-11-03/">results page</a>.

City Council’s canvass of the November 3 municipal election is scheduled for 11 a.m. on November 17, in the Council Chambers at City Hall, 301 W. Second Street. The City calendar says the actual canvass date could be earlier, pending the election returns from the counties. On the canvass day, the Council orders any necessary runoff.

If the Council orders a runoff, early voting by personal appearance runs November 30 through December 8. A ballot-by-mail application must be received by December 1, not merely postmarked. Election Day is December 12.

The City scheduled a runoff candidate panel, if one is needed, for November 20 at 6:30 p.m. in the City Hall Council Chambers, 301 W. 2nd St. The five district panels before November 3 are over; dates are in the panels section below.

<h3 id="what-to-bring">What to bring (record)</h3>

November 3 polls are closed. The rules below are what VoteTexas and the County Clerk published for this election, read October 17, 2026.

**One of these seven photo IDs**, if the voter had one:

- Texas driver license (DPS)
- Texas Election Identification Certificate (DPS)
- Texas personal identification card (DPS)
- Texas handgun license (DPS)
- U.S. military ID card with a photo
- U.S. citizenship certificate with a photo
- U.S. passport (book or card)

For a voter age 18 to 69, the ID could be expired up to four years. For a voter 70 or older, it could be expired for any length of time. A citizenship certificate does not expire. Election Identification Certificates are free at DPS driver license offices.

**No photo ID, and no reasonable way to get one.** A voter could still cast a regular ballot. The supporting document had to show the voter’s name: a voter registration certificate or another government document with a name and an address, a current utility bill, a bank statement, a government check, a paycheck, or a certified U.S. birth certificate. The voter then signed a Reasonable Impediment Declaration at the polls. The impediments the form accepts are lack of transportation, disability or illness, lack of a birth certificate or other documents needed to get an ID, work schedule, family responsibilities, a lost or stolen ID, or an ID applied for but not yet received. A false statement on the form is perjury.

**A photo ID left at home.** The voter could go back for it and return before the polls closed, or vote a provisional ballot and show the ID to the voter registrar within six days of the election.

**At the polls:** a phone, camera, tablet or laptop was not allowed in the room where people vote, and sound or images could not be recorded within 100 feet of a voting station. Campaign signs and literature were not allowed within 100 feet of the entrance. Clothing about a candidate, measure or party was not allowed, and a voter could be asked to cover it.

**Curbside voting:** a voter who could not enter the building could use the marked curbside space. Each site had a number to call or text, and an election officer brought the ballot to the car. That option applied during early voting and on Election Day.

### Registration for a later election

The October 5, 2026 registration deadline for the November 3 election has passed. Nothing in this section reopens it. For a runoff, if one is ordered, the November 12 registration deadline arrives before the scheduled canvass on November 17. Registration cannot wait for that order. Filling out that form online alone does not complete registration.

## Austin Council on October 22: three items worth following

**Reviewed October 11, 2026; agenda rechecked October 18.** The City's agenda for the Thursday, October 22 Council meeting is posted as final, with a 10 a.m. start at City Hall. It now runs to 53 numbered items: the City added items 52 and 53 after October 11, and item 2 now reads "This item has been removed." The three items below are a selection, not the complete agenda, and not a prediction of what Council will decide. [Every item, in the City's wording, is on Y'all, City Hall's agenda page](https://yallcityhall.org/agenda/).

### Item 27: $224 million more for the airport's utility backbone

**Item 27** would amend the construction contract with **Sundt/Archer Western** for the airport's Utility Infrastructure Campus Wide Project, adding **$224,000,000** for a revised total of up to **$344,000,000**. Council approved the original contract, up to $120,000,000, on July 18, 2024.

Staff says the project carries the airport's storm drainage, water quality treatment and detention, electrical distribution and communications systems, and that Concourse B and the Arrivals and Departures Hall have grown in size and scope since the contract was approved. The added money would cover three planned work packages: larger storm drainage trunk lines, an expanded water quality pond system tied to the existing deicing pond, and more redundancy in medium-voltage power distribution. Staff writes that a delay would affect the schedules for the Central Utility Plant relocation, the Arrivals and Departures Hall, and Concourse B and its tunnel.

The money comes from Austin Aviation's capital budget. Staff says the department has applied for federal grants to cover part of the cost. The work stays inside the airport campus, in District 2. The Airport Advisory Commission was scheduled to review the item on October 14, before the Council vote.

**What to check after October 22:** whether Council approves the full $224 million, and the federal grant decisions staff mentions.

### Item 49: parkland for South Pleasant Valley Road

**Item 49** is a public hearing on using about **121,156 square feet** of dedicated parkland in the **Country Club Creek West Greenbelt** for a roadway. Under Chapter 26 of the Texas Parks and Wildlife Code, Council can approve a non-park use only if it finds there is no feasible and prudent alternative and that planning has minimized harm to the land.

The road is the South Pleasant Valley Corridor Gap project. Staff describes it as four vehicle lanes with transit priority treatments, a raised median, bicycle facilities for all ages and abilities, and sidewalks where they are missing. Austin Transportation and Public Works would pay Austin Parks and Recreation **$1,368,050** in mitigation and provide **$107,735** in park improvements.

On September 28, the Parks and Recreation Board voted 8-0 that it could not recommend approval without more information about community involvement. It asked Council to weigh community outreach and traffic impact before deciding.

**What to check after October 22:** whether Council makes the Chapter 26 findings and approves the change, delays it, or asks for more outreach.

### Item 44: what Austin asks for on Parmer Lane

**Item 44** is a resolution from Council Member **Krista Laine**, co-sponsored by Council Members Vanessa Fuentes, Zohaib "Zo" Qadri, Mike Siegel and Paige Ellis. The Capital Area Metropolitan Planning Organization and the Texas Department of Transportation are studying Parmer Lane (FM 734) from MoPac to Whitestone Boulevard in Cedar Park. The draft resolution says the short-term ideas are mostly intersection changes, and the long-term concepts include controlled-access main lanes with grade separations and frontage roads on both sides.

The draft would direct the City Manager to work with both agencies so that any Parmer proposal provides continuous walking and biking facilities and new signaled crossings, with routes to McNeil High School and Jollyville Elementary as examples. It also asks that proposals not increase vehicle miles traveled or greenhouse gas emissions, minimize roadway widening and tree loss, and pursue lower speed limits. For long-term concepts, it asks for safe crossings at least every quarter mile and design speeds no higher than 40 mph where there are driveways, crossings or homes. It would also direct the City to advocate for a full environmental review of any proposal for the corridor.

A Council resolution directs City staff. It does not decide what the state builds on Parmer, which is a state road.

**What to check after October 22:** the vote, any amendments to the list of requests, and the next step in the CAMPO and TxDOT study.

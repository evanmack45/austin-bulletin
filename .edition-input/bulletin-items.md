## News

**Bulletin original: Licensed short-term rentals rise since September** [ORIGINAL — Tally weekly finding]
Austin had 3,663 active short-term rental licenses on Nov. 10, 2026, the most in the City's daily count, which starts Feb. 28, 2025. That is up 648 (21.5%) from 3,015 on Sept. 30. The same stretch last year added 103 (2,503 to 2,606, up 4.1%).
**Why it matters:** The City pauses all new short-term rental license applications, renewals and hotel occupancy tax payments Nov. 16 through Dec. 3 while it moves to a new system launching Dec. 4. The dataset won't update during the pause.
Caveat: these are active licenses, not listings, bookings or rentals actually operating; the count is net, and the data doesn't say why it rose. Comparison window is 41 days.
Sources: [City dataset, Active Short-Term Rental Counts (mydx-h5dy)](https://data.austintexas.gov/d/mydx-h5dy) · [City of Austin short-term rentals page](https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/short-term-rentals)
Desk note: dataset now also has Nov. 11 (3,663, same as Nov. 10; not on Tally's sheet, not printed). Nov. 10 and Sept. 30 re-queried live ~7:36 a.m.: 3,663 and 3,015, unchanged. City STR page re-checked ~7:35 a.m.: pause and Dec. 4 launch unchanged.

**North Austin flood project near Cook Elementary hits a milestone**
New weirs on Quail Valley Boulevard and Mountain Quail Road now send Little Walnut Creek floodwater into a culvert under Mearns Meadow Boulevard and on to a pond at Quail Creek Neighborhood Park. The project costs about $28.8 million, partly from 2018 bonds. The City says some homes remain in the 100-year floodplain, and pond work is still unfinished.
Source: [City of Austin release, Nov. 10](https://www.austintexas.gov/watershed-protection/news/city-austin-celebrates-little-walnut-creek-flood-risk-reduction-milestone)

**30-year habitat permit behind west Travis preserve is up for renewal**
The Balcones Canyonlands Conservation Plan, run by Travis County and the City under a 1996 federal permit, has reached its 30-year term. The county and City want residents and biologists for two advisory groups that will help rewrite it; applications close Jan. 8, 2027. The plan stays in effect meanwhile.
Source: [City of Austin / Travis County release, Nov. 10](https://www.austintexas.gov/water/news/balcones-canyonlands-conservation-plan-permit-renewal-underway)

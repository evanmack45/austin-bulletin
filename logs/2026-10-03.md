## The Briefing

Headline: "Monday is the registration cutoff. The rolls are still catching
up." Three strands of the same ballot, two of them reported for the first
time yesterday. Travis County is working through more than 35,000 voter
registration records the Texas Department of Public Safety never forwarded —
about 32,000 of them delivered to the Tax Office in a single overnight batch
on Sept. 22–23, some filed nearly a year ago. DPS attributes the failure to a
third-party vendor's October 2025 software update, says it found the problem
in late August and fixed it Sept. 13. Separately, eight Austin City Council
candidates missed the Aug. 24 city financial-disclosure deadline and seven
missed the Sept. 8 state one, with candidates in all five races among them;
the Ethics Review Commission told Community Impact the pattern repeats
election after election. Registration closes Monday, early voting opens
Oct. 19, and the Nov. 3 ballot carries five council seats, a bond election
and Proposition I.

The reader-facing point is the one the item makes plainly: a registration
counts from the date of the DPS transaction, so anyone who registered that
way by Monday is eligible even if the county has not processed the record.
A provisional ballot is the backstop.

Hero chart: `ballot-paperwork.png`, a timeline running from the Aug. 24
disclosure deadline to the "next" marker at early voting and Election Day.
Every entry sourced to Community Impact or KXAN.

**Topic concentration (EDITORIAL neutrality rule 6).** This is the second
Briefing in eleven days on the registration backlog — 2026-09-23 led with
"Twelve days to register, and the state has just found applications it never
sent" — and yesterday's City Hall & county lead was the Oct. 5 deadline. That
is a real concentration and it is a decision, not a default. What is new
today is the Travis County count (35,000, with the 32,000 overnight batch),
the candidate-disclosure failures across all five races, and the fact that the
deadline is now two days out rather than twelve. Neither of the first two had
run anywhere in the paper before. The alternative threads considered and
rejected: the DA José Garza oversight hearing (briefed 2026-10-01, and
yesterday's only new element was the House speaker's statement), ACL weekend
one (led Business & street life yesterday), and the September gasoline record
(the 2026-09-20 Briefing was Austin gas prices). **If registration or the
ballot is still the strongest candidate on Monday, it must not lead a third
time — that would make this a paper about one thing.**

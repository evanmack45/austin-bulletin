## Selection

**The Briefing — "A bigger flood fund, hours before the watch."** Travis County
Commissioners Court adopted a $2.3 billion budget Tuesday that raises the county's
reserve fund for disasters to $35 million from $18 million — an account the county
emptied responding to the July 2025 floods that killed nine people. On Tuesday evening
the National Weather Service posted a Flood Watch covering Travis County, in effect
7 p.m. Wednesday to 7 p.m. Friday, 2 to 4 inches of rain with isolated amounts up to 8
and a stated potential for life-threatening flooding. The household number sits in the
same document: a home valued at $372,633 owes the county $1,439 this year, about $30
less than last. Who Pays ladder tier 1 (household money, dated and quantified) running
into tier 2 (a safety failure with accountability attached), and the two are genuinely
the same story this week rather than a manufactured link.

**Topic-concentration note.** Yesterday's Briefing ("Zilker is clearing its drains")
was also about this storm, so leading on rain again needed a reason beyond its being
the biggest thing happening. The reason is that the news changed state: yesterday there
was no active alert for Travis County and the story was preparation; this morning the
watch is issued, dated and bounded, and the budget vote gives it a money spine that
yesterday's did not have. Two weather-adjacent leads in a row is the ceiling — if rain
leads Thursday it should be because something flooded, not because rain is forecast.
Week's Briefing subjects so far: voter registration, school cooling, water costs,
housing permits, the ICE-shooting warrant, transit fuel costs, flood prep, this. No
subject has led more than twice.
